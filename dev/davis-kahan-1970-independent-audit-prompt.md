# Independent Davis--Kahan 1970 theorem-statement audit prompt

You are an independent mathematical auditor. Your task is to decide whether this repository justifies the claim of **100% theorem-statement-level formal coverage** of Chandler Davis and W. M. Kahan, *The Rotation of Eigenvectors by a Perturbation. III* (1970).

You do **not** have a Lean compiler. Do not infer compilation from source code or from the census. The user will provide a machine-generated certificate bundle produced by a separate machine with the repository's pinned Lean toolchain. Treat compiler evidence and mathematical-source fidelity as separate questions.

   `python3 scripts/certify_davis_kahan_1970.py --clean`;
3. inside that bundle:
   - `certificate.json`;

The repository uses one distributable mathematical source specification:

- `dev/davis-kahan-1970-statement-map.json` — machine-readable source-specification-passage-to-Lean registration;
- `dev/davis-kahan-1970-full-source-census.json` — the repository's claimed mathematical status;
- `dev/davis-kahan-1970-independent-audit-template.md` — the same comparison format without compiler evidence;
- `formalization.yaml` — project-level bookkeeping and validation policy.

The static formalization audit must be possible from the checked-in `DavisKahan1970_part_III.tex` plus the Lean sources and certificate. A private modernized transcription is not a dependency. If a lawful copy of the original paper or the private transcription is separately supplied, it may be used for a distinct **distillation-fidelity audit**: verify that the checked-in transformative reconstruction itself has not omitted or altered any mathematical source claim. Do not make ordinary source-to-Lean certification depend on that private material.

## Completion rule you must audit

For this project, 100% means:

- every mathematical claim designated as a completion obligation has a Lean statement matching the source claim at the source's actual mathematical scope; or
- if the printed claim is false, there is a compiled formal counterexample/refutation satisfying the printed hypotheses and falsifying the printed conclusion.

Proof strategy need not resemble the paper. Stronger reusable theorems, alternative reductions, and multiple Lean declarations jointly covering one source claim are acceptable.

**Proposition 4.4 is intentionally expected to be a refutation.** Do not demand a proof of the false printed proposition. Instead verify that the formal counterexample really lies inside the printed hypotheses and contradicts the printed conclusion.

The four Section 10 questions are retained for source accounting but are not theorem-completion obligations.

## Second: audit every registered source row independently

Work through all 49 rows in `statement-audit.md` in order. Do not sample.

For each row:

1. Read the registered distributable source-specification passage before reading the census notes.
2. Treat the census status (`compiled_exact`, `refuted_as_transcribed`, etc.) as a proposition to test, not as evidence.
3. Read the compiler-printed types of the listed **primary review declarations**.
4. If those do not clearly cover every clause, inspect the row's full declaration mapping and the corresponding types in `signatures.json`; inspect the Lean source declarations as needed.
5. Decide whether the declarations jointly reproduce the source statement. It is acceptable for one source claim to be factored into several Lean declarations, but their conjunction must actually cover every mathematical clause.

For every mathematical row, explicitly check these axes where applicable:

- all hypotheses and quantifier order;
- the exact conclusion, including existence and uniqueness clauses;
- real versus complex scalar scope;
- finite-dimensional versus arbitrary/separable Hilbert-space scope;
- compactness and finite-rank assumptions;
- bounded versus densely-defined/unbounded operator scope and domain/core assumptions;
- spectral separation hypotheses and constants;
- acute, nonacute, crossed-defect, and direct-rotation existence hypotheses;
- choice dependence versus canonical constructions;
- eigenvalue/singular-value/principal-angle indexing and multiplicities;
- zero-angle and `pi/2` defect branches;
- finite sums versus infinite sums and whether divergent extended-real cases are represented;
- ideal membership versus merely conditional norm inequalities;
- whether an “equivalent formulation” has a compiled dictionary theorem connecting the repository's notation to the paper's notation;
- all symmetry/interchange clauses and textual extensions attached to a numbered theorem.

Use exactly one verdict per row:

- **PASS exact** — the compiled Lean statement(s) jointly match the registered source claim at its actual scope;
- **PASS refuted** — the printed claim is false and the compiled formal refutation is inside the source hypotheses;
- **FAIL scope** — conclusion is related but Lean assumes more or covers less than the source;
- **FAIL conclusion** — the Lean conclusion is not the same mathematical assertion;
- **FAIL missing clause** — part of a multi-clause source statement is absent;
- **FAIL source specification** — the registered source-specification passage is malformed, misleading, or omits context needed to state the claim correctly;
- **UNCERTAIN** — evidence supplied is insufficient to decide.

Do not upgrade an `UNCERTAIN` row merely because nearby infrastructure strongly suggests the intended theorem is true.

## High-risk rows and context traps

Give extra attention to the following known historical failure modes. These are hints about what to inspect, not instructions about what verdict to reach.

### Sections 1 and 2

- Section 1 contains mathematical framework and residual-minimization assertions outside numbered theorem environments. Make sure the register has not reduced these to mere definitions when inequalities/minimality are asserted.
- In Section 2, keep the **directed** angle `Theta_0` / residual `R` conclusions separate from the **ambient** angle `Theta` / perturbation `H` conclusions. The tan, sin(2 theta), and tan(2 theta) theorems each have distinct paired conclusions; changing `R` to `H` without also changing the angle object is not source-faithful.
- The unrestricted `tan 2 Theta` row was a historical overclaim and remains a high-risk regression target. The printed theorem does not assume angle-spectrum pole exclusion or perturbed-Q-block spectral placement. The current audit registers exact directed-residual and ambient wrappers over both complex and real Hilbert spaces that derive pole exclusion internally from only the printed gap/invariance/off-diagonal assumptions. Verify those exact declaration types directly; reject as `FAIL scope` any regression that reintroduces an explicit pole hypothesis, a quarter-angle branch, perturbed-Q-block placement, complex-only scope, or a narrower norm class.
- The paper's ambient convention is real **or** complex separable Hilbert space, with finite dimension only a special case. Do not accept a finite-dimensional or complex-only theorem as exact when the source is unqualified.
- Section 2 includes statements about applicability to unbounded operators. Check whether the source-facing declarations really represent those domain assumptions rather than only the bounded specialization.

### Section 3

- Distinguish the paper's acute condition from **uniform** acuteness. Earlier versions of this formalization conflated them.
- Proposition 3.2 includes the nonacute existence/nonuniqueness issue through crossed defect spaces.
- Corollary 3.2 requires the full nonacute polar resolution and reversal behavior, not only the canonical acute constructor.
- Proposition 3.4 is printed at nonacute direct-rotation scope; an `IsUniformlyAcute` specialization is insufficient.

### Section 4

This was the last major source-fidelity frontier, so inspect it especially carefully.

- Proposition 4.1 has **two printed formulations**. Verify both:
  1. the orthonormal-vector/principal-angle statement; and
  2. the singular-value / approximation-number minimality statement, including the direct-rotation value formula.
- Verify that the repository's `principalAngleSequence` is connected by compiled theorems to the paper's principal-angle/eigenvalue notation, rather than simply reusing the symbol `theta` for a newly defined sequence.
- Check compact matched-crossed-defect **nonacute** scope, including real and complex fields.
- Corollary 4.1 must cover the paper's unitarily invariant norm class at the same source scope, not only Ky Fan norms or an acute specialization.
- Proposition 4.2 is an infinite series statement and explicitly allows an infinite right-hand side. Verify the formal type has the correct infinite/extended-real semantics.
- Proposition 4.3 likewise needs the nonacute source scope and arbitrary represented unitarily invariant norm endpoint.
- Proposition 4.4 should receive **PASS refuted** only if the counterexample satisfies the printed real-space and angle hypotheses and genuinely beats the direct rotation for a norm in the claimed class.

### Section 5

- Theorem 5.1 has an `A/B` interchange clause and a stated densely-defined unbounded-`A` extension in surrounding prose. Make sure those are registered and covered, not lost because they occur outside the theorem environment. For the bounded theorem, prefer the literal source-facing wrapper that carries a two-sided inverse operator and the printed `‖A⁻¹‖` bound; the weaker reusable left-inverse theorem is not itself the best declaration for statement comparison.
- Check both Hilbert--Schmidt and operator-norm Hermitian inequalities in the intervening source prose.

### Section 6 and appendix material

- Do not audit only numbered theorem blocks. The source contains mathematical appendix/proof infrastructure that the census intentionally treats as obligations.
- Theorem 6.3 and Lemma 6.3 have historically been easy to narrow accidentally to finite trial spaces or the wrong scalar field.

### Sections 7--9

- Sections 7's proof identities are tracked because they are mathematical claims, even though they are proof machinery rather than headline theorems.
- Section 8 has multiple branches and real/complex source endpoints; make sure a single branch theorem is not being counted as the full source statement.
- Section 9 contains a numerical/model example with multiple equations and an infinite-dimensional residual example. Verify the registered groups actually cover every equation/claim named in the source passage rather than merely the final bound.

### Section 10

Review the four questions for faithful documentation, but do not count unanswered source questions as formalization failures. Question 10.1 may have a modern partial resolution; distinguish that from claiming the original general question was solved in the 1970 paper.
