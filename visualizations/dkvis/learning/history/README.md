# How eigenvector stability became a subject

A standalone historical overview for viewers who have encountered vectors and
eigenvectors. No other course lecture is required. It introduces the people,
publications, and questions behind principal angles, approximation, operator
perturbation, Davis–Kahan, statistical use, and the transport work here.

The numbered lecture is **000505**, stable ID `history_overview`, deck
**`history-overview`** now has 25 scenes, including an explicit attributed
Sylvester portrait. Its roughly 2,900-word core narration takes about
20 minutes at 150 words/minute, before animation and question pauses.

From `visualizations/`:

```bash
make slides DECK=history-overview
make text DECK=history-overview
make video DECK=history-overview TTS_PROFILE=crall-math-dk-v2-breeze-tts2
```

It is included in `make lectures-video`. Restart an existing library server to
load the new registration. HTML, PDF, and narration exports share its numbered
public name. Generated files remain ignored.

`publications.yaml` supplies compact visible cards; `sources.md` preserves the
full titles and links. Original mathematical authorship and GPT-6.1-Sol's
lecture authorship are separate. The shared cover reads `provenance.yaml`.
Illustrations are schematic and labelled where examples are used. Publication
landmarks are equally spaced for legibility, not drawn to a numerical scale.
Parallel traditions are revisited rather than presented as a single chain of
historical influence.

The history lecture has its own question-setting scene and four study questions.
It has no artificial Lean facts or computation bonus: it teaches historical
relationships, with concept lectures supplying their own formal companions.

The [people-focused companions](../history_people/README.md) develop the
biographies with credited portraits. The overview fetches its Sylvester image
through the same [portable asset store](../../../docs/EXTERNAL_ASSETS.md).
