\begin{abstract}
We formalize the spectral subspace perturbation theory of Davis and Kahan
(1970) in Lean~4. Of 29 results identified in the original paper, 28 are
proved at their reviewed scope and one, Proposition~4.4, is refuted. The
development covers real and complex Hilbert spaces, unbounded self-adjoint
operators, interval/exterior and ordered half-infinite spectral gaps, and
unitarily invariant operator-ideal norms wherever defined. In particular, a
formalization of the sine-theta estimate identifies the source's positive
angle operator with the modulus of the rectangular block in its Sylvester
proof.

Proposition~4.4 claims that the canonical direct rotation minimizes full
displacement for every unitarily invariant norm under a real short-angle
condition. A formalized family in $\mathbb R^4$ gives counterexamples at
arbitrarily small positive angles. We identify the invalid inequality in the
original proof and establish a sharp quantitative replacement: for an acute
pair with maximal principal angle $\alpha$, the canonical rotation $R$ and
any admissible orthogonal or unitary map $W$ satisfy
$N(I-R)\leq\sec(\alpha/2)N(I-W)$, with the factor optimal uniformly over
unitarily invariant norms. We also formalize the exact trace-norm minimum
for the real counterexample family. The development supplies reusable
operator-theoretic foundations and formalizes the principal
Yu--Wang--Samworth perturbation bounds, exposing two errors in their printed
formulas.
\end{abstract}

direct rotations; unitarily invariant norms; formalized mathematics; Lean.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Davis and Kahan's perturbation bounds relate the change in a spectral
subspace of a self-adjoint operator to the size of a residual and the
separation of spectra \citep{davis1970rotation,kato1976perturbation}.
Their 1970 treatment includes direct rotations of subspaces, inequalities
for unitarily invariant norms, and sine and tangent estimates for unbounded
operators. The finite-dimensional theory underlies many spectral algorithms
\citep{stewart1990matrix,wedin1972perturbation}. In statistics, the
population-gap bounds of Yu, Wang, and Samworth \citep{yu2015davis} make
these estimates useful when the empirical spectrum is itself random.

We have formalized the original Davis--Kahan results in Lean~4
\citep{demoura2021lean4}, using Mathlib \citep{mathlib2020}. The central
sine-theta estimate has a scope substantially broader than its familiar
matrix version. Its self-adjoint operators may be unbounded, its spectral
gap may be expressed through ordered half-lines rather than a finite
interval, and the unitarily invariant norm need only be defined on the
operators appearing in the estimate. These hypotheses require a formal
treatment of domains, spectral calculus, and symmetric operator ideals.
They also require care in identifying the positive angle operator in the
source with the modulus of the rectangular operator used in the proof.
We develop this identity in \cref{sec:sine-theta}; the corresponding Lean
signature is displayed beside the mathematical theorem.

A review of the source statements also found an incorrect extremal
claim in Section~4. Proposition~4.4 asserts that, for real subspaces with
largest principal angle at most $\pi/3$, the canonical direct rotation
minimizes $N(I-W)$ over all orthogonal maps carrying one subspace onto
the other, for every unitarily invariant norm $N$. We give a real
four-dimensional counterexample for every positive acute angle. It is the
realification of the phase mechanism already exhibited over $\mathbb C$
by Davis and Kahan themselves \citep[Example~4.2]{davis1970rotation}.
The formalization pinpoints the invalid Ky Fan inequality in their proof,
recovers the valid $Q$-norm assertion, and establishes the sharp factor
$\sec(\alpha/2)$ for arbitrary unitarily invariant norms. The historical
norm-minimization work of \citet{bolotnikov2001minimal} and subsequent
Grassmann-angle results \citep{qiu2006grassmann,zhang2007angular,zhang2010canonicalangles}
provide the appropriate context for these deductions.

We separately formalize the principal Yu--Wang--Samworth (YWS)
perturbation inequalities and correct a missing square and a rank-boundary
convention in their printed statements. Across the Davis--Kahan
source, 28 of 29 reviewed results are proved at their stated scope; the
remaining claim is refuted as printed. The exact result correspondence is
recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:dk-inventory}, with the review method in
Appendix~\ref{app:source-review}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formalize the 1970 Davis--Kahan results, including the full
    real/complex, unbounded, operator-ideal sine-theta theorem and its
    interval/exterior and ordered half-infinite gap alternatives. We
    develop the spectral, geometric, and operator-ideal foundations needed
    for these statements.
  \item We refute Proposition~4.4 within its real short-angle hypotheses,
    prove failure at arbitrarily small angles, and formalize both its
    $Q$-norm consequence and the sharp universal multiplicative bound for
    full displacement. The real counterexample also yields an exact,
    formally verified trace-norm minimum.
  \item We formalize the principal YWS bounds and correct two errors in
    their printed formulations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Source coverage and the sine-theta theorem}
\label{sec:coverage}

The formalization covers \DKResultCount{} result-level statements in Davis
and Kahan's paper: the Section~2 sine and tangent inequalities, the geometry
and extremal properties of direct rotations, the bounded and unbounded
Sylvester estimates, the generalized Section~6 perturbation bounds, and the
spectral branch-selection theorems. \DKProvedCount{} have checked Lean proofs
with their reviewed source hypotheses; Proposition~4.4 is refuted as printed.
Appendix~\ref{app:dk-inventory} specifies the counting rule and gives the
complete result table, and Appendix~\ref{app:source-review} states the
comparison procedure.

These results depend on spectral and operator-ideal constructions that cannot
be reduced to finite matrices without changing the assumptions of the source
statements. The finite-dimensional YWS specialization covers Theorems~1--3 and
their supporting identities and boundary conventions. Its smaller source
account, including the two printed defects, appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:yws-coverage}.

\subsection{The full-scope sine-theta theorem}
\label{sec:sine-theta}

\subsubsection{Trial and exact subspaces}

Davis and Kahan distinguish a reference operator $A$ from a perturbed operator
$A+H$.  For the formal statement we write $A$ for the perturbed full-space
operator, corresponding to the source's $A+H$.

Let $E$ be the ambient Hilbert space.  Let $A_0$ be a self-adjoint operator on a
trial-coordinate space $F$, and let
\[
  E_0:F\to E
\]
be an isometric coordinate map for the trial subspace.  The residual compares
applying the ambient operator after embedding with applying the trial operator
before embedding:
\begin{equation}
  R = AE_0-E_0A_0.
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
When the operators are unbounded, \cref{eq:residual} is asserted on
$\operatorname{dom}(A_0)$ and $R$ is the bounded residual that agrees there.
The Lean predicate \texttt{IsTrialResidual} packages the isometry, the required
domain inclusion, and this residual identity.

Let $F_0:H\to E$ be isometric coordinates for the desired exact spectral
subspace and let $F_1:G\to E$ be isometric coordinates for its orthogonal
complement.  A self-adjoint operator $\Lambda_1$ on $G$ describes the
complementary spectral block through
\[
  AF_1 = F_1\Lambda_1
\]
on its domain.  The predicate \texttt{IsExactSpectralDecomposition} also records
orthogonality and completeness of the two coordinate ranges.

\subsubsection{The sine operator and its rectangular representative}

The source angle operator acts on the trial-coordinate space.  If
$P=F_0F_0^*$ is the orthogonal projection onto the desired exact subspace, then
\[
  \cos\Theta_0 = (E_0^*PE_0)^{1/2},
  \qquad
  \sin\Theta_0 = (I-E_0^*PE_0)^{1/2}.
\]
The analytic proof uses the rectangular complementary block
\begin{equation}
  S=(I-P)E_0.
  \label{eq:rectangular-sine}
\end{equation}
The source-facing Lean theorem is stated on a positive operator on the
trial-coordinate space.  The rectangular block has codomain $E$, while the
positive sine has codomain $F$.  Their relationship is
\[
  S^*S
  = E_0^*(I-P)E_0
  = I-E_0^*PE_0,
\]
so
\begin{equation}
  |S|=(S^*S)^{1/2}=\sin\Theta_0.
  \label{eq:modulus-sine}
\end{equation}
The Lean API constructs the directed angle on trial coordinates by functional
calculus and defines its sine by applying $\sin$ through the same calculus.
Under the isometry hypotheses, Lean proves that this operator is exactly the
modulus $|S|$ in \cref{eq:modulus-sine}.  A polar decomposition $S=U|S|$ and
the ideal laws then imply
\[
  N(S)=N(\sin\Theta_0)
\]
whenever the norm is defined.  The rectangular theorem is the analytic proof
engine; the selected source-facing theorem transports its conclusion to the
positive sine operator.

\subsubsection{Gap geometry and the inequality}

Fix $\delta>0$. The separation hypothesis has the following alternatives.
For some $\beta\leq\alpha$, one of the two spectra
$\sigma(A_0)$ and $\sigma(\Lambda_1)$ lies in $[\beta,\alpha]$ and the other
lies in
\[
 (-\infty,\beta-\delta]\;\cup\;[\alpha+\delta,\infty).
\]
Alternatively, for some real $b$, the operator forms satisfy either
\[
 A_0\geq (b+\delta)I,\quad \Lambda_1\leq bI,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 A_0\leq bI,\quad \Lambda_1\geq (b+\delta)I.
\]
Each form inequality is asserted on the domain of the corresponding operator;
for example, $A_0\geq aI$ means
$\langle A_0x,x\rangle\geq a\|x\|^2$ for every
$x\in\operatorname{dom}(A_0)$. For self-adjoint operators the source's ordered
spectral half-line hypotheses imply these form bounds. The Lean predicate
\texttt{FormBoundedSylvesterGap} records precisely the finite-interval
alternative, in either spectral order, and these two ordered form alternatives.

Both blocks may be unbounded in an ordered configuration. For instance,
on $\ell^2(\mathbb N_{\geq1})$, take diagonal operators
$A_0e_n=(n+\delta)e_n$ and $\Lambda_1e_n=-ne_n$, on the domains where their
images are square summable. They satisfy the first ordered configuration
with $b=0$, while neither spectrum lies in a finite interval. This example
explains why retaining the half-infinite alternatives changes the theorem's
applicability.

The source also writes a common-domain equality for the unbounded trial
configuration.  That equality discharges the forward domain condition consumed
by \texttt{IsTrialResidual}.  Source correspondence is checked from the combined
hypotheses, with this equality supplying the required domain condition.

Appendix~\ref{app:lean-guide} explains the general Lean syntax in the
following theorem signature; the geometric and spectral hypotheses have been
defined above.

\begin{theorem}[Sine-theta estimate]
\label{thm:sine-theta}
Let the scalar field be $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, let $E$ be separable,
and let $A$, $A_0$, and $\Lambda_1$ be self-adjoint operators on the
Hilbert spaces above. Suppose the isometries, domain inclusions, residual
identity, exact complementary decomposition, and one of the three gap
configurations hold. Let $N$ be a supported normalized symmetric
operator-ideal norm. Whenever $\sin\Theta_0$ and $R$ belong to its domain,
\begin{equation}
 \delta\,N(\sin\Theta_0)\leq N(R).
 \label{eq:dk-sine}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Lean signature for the sine-theta estimate. The surrounding
  scalar-field and Hilbert-space context is omitted. The positive
  trial-coordinate sine is \texttt{sourceDirectedSinThetaOperator E0 F0};
  \texttt{FormBoundedSylvesterGap} encodes the interval/exterior and ordered
  half-infinite separation hypotheses described above.},
  label={lst:dk-sintheta-headline}]
theorem sinTheta_unbounded_formGap_whereDefinedUIN_rclike
    [TopologicalSpace.SeparableSpace E]
    (N : NormalizedSymmetricOperatorIdealFamily K)
    (A : LinearPMap K E E)
    (A0 : LinearPMap K F F)
    (Lambda1 : LinearPMap K G G)
    (E0 : ContinuousLinearMap K F E)
    (F0 : ContinuousLinearMap K H E)
    (F1 : ContinuousLinearMap K G E)
    (R : ContinuousLinearMap K F E)
    (hA : IsSelfAdjoint A)
    (hA0 : IsSelfAdjoint A0)
    (hLambda1 : IsSelfAdjoint Lambda1)
    (htrial : IsTrialResidual A A0 E0 R)
    (hexact : IsExactSpectralDecomposition A Lambda1 F0 F1)
    (delta : Real) (hdelta : 0 < delta)
    (hgap : FormBoundedSylvesterGap A0 Lambda1 delta) :
    N.Mem (sourceDirectedSinThetaOperator E0 F0) ->
    N.Mem R ->
      delta * N.gaugeReal (sourceDirectedSinThetaOperator E0 F0) <=
        N.gaugeReal R
\end{lstlisting}

The two ideal-membership hypotheses express the ``where-defined'' restriction.
The proof first estimates the rectangular map $S=(I-F_0F_0^*)E_0$ and then
uses \cref{eq:modulus-sine} and modulus invariance of the ideal gauge. The
ambient Hilbert-space instances are omitted from the displayed Lean signature.
The theorem contains all three separation configurations encoded by its gap
hypothesis.  The ordered half-infinite cases have distinct hypotheses from
the finite interval/exterior specialization, so source correspondence is checked
against the complete family of gap hypotheses.

\subsubsection{Why the Sylvester estimate gives the angle bound}

Set $X=F_1^*E_0$. The exact complementary decomposition gives
$S=F_1X$, and multiplication by the isometry $F_1$ preserves the supported
singular-value gauges. Self-adjointness, the domain hypotheses, and the
residual identity yield the Sylvester relation
\[
 \Lambda_1 X-XA_0=F_1^*R
\]
on $\operatorname{dom}(A_0)$, with the left side represented by the bounded
right side. The separated Sylvester estimate gives
\[
 \delta N(X)\leq N(F_1^*R)\leq N(R).
\]
Combining this with $N(X)=N(S)=N(\sin\Theta_0)$ proves the conclusion.
For unbounded operators, deriving the domain compatibility for $X$ is part of
the argument; the displayed relation is not an everywhere-defined composition
of unbounded maps. The constant-one estimate uses the specified interval or
ordered gap geometry, rather than an arbitrary assertion that the two spectra
are disjoint.

\section{Mathematical architecture of the Davis--Kahan development}
\label{sec:dk-rest}

The proofs draw on three closely related areas: the geometry of two
subspaces, symmetric operator ideals and majorization, and Sylvester
estimates for spectrally separated operators.

\subsection{Direct rotations and the geometry of two subspaces}

Let $P$ and $Q$ be the orthogonal projections onto closed subspaces $U$ and
$V$.  Davis and Kahan organize their geometry through the operators obtained
from $P$, $Q$, and their complements.  In the acute case, the compression
$PQP$ has no kernel on $U$ and determines the cosine of the angle operator;
the complementary compression determines its sine.  Polar decomposition then
supplies the partial isometries that pair the corresponding principal
subspaces.  Combining these pieces gives the direct rotation, an orthogonal or
unitary operator $R$ satisfying $R(U)=V$ and rotating along the principal
planes.

This construction explains several results that look separate when stated only
at theorem level.  Existence and uniqueness in the acute case, the
principal-square-root characterization, commutation with the angle operators,
and reversal symmetry all express compatibility among the same projection and
polar-decomposition data.  The nonacute theory records the additional kernel
conditions needed when principal angles reach $\pi/2$.  For compact angle
operators, the resulting decomposition reduces to the familiar sequence of
principal angles; the general Hilbert-space statements do not require such a
discrete spectrum.

Section~8 returns to this geometry from the spectral side.  Perturbation
smallness and spectral repulsion rule out the wrong branch and place the two
spectral subspaces in the acute regime where the direct rotation is available.
In the formal development this branch-selection argument uses the same
unbounded spectral calculus as the perturbation theorems.  Acuteness therefore
follows from the spectral hypotheses, with no separate matrix-level assumption.

\subsection{Approximation numbers, majorization, and unitarily invariant norms}

Davis and Kahan formulate many extremal statements simultaneously for broad
classes of unitarily invariant norms.  In finite dimensions such a norm can be
described by a symmetric gauge function of the singular values.  The
infinite-dimensional formalization uses approximation numbers as the singular
value sequence of a compact operator and symmetric norming functions to encode
the gauge.  Ky Fan partial sums then provide the bridge from pointwise or
majorization statements to norm inequalities.

This layer is essential in Section~4.  If $R$ is the direct rotation and $W$
is any unitary carrying $U$ onto $V$, the restricted displacement has
singular-value extremality strong enough to imply
unitarily invariant norm minimality.  Davis and Kahan also prove a
majorization theorem for the squared full displacement
$(I-W)^*(I-W)$.  Squaring changes the relevant singular values from
$s_j(I-W)$ to $s_j(I-W)^2$, so a majorization statement at the squared level
does not automatically imply the corresponding statement for the unsquared
trace norm.  Proposition~4.4 attempts precisely that stronger passage. The
counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:prop44} shows where it fails; the
$Q$-norm assertion and quantitative bound in
Section~\ref{sec:sharp-displacement} give two valid conclusions.

The same operator-ideal machinery is used in the perturbation inequalities.
For the sine theorem, the statement $\delta N(S)\leq N(R)$ is meaningful for
any supported symmetric operator ideal once both $S$ and the residual belong to
that ideal.  The formal theorem therefore keeps the source's ``where-defined''
convention and does not build an accidental finite-dimensional compactness
assumption into the statement.

\subsection{Sylvester separation and unbounded operators}

The analytic engine behind the sine and tangent estimates is the Sylvester map
\[
  X \longmapsto AX-XB.
\]
Under the interval/exterior or ordered spectral-gap configurations used above,
Davis and Kahan obtain lower bounds of the form
\[
  \delta\,N(X) \leq N(AX-XB).
\]
The perturbation theorems result by identifying the appropriate off-diagonal or
trial-subspace operator $X$ and rewriting its Sylvester residual in terms of the
original perturbation.

The unbounded case forces domain information into this argument.  Expressions
such as $AX-XB$ are only meaningful on the appropriate domains, and the
spectral separation may be encoded by a finite interval/exterior configuration
or by an ordered pair of half-lines.  The reusable development represents
unbounded operators as domain-carrying linear partial maps, develops the
self-adjoint and semibounded interfaces needed by the proof, and connects
spectral hypotheses to a form-bounded Sylvester gap.  Strong spectral cutoffs
reduce parts of the argument to bounded operators; convergence of the relevant
approximation numbers then permits passage back to the unbounded setting.

Section~6 reuses this engine after weakening the trial-coordinate assumptions.
Reflection pinching and direct-sum norm comparisons control the resulting block
operators, while the Sylvester estimate supplies the spectral-gap factor.  This
produces the generalized sine and tangent theorems and the pairwise-gap
square-norm estimate at the scope stated in the source.

\subsection{Formal foundations and ancestry}

Several of the preceding objects had no sufficiently general interface in the
available Lean libraries when this work began.  The development therefore adds
a focused theory of principal angles and projections, approximation numbers and
symmetric operator ideals, polar decomposition and direct rotations, Borel
spectral calculus, unbounded self-adjoint partial maps, and real/complex
transfer.  Complexification is used to prove analytic results once when the
complex spectral theorem is the natural tool and then descend the conclusions
to real Hilbert spaces where Davis and Kahan state them as well.

Mathlib supplies the general algebraic, topological, Hilbert-space, and
continuous-functional-calculus foundation \citep{mathlib2020,dedecker2025cfc}.
Parts of the projection-valued-measure, Borel-calculus, and self-adjoint
infrastructure were copied, adapted, or generalized from Spectra
\citep{bornemann2026spectra}.  Reusable declarations from this project are
organized under the \texttt{TauCeti.*} namespaces and are being prepared for
integration with Tau Ceti \citep{tauceti2026}.  The paper-specific
Davis--Kahan package contains the source statements, examples, and the
Proposition~4.4 counterexample; Appendix~\ref{app:source-review} records the
statement-level review procedure.

\section{Yu--Wang--Samworth as a statistical specialization}
\label{sec:yws}

YWS \citep{yu2015davis} gives a finite-dimensional population-gap version of
subspace perturbation theory.  Let
$\Sigma,\widehat\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}$ be symmetric, with population
eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_p$.  For
$1\le r\le s\le p$, write $d=s-r+1$ and let $V,\widehat V$ contain orthonormal
eigenvectors at indices $r,\ldots,s$.  With population gap
\[
  \Delta=
  \min\{\lambda_{r-1}-\lambda_r,\lambda_s-\lambda_{s+1}\}>0,
\]
YWS Theorem~2 gives
\begin{equation}
  \|\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\|_{\mathrm F}
  \le
  \frac{2\min\{\sqrt d\,\|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}},
                    \|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm F}\}}
       {\Delta}.
  \label{eq:yws-sintheta}
\end{equation}
An aligned-frame bound follows with an additional factor $\sqrt2$.
Here $\lambda_0=+\infty$ and $\lambda_{p+1}=-\infty$ are the usual
boundary conventions. Theorem~3 applies the same population-gap principle to left and right singular
subspaces through the corresponding Gram matrices.  The formalization checks
Theorems~1--3 and the supporting identities and conventions needed for those
claims; Appendix~\ref{app:yws-coverage} gives the exact scope.

Two printed defects appear in this comparison.  Equation~(4) omits a square.
For real unit vectors $u,v$, the corrected identity is
\begin{equation}
(2\langle v,u\rangle)^2(1-\langle v,u\rangle^2)
=
\frac14\|v-u\|^2(2-\|v-u\|^2)^2(4-\|v-u\|^2).
\label{eq:yws-equation4-corrected}
\end{equation}
Lean verifies both a numerical counterexample to the printed equation and the
corrected identity in general.

The second defect is the boundary convention in Theorem~3.  The assignment
$\sigma^2_{\operatorname{rank}(A)+1}:=-\infty$ can make the denominator
infinite when the selected block reaches the rank boundary, giving a zero upper
bound even when the compared singular subspaces differ.  The Gram-matrix proof
uses the ambient spectrum, where singular values after the rank and before the
ambient dimension are zero.  The corrected theorem extends the singular values
by zero through the ambient dimension and places the terminal convention after
that dimension.  A checked $2\times2$ rank-one example witnesses the failure
of the printed convention.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Classical perturbation theory for invariant and singular subspaces includes the
Davis--Kahan results, Wedin's singular-subspace bounds, and later matrix and
operator treatments
\citep{davis1970rotation,wedin1972perturbation,stewart1990matrix}.
Sylvester-equation methods provide closely related estimates for operator
angles and unbounded self-adjoint operators
\citep{grubisic2005sylvester,grubisic2010tan}.  YWS packages a
finite-dimensional version around a population eigengap for statistical use
\citep{yu2015davis}; related spectral perturbation bounds are now standard in
high-dimensional statistics \citep{cape2019two,chen2021spectral}.

The formal development builds on Lean~4 and Mathlib
\citep{demoura2021lean4,mathlib2020}.  Large source-driven formalizations such
as Flyspeck and the Liquid Tensor Experiment show how verification of a
specific body of mathematics can require substantial library construction
\citep{hales2017kepler,scholze2022liquid}.  Spectra is technically close to the
present work because it develops spectral and operator infrastructure in Lean;
we directly adapted and generalized parts of that development
\citep{bornemann2026spectra}.

The comparison with Davis and Kahan's statements depends on the domains of
unbounded operators, the form of the spectral gap, and the ideal membership
hypotheses. In particular, \cref{eq:modulus-sine} connects the source angle
operator with the rectangular Sylvester block used in the formal proof.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formalized 28 of the 29 identified results of Davis and Kahan (1970)
at their reviewed scope and refuted Proposition~4.4 as printed. The
full-scope sine-theta estimate retains unbounded self-adjoint operators,
ordered half-infinite gaps, and the operator-ideal hypotheses of the
original argument. Its proof uses an explicit modulus identity between
the positive angle operator and a rectangular Sylvester block.

The real four-dimensional counterexamples to Proposition~4.4 exist at
arbitrarily small angles. The valid squared-displacement theorem yields
$Q$-norm minimality, while the general sharp comparison gives a factor
$\sec(\alpha/2)$ for unitarily invariant norms. Both the bound and its
uniform optimality, together with the exact real trace-norm minimum, are
formalized. The accompanying operator-theoretic foundations are reusable,
and the YWS development provides a further instance of source-level
verification and correction.

\section{Reading the Lean signatures}
\label{app:lean-guide}
\label{app:reading-lean}

A Lean theorem declaration has the form
\[
  \texttt{theorem name (parameters) (hypotheses) : conclusion := proof}.
\]
Parameters introduce the mathematical objects under discussion.  A hypothesis
such as \texttt{(hA : IsSelfAdjoint A)} supplies the corresponding fact;
bundled predicates such as \texttt{IsTrialResidual} and
\texttt{IsExactSpectralDecomposition} collect related assumptions.  Braces mark
arguments normally inferred by Lean, while square brackets request typeclass
instances supplying ambient algebraic, analytic, or topological structure.  The
proposition after the final colon is checked by Lean's kernel
\citep{demoura2021lean4}.

In \Cref{lst:dk-sintheta-headline}, \texttt{RCLike} is the Mathlib abstraction
covering $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$.  A type
\texttt{ContinuousLinearMap K F E} is a continuous linear map.  A type
\texttt{LinearPMap K E E} is a linear partial map whose
domain is a submodule; this is the representation used for possibly unbounded
operators.  \texttt{N.Mem T} states that the ideal gauge associated with $N$ is
finite on $T$, and \texttt{N.gaugeReal T} is the corresponding real value.  The
suffix \texttt{Real} refers to the codomain of the gauge and not to the scalar
field.
