\section{Source-correspondence review}
\label{app:source-review}

A result is accepted only after two distinct checks.

\paragraph{Mechanical verification.}
The declaration must resolve in the ordinary project build, and every theorem
on which it depends must be accepted by Lean's kernel.  This establishes the
formal proposition exactly as encoded.

\paragraph{Source comparison.}
The encoded proposition is compared with the cited source location.  The review
checks the mathematical objects, hypotheses, conclusion, scalar field,
dimension, norm class, boundary conventions, and operator-domain assumptions.
For a nonliteral representation, the review also records the theorem or
mathematical argument that justifies the correspondence.  \Cref{eq:modulus-sine} is the main example: the public Lean sine-theta theorem
is stated on the positive sine operator, while its rectangular analytic proof
engine is connected to that operator by an exact modulus identity and
polar-decomposition transport of the symmetric ideal gauge.

This separation prevents theorem names from serving as evidence of fidelity.
For example, the proved declaration
\texttt{sinTheta\_unbounded\_intervalExterior\_characterizedWitness\_rclike}
remains a useful finite-gap theorem, but it is not the selected source endpoint
because it does not include the half-infinite gap cases.  Conversely,
Proposition~4.4 is complete in the inventory even though its printed assertion
has no proof: the completed formal treatment consists of the transcribed claim
as a proposition, a checked counterexample/refutation, a diagnosis of the
source proof, and repaired theorems.

\section{Formalization procedure}
\label{app:formalization-process}

For each source theorem, we identified its standing assumptions, selected a
mathematical representation in Lean, and proved the required intermediate
results against the pinned Mathlib and Tau Ceti dependencies. Proof
development used Lean's elaborator and kernel to resolve domain conditions,
separation hypotheses, and norm-ideal membership. The source-correspondence
review in Appendix~\ref{app:source-review} was performed separately from
compilation; it compared the elaborated statement with the original theorem,
including conventions inherited from surrounding text.

ChatGPT, Claude Code, and Codex were used for literature and library search,
proof design, implementation, debugging, and manuscript preparation. The
human authors selected and reviewed the mathematical statements and are
responsible for their interpretation and the submitted text. The main text
and the source inventory identify the formal results; the project archive
contains the corresponding proofs and pinned dependencies.

\section{Artifact organization}
\label{app:artifact}

The paper-facing Davis--Kahan declarations are under
\path{DavisKahan/Sources/DavisKahan1970}.  Reusable mathematical infrastructure
is primarily under \path{ForTauCeti}; its declarations already use final
\texttt{TauCeti.*} namespaces.  The maintained Davis--Kahan result inventory is
\path{dev/davis-kahan-1970-formalization-result-inventory.json}.  This organization permits
source-audit records and paper-specific counterexamples to remain local to the
paper package while reusable analysis is prepared for upstream integration.


\subsection{Displacement theorems beyond the original inventory}
\label{app:small-angle-artifact}

The 29-target count concerns results stated in the 1970 paper. The fixed
counterexample and failure of the published Ky Fan inequality are in
\path{DavisKahan/FiniteDimensional/DirectRotation/ShortRotationCounterexample.lean}.
The parameterized family is in
\path{DavisKahan/FiniteDimensional/DirectRotation/SmallAngleCounterexample.lean},
with source-facing aliases in \path{DavisKahan/Sources/DavisKahan1970/Section4SmallAngle.lean}.
The Lean theorem
\texttt{arbitrarilySmall\_fullDisplacement\_refuted} proves failure below
every positive angle threshold.

The following additional modules contain the proved quantitative results:
\path{ProjectionDisplacement.lean} proves the projection-distance lower
bound Proposition~\ref{prop:projection-lower}; \path{TraceOptimality.lean} establishes
the global real-family minimum Corollary~\ref{cor:real-trace-optimum}; and
\path{SharpDisplacement.lean} proves the real/complex unitarily invariant
seminorm inequality \cref{thm:sharp-displacement}, its fixed-angle
sharpness, and the optimal common factor $2/\sqrt3$ for the $\pi/3$ range.
These files are in \path{DavisKahan/FiniteDimensional/DirectRotation}.

The same directory contains \path{ComplexTraceOptimality.lean},
\path{ComplexTraceAssembly.lean}, and \path{ComplexTraceFiniteModel.lean},
which establish planar and finite block-model trace optima. The general
finite-dimensional complex theorem, with no supplied coordinate model or
acute-angle assumption, is in \path{ComplexPhaseTrace.lean}
(\texttt{exists\_global\_complex\_trace\_minimizer}). The matched crossed-defect
isometry needed at right angles is in \path{ComplexCrossedDefects.lean}.
\path{ComplexTracePrincipalAngles.lean} proves the explicit
$2\sum_j\sin\theta_j$ formula and the attained all-pairs optimum
\cref{thm:complex-trace-all-pairs}.
The reusable real/complex identity \cref{prop:projection-trace-sine},
including unequal ranks, is in
\path{ForTauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/TraceSine.lean}.
These are additional theorems beyond the fixed 29-result source inventory.
The real line/hyperplane norm formula in the optional working draft remains
a conventional argument without a corresponding general Lean theorem.

The smoothed optimization results in \cref{sec:smoothed-transport} are
also in the direct-rotation directory. \path{SmoothedSpectralCost.lean}
identifies the literal singular-value penalty with its weighted nuclear
norm. \path{SmoothedPrincipalAngles.lean} proves
\path{exists_unitary_minimizing_smoothed_transport_principalAngles},
the attained global principal-angle formula.
\path{SmoothedExplicitOptimizer.lean} defines
\texttt{optimalSmoothedTransport} and proves its admissibility, exact cost,
and global optimality. \path{SmoothedSubspaceStability.lean} proves
\path{smoothed_transport_growth_of_principalAngles_le}, with the
explicit positive coefficient used in \cref{cor:smoothed-stability}.
\path{SmoothedCommutingCorrection.lean} defines the literal operator
singular-value distance and its named nearest commuting correction;
\path{optimalSmoothedCorrection_isMin_of_commute} proves the global
minimality in \cref{cor:smoothed-commuting-correction}.
\path{SmoothedCriticalStability.lean} proves the root growth inequality and
fourth-power error certificate in \cref{prop:smoothed-critical-stability},
with an explicitly constructed critical rotation. Its hypotheses specify
the skew-adjoint unitary and its reversal by the actual source reflection.
\path{SmoothedQuarticStability.lean} proves
\path{smoothed_transport_quartic_growth} and
\path{smoothed_transport_quartic_error_bound} for all angles at or below
the cutoff, and \path{smoothed_canonical_unique_minimizer} proves feasibility,
global minimality, and uniqueness there. These are the endpoints for
\cref{thm:smoothed-quartic-stability}; arbitrary mixtures of critical and
smaller angles are permitted.
\path{SmoothedCanonicalOptimality.lean} proves
\path{smoothed_canonical_isMin_iff} and
\path{optimalSmoothedTransport_lt_canonical_of_supercritical}, giving the
necessary-and-sufficient accepted-principal-angle criterion and the strict
improvement by the explicit optimizer in \cref{cor:smoothed-canonical-cutoff}.
\path{SmoothedCriticalFlatness.lean} proves the quartic scalar upper bound
and failure of every local positive quadratic modulus.
\path{SmoothedCriticalTransportFlatness.lean} realizes that failure for
actual planar unitary transporters at the exact cutoff, with the literal
singular-value cost and Frobenius distance. Its endpoint
\path{smoothed_critical_planar_no_quadratic_growth} certifies the final
assertion of \cref{prop:smoothed-critical-flatness}.
The global lower-bound endpoints quantify over every admissible complex
unitary; they require no supplied principal-plane coordinates or restriction
on mixing. The general angle theorem supplies an optimizer; the stability theorems
supply quadratic growth below the cutoff and quartic growth through equality.
The critical-family theorem gives its sharper root estimate. These results do not classify all supercritical optimizers.

\subsection{Reproduction}

The root \path{lean-toolchain} and \path{lake-manifest.json} pin the
compiler and dependencies. From the repository root, the ordinary build
checks the source-facing development:
\begin{verbatim}
lake exe cache get
lake build
\end{verbatim}

smoothed optimization extensions. They may also be built explicitly:

lake exe cache get
