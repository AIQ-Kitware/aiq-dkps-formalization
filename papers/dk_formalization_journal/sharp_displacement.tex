\section{Sharp bounds for full displacement}
\label{sec:sharp-displacement}

Let $U,V$ be subspaces of the same finite-dimensional real or complex
Hilbert space, with orthogonal projections $P,Q$. An admissible orthogonal
or unitary operator $W$ satisfies $WU=V$. We write $N$ for a unitarily
invariant norm, or more generally a unitarily invariant seminorm. The
principal angles of $U$ and $V$ are $\theta_j\in[0,\pi/2]$; for an acute
pair let $\alpha=\max_j\theta_j<\pi/2$, with $\alpha=0$ when
$U=V=\{0\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Projection-distance lower bound]
\label{prop:projection-lower}
For every admissible $W$ and every unitarily invariant seminorm $N$,
\begin{equation}
  N(P-Q)\leq N(I-W).
  \label{eq:projection-lower}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Relative to $U\oplus U^\perp$, write
$W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$J=2P-I$. The off-diagonal part of $I-W$ is
\[
 \frac{(I-W)-J(I-W)J}{2}
 =-\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\C&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
By the triangle inequality and unitary invariance, its $N$-seminorm is
at most $N(I-W)$. Because $W(U)=V$, the singular values of $A$ are the
principal cosines $\cos\theta_j$. Unitarity gives
$C^*C=I-A^*A$ and $BB^*=I-AA^*$, so the nonzero singular values of both
$B$ and $C$ are the principal sines. The off-diagonal matrix therefore
has singular values $\sin\theta_j$ twice, with zeros for the remaining
dimensions. The difference $P-Q$ has the same singular-value list
\citep{zhang2007angular,bhatia1997matrix}. Unitary invariance proves
\cref{eq:projection-lower}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp full-displacement comparison]
\label{thm:sharp-displacement}
Let $U,V$ be an acute pair with largest principal angle $\alpha$ and
canonical direct rotation $R$. For every unitarily invariant seminorm $N$
and every admissible $W$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad N(I-R)\leq\sec(\alpha/2)\,N(P-Q)
                \leq\sec(\alpha/2)\,N(I-W).\quad}
 \label{eq:sharp-displacement}
\end{equation}
For every fixed $0<\alpha<\pi/2$, the coefficient $\sec(\alpha/2)$
is best possible among coefficients valid for all dimensions, scalar
fields, and unitarily invariant norms. Consequently, the best common
coefficient under the hypothesis $\alpha\leq\pi/3$ is $2/\sqrt3$;
$\sqrt2$ is a uniform bound for all acute pairs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
 caption={Lean signature for the sharp displacement bound, over real and
 complex finite-dimensional spaces for every unitarily invariant seminorm.
 The scalar-field and ambient-space context is omitted, and the linear
 isometry equivalence type is written without its Unicode notation.},
 label={lst:sharp-displacement}]
theorem cos_half_arcsin_projectionGap_mul_uiNorm_directRotation_le_displacement
    (N : UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm K E E)
    (U V : Submodule K E)
    [U.HasOrthogonalProjection] [V.HasOrthogonalProjection]
    (hacute : IsAcute U V)
    (W : LinearIsometryEquiv K E E)
    (hmap : U.map W.toLinearMap = V) :
    Real.cos (Real.arcsin (U.projectionGap V) / 2) *
      N (LinearMap.id - (directRotation U V hacute).toLinearMap) <=
        N (LinearMap.id - W.toLinearMap)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{proof}
On a principal plane with angle $\theta_j$, the singular values of
$I-R$ are $2\sin(\theta_j/2)$, twice, whereas those of $P-Q$ are
$\sin\theta_j$, twice. Both operators vanish on the remaining fixed
summand. As $0\leq\theta_j\leq\alpha<\pi/2$,
\[
 2\sin(\theta_j/2)
 =\frac{\sin\theta_j}{\cos(\theta_j/2)}
 \leq\sec(\alpha/2)\sin\theta_j.
\]
Monotonicity of symmetric gauge functions gives the first inequality in
\cref{eq:sharp-displacement}; the second is
Proposition~\ref{prop:projection-lower}. For the family in
\cref{thm:small-angle}, both principal angles equal $\alpha$ and
\[
 \|P-Q\|_1=4\sin\alpha
 =\|I-W_\alpha\|_1,
 \qquad \|I-R_\alpha\|_1=8\sin(\alpha/2).
\]
Thus the ratio to the best admissible trace-norm displacement is exactly
$\sec(\alpha/2)$. At $\alpha=\pi/3$ it is $2/\sqrt3$, and it tends
to $\sqrt2$ as $\alpha\uparrow\pi/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact trace minimum for the real family]
\label{cor:real-trace-optimum}
For the subspaces $U,V_\theta\subset\mathbb R^4$ of
\cref{thm:small-angle}, $0<\theta<\pi/2$,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{W\in O(4):\,WU=V_\theta}\|I-W\|_1
 =\|P-Q\|_1=4\sin\theta,
 \label{eq:real-trace-minimum}
\end{equation}
with the minimum attained by the explicit $W_\theta$ in
\cref{eq:wtheta}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The singular-value calculation in \cref{thm:small-angle} gives
$\|P-Q\|_1=4\sin\theta=\|I-W_\theta\|_1$.
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:projection-lower} to any other admissible orthogonal
map.
\end{proof}

The factor is optimal uniformly over unitarily invariant norms, not for
an individual norm: $Q$-norms, for example, admit the stronger constant one.
The projection bound, comparison theorem, fixed-angle sharpness, and real
minimum are formalized in Lean; see Appendix~\ref{app:small-angle-artifact}.

\subsection{Complex principal-plane minimizers}
\label{sec:complex-trace-model}

Davis and Kahan's complex phase construction
\citep[Example~4.2]{davis1970rotation} gives an exact trace minimum on
a principal plane. In suitable orthonormal coordinates, put
\begin{equation}
 R_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&i\sin\theta\\
                    i\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad W_\theta=e^{i\theta}R_\theta.
 \label{eq:complex-phase}
\end{equation}
Both maps carry the first coordinate line to the same target line, while
$W_\theta$ has eigenvalues $1,e^{2i\theta}$ and hence
$\|I-W_\theta\|_1=2\sin\theta$ for $0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2$.
The projector difference on that plane has the same trace norm.

The formal development proves global trace-norm optimality for finite
orthogonal sums of these complex principal-plane models, including zero
and right angles, and with an unchanged orthogonal summand. The result is
preserved under an explicitly supplied ambient unitary equivalence.
For these models the minimum is $2\sum_j\sin\theta_j$. No acute-angle
assumption is needed. The distinction between this model theorem and an
all-pairs theorem is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:small-angle-artifact}.

\begin{proposition}[Complex trace minimum: proposed all-pairs formulation]
\label{prop:complex-trace-all-pairs}
For any equal-dimensional subspaces $U,V$ of a finite-dimensional complex
Hilbert space, with principal angles $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_k$,
\[
 \min_{W\text{ unitary}:\,WU=V}\|I-W\|_1
 =\|P-Q\|_1=2\sum_{j=1}^k\sin\theta_j.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:projection-lower}. The cosine--sine
decomposition of $U,V$ splits the nonzero principal angles into mutually
orthogonal complex two-planes, with a common fixed summand. On each plane
use the phase-adjusted map $W_{\theta_j}$ from
\cref{eq:complex-phase}; it transports the corresponding source line
to its target line with trace displacement $2\sin\theta_j$. The identity
on the fixed summand contributes zero. The orthogonal sum is an
admissible unitary attaining $2\sum_j\sin\theta_j=\|P-Q\|_1$.
\end{proof}
