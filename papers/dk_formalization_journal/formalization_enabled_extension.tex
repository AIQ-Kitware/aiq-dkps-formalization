\section{A follow-on theorem enabled by the formal development}
\label{sec:example-transport}

We prove an exact formula for the smallest trace-norm displacement of a real
orthogonal map carrying one given subspace onto another. The formula is a
weighted-matching problem on their principal angles. Its proof uses the
principal-angle geometry, real and complex operator constructions, and
singular-value norms developed for the formalization, together with
additional optimization arguments.

Let $\like{Usource}{K},\like{Vtarget}{L}\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ have the same dimension and let
$\like{theta}{\theta_1},\ldots,\like{theta}{\theta_m}\in(0,\pi/2]$ be their positive principal angles,
counted with multiplicity.  A matching $\sym{Matching}$ on the angle labels consists
of disjoint unordered pairs $\{i,j\}$ from $\{1,\ldots,m\}$;
labels outside the pairs are unmatched.
\jonsays{This could be made more clear, instead of saying "disjoint" we say we are picking pairs without replacement, or something more intuitive...}

Each positive principal angle describes a source line and a target line
in a two-dimensional principal plane. Transporting that plane independently
allows a rotation through $\like{theta}{\theta_i}$, with trace displacement
$4\sin(\like{theta}{\theta_i}/2)$, or a reflection carrying the source line
to the target, with trace displacement $2$. A pair of labels $\{i,j\}$
allows the two principal planes to interact on their four-dimensional sum.
For each possible pair of distinct labels $i,j$, define its paired cost
$\like{PairCost}{e_{ij}}$; for each unmatched label $i$, define its singleton
cost $\like{SingletonCost}{s_i}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nuclear-matching-costs}
 \like{PairCost}{e_{ij}}=4\sin\bigl((\like{theta}{\theta_i}+\like{theta}{\theta_j})/2\bigr),\qquad
 \like{SingletonCost}{s_i}=\min\{4\sin(\like{theta}{\theta_i}/2),2\}.
\end{equation}
The paired cost is attained by an orthogonal transporter on the two-plane
sum. Common zero-angle directions can remain fixed.

\begin{theorem}[Real trace-norm matching reduction]
\label{thm:real-matching-example}
For equal-dimensional real subspaces $\like{Usource}{K},\like{Vtarget}{L}$ as above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-nuclear-match}
\min_{\substack{\sym{Wcompetitor}\in O(n)\\\sym{Wcompetitor}(\like{Usource}{K})=\like{Vtarget}{L}}}\|I-\sym{Wcompetitor}\|_1
=
\min_{\sym{Matching}\text{ a matching}}
\left(
\sum_{\{i,j\}\in \sym{Matching}}\like{PairCost}{e_{ij}}
+
\sum_{i\text{ unmatched}}\like{SingletonCost}{s_i}
\right).
\end{equation}
Both minima are attained.  The equality includes repeated angles,
right angles, and common ambient directions; the left side has no
principal-plane or determinant constraint.
\end{theorem}

We can solve the matching problem by maximizing the savings from pairing angles.
Starting from the all-singleton cost $C_0=\sum_i s_i$, pairing $i,j$ saves
$\like{MatchingSaving}{g_{ij}}=s_i+s_j-e_{ij}$. Thus a matching $\sym{Matching}$ costs
$C_0-\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\sym{Matching}}g_{ij}$, and its edges must have disjoint
endpoints. Figure~\ref{fig:real-nuclear-matching} represents these pairing
choices as a weighted graph for a three-angle example.

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
We first work with $1<p<2$, where the displacement power sum is
differentiable, and recover the trace norm by a limit. Minimize
$\like{PowerCost}{\Phi_p}(\sym{Wcompetitor})=\sum_j s_j(I-\sym{Wcompetitor})^p$.
Write $\like{Psource}{P_K}$ and $\like{Qtarget}{P_L}$ for the orthogonal
projections onto the two subspaces. An orthogonal map carrying an
orthonormal basis of $\like{Usource}{K}$ to one of $\like{Vtarget}{L}$,
and extending to their orthogonal complements, gives a feasible transporter.
The orthogonal group $O(n)$ is closed and bounded in
$\mathbb R^{n\times n}$, hence compact by the Heine--Borel theorem.
The transport constraint
$\sym{Wcompetitor}\like{Psource}{P_K}\sym{Wcompetitor}^T=\like{Qtarget}{P_L}$
is closed, and $\like{PowerCost}{\Phi_p}$ is continuous. The extreme value
theorem therefore gives a minimizer on the nonempty compact feasible set.

We next decompose the ambient space into mutually orthogonal subspaces
$\mathbb R^n=\bigoplus_a V_a$ preserved by
$\like{Psource}{P_K}$, $\like{Qtarget}{P_L}$, and the minimizing
$\sym{Wcompetitor}$. Each block $V_a$ will have dimension at most four.
Preservation by the projections splits the source and target across these
blocks; preservation by the transporter makes the displacement cost add
over them. A block containing one positive principal angle gives a
singleton choice, while a block containing two can give a paired choice.
We construct this decomposition by considering smooth curves
$\sym{Wcompetitor}(t)$ of orthogonal maps satisfying
$\sym{Wcompetitor}(0)=\sym{Wcompetitor}$ and
$\sym{Wcompetitor}(t)\like{Usource}{K}=\like{Vtarget}{L}$.
Since $\sym{Wcompetitor}$ minimizes the cost, the derivative of
$\like{PowerCost}{\Phi_p}(\sym{Wcompetitor}(t))$ at $t=0$ vanishes along
every such curve. This condition is called stationarity.

Let $\sym{SkewDirection}$ be a real skew-symmetric matrix. Then
$\sym{Wcompetitor}\exp(t\sym{SkewDirection})$ is orthogonal for every real $t$.
If $\sym{SkewDirection}$ commutes with $\like{Psource}{P_K}$, its exponential
preserves the source subspace, so this curve consists of feasible
transporters. The derivative of the cost is represented by a
skew-symmetric spectral gradient $\sym{SpectralGradient}$:
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\like{PowerCost}{\Phi_p}(\sym{Wcompetitor}\exp(t\sym{SkewDirection}))\right|_{t=0}
 =-\operatorname{tr}(\sym{SpectralGradient}\sym{SkewDirection}).
\]
It commutes with $\sym{Wcompetitor}$. At a minimum the derivative vanishes
for every skew-symmetric matrix preserving the source and its orthogonal
complement. Taking variations within each of these two subspaces forces
the two diagonal blocks of $\sym{SpectralGradient}$ to vanish. Equivalently,
\[
 \sym{Jreflect}\sym{SpectralGradient}\sym{Jreflect}=-\sym{SpectralGradient},
 \qquad \sym{Jreflect}=2\like{Psource}{P_K}-I.
\]

Let $V_g$ be an eigenspace of the symmetric matrix
$-\like{SpectralGradient}{G^2}$ with positive eigenvalue
$\like{GradientLevel}{g^2}$. On this space,
$\sym{SpectralGradient}/\sym{GradientLevel}$ squares to $-I$, so it can
serve as multiplication by $i$. The reflection $\sym{Jreflect}$
anticommutes with this complex structure and acts as complex conjugation.
The transporter is complex-linear and unitary in these coordinates.
For $f_p(t)=(2\sin(t/2))^p$ on $0<t<\pi$, the equation
$f_p'(t)=\sym{GradientLevel}$ has at most two solutions, say $a,b$
(allowing $a=b$).
Thus the transporter has at most two phases on this gradient level and
can be written
\[
 \sym{Wcompetitor}|_{V_g}=e^{ia}E+e^{ib}(I-E),
\]
where $E$ is the orthogonal projection onto the first phase space.

The projection $E$ and conjugation $\sym{Jreflect}$ admit common invariant
complex lines and planes. To see the dimension bound, choose coordinates
in which $\sym{Jreflect}$ is coordinate conjugation and write $E=X+iY$.
Since $E$ is an orthogonal projection, $X$ is real symmetric, $Y$ is real
skew-symmetric, and
\[
 X^2-Y^2=X,\qquad XY+YX=Y.
\]
The second identity pairs the $\lambda$ and $1-\lambda$ eigenspaces of
$X$ through $Y$; the first gives
$Y^2=-\lambda(1-\lambda)I$ on each such eigenspace.
Choosing orthonormal pairs in these spaces gives real lines and planes
preserved by $X,Y$, whose complex spans are preserved by $E$ and
$\sym{Jreflect}$. Viewed in the original real space, these spans have
dimension two or four. They are preserved by $\sym{Wcompetitor}$ and
$\like{Psource}{P_K}=(\sym{Jreflect}+I)/2$, hence also by
$\like{Qtarget}{P_L}=\sym{Wcompetitor}\like{Psource}{P_K}\sym{Wcompetitor}^T$.
On the zero-gradient space, the transporter has eigenvalues $+1$ and
$-1$ and is a reflection. The two-projection decomposition for its
$-1$ eigenspace projection and $\like{Psource}{P_K}$ gives real blocks
of dimension at most two. A one-dimensional $-1$ block can be replaced
by $+1$ without changing the transported subspace, so it cannot occur at
a minimum.

Replacing the transporter on one common invariant block preserves feasibility,
so a global minimizer restricts to a minimizer on each block.
Solving the one- and two-angle block problems gives singleton and paired
costs depending on $p$. A block with one positive angle contributes its
one-angle minimum; a block with two contributes either two one-angle
minima or their paired minimum. Blocks with only zero angles can remain fixed.
Principal-angle multisets and displacement costs add
over these common blocks, so their choices define a matching. Conversely,
any matching can be realized by taking the orthogonal sum of the
corresponding one- and two-plane transporters. This proves the matching
minimum for $1<p<2$.

Finally let $p$ decrease to $1$. In fixed finite dimension, $\like{PowerCost}{\Phi_p}(\sym{Wcompetitor})$
converges uniformly to $\|I-\sym{Wcompetitor}\|_1$ on the compact feasible set. The scalar
block minima converge to $\like{SingletonCost}{s_i}$ and
$\like{PairCost}{e_{ij}}$ in \cref{eq:nuclear-matching-costs}, and there
are only finitely many matchings. Passing to the limit therefore proves
\cref{eq:real-nuclear-match}. Compactness gives an
attaining transporter at the endpoint.
\end{proof}

For $\like{theta}{\theta_1}=\like{theta}{\theta_2}=\pi/6$, the direct rotation costs
$8\sin(\pi/12)>2$, whereas the paired competitor costs
$4\sin(\pi/6)=2$. The theorem includes comparison with every ambient
orthogonal transporter, so this paired cost is globally minimal in the
example.

Davis and Kahan's complex-phase example already shows how a phase change
can decrease full trace displacement \citep[Example~4.2]{davis1970rotation}.
Bolotnikov, Li, and Rodman formulate the ambient unitary minimal-distortion
problem and prove Q-norm and Frobenius optimality results
\citep[Problem~1.1 and Theorems~3.1, 4.2]{bolotnikov2001minimal}. The formula
above specifies the unrestricted real trace-norm minimum through singleton
and paired angle costs. The real field permits pairing between distinct
principal planes, which is why the answer is an optimization over matchings.
