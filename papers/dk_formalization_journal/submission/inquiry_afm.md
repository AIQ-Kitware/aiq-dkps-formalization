Dear Editors,

My colleagues and I are preparing *Davis--Kahan (1970) in Lean: Formalization,
Counterexample, and Repair*. Before submitting, we would appreciate your
assessment of its fit with AFM's mathematical-insight criterion.

The development treats the 29 result-level targets of Davis and Kahan's
1970 paper: 28 have Lean proofs at their reviewed source scope, while
Proposition 4.4 is refuted by a Lean-checked real four-dimensional example.
The formalization includes unbounded self-adjoint operators, real and
complex Hilbert spaces, finite and half-infinite gap configurations, and
unitarily invariant norms. The paper explains the domain hypotheses and
the modulus identity linking the source's positive sine operator to the
rectangular operator used in the proof.

The manuscript also develops an explicit family showing that the
Proposition 4.4 failure persists at arbitrarily small positive principal
angles, identifies the invalid norm inequality in the printed proof, and
formalizes a Q-norm correction derived from Proposition 4.3. The parameterized family is presented with a conventional proof,
separately from the existing machine-checked fixed-angle refutation.

We distinguish these findings from earlier trace-norm counterexamples and
Q-norm minimization theory. The paper's subject is the mathematics and its
formal representation, rather than an evaluation of an AI workflow.
The public development is at
https://github.com/AIQ-Kitware/aiq-dkps-formalization.

Both the formal proofs and the manuscript have been developed with
extensive AI assistance. Would this mathematical contribution be within
AFM's scope? We would also appreciate clarification of how AFM's
human-authorship requirement applies to extensive AI-assisted drafting
followed by substantive revision and approval by the human authors.

We would be happy to provide the current manuscript.
