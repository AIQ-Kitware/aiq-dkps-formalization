\begin{proposition}[All minimizers for an acute pair]
\label{prop:schatten-all-acute-minimizers}
Suppose that every principal angle of $P,Q$ is less than $\pi/2$, and let
$R$ be their canonical direct rotation. In a cosine--sine decomposition,
group equal positive principal angles into summands
$M_\theta\otimes\mathbb C^2$, with
$R=I_{M_\theta}\otimes R_\theta$ on each such summand. Set
$\delta_p(\theta)=0$ for $\theta\leq\vartheta_p$.
Then all minimizing transporters are the identity on the fixed summands and
have the form
\begin{equation}
 W\big|_{M_\theta\otimes\mathbb C^2}
       =\exp\bigl(i\delta_p(\theta)S_\theta\bigr)\otimes R_\theta,
 \qquad S_\theta=S_\theta^*=S_\theta^{-1},
 \label{eq:schatten-all-acute-minimizers}
\end{equation}
where the self-adjoint unitaries $S_\theta$ may be chosen independently.
In particular, the minimizing transporter is unique if and only if all
principal angles are at most $\vartheta_p$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A global minimum is a local minimum. The nonzero-gradient blocks in the
proof of \cref{thm:schatten-independent-planes} split into canonical
planes of angle $a<\vartheta_p$ and balanced planes of angle
$\sigma>\vartheta_p$. On a balanced plane the eigenphases are $(b,-a)$
or $(a,-b)$, so the transporter is $e^{i\delta}R_\sigma$ or
$e^{-i\delta}R_\sigma$, with $\delta=(b-a)/2$. The scalar uniqueness
in \cref{prop:schatten-scalar-diagram} fixes
$\delta=\delta_p(\sigma)$ as a function of the geometric angle.
The coincident-root blocks give $R_{\vartheta_p}$.

These decompositions reduce $P,Q$, and their planes are therefore
subordinate to the equal-angle summands of the cosine--sine decomposition.
The zero-gradient analysis in \cref{prop:schatten-local-is-global} leaves
only the identity on fixed directions and right-angle blocks. Acuteness
excludes the latter. Thus a minimizer has only the two stated scalar
phases on each supercritical equal-angle summand. Their orthogonal
multiplicity subspaces are the $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces of a
self-adjoint unitary $S_\theta$, giving
\cref{eq:schatten-all-acute-minimizers}.

Conversely, diagonalize each $S_\theta$. The displayed operator becomes
an orthogonal sum of scalar-phase minimizers, so it attains
\cref{eq:schatten-unrestricted-minimum}. Below and at the transition
the phase is zero. At any supercritical angle, the choices
$S_\theta=I$ and $S_\theta=-I$ give distinct minimizers, proving the
uniqueness criterion.
\end{proof}

If a supercritical angle has multiplicity $m$, its minimizing choices are
parametrized by the orthogonal projections $E$ on $\mathbb C^m$, through
$S=2E-I$. The parameter space is consequently the disjoint union
$\coprod_{k=0}^{m}\operatorname{Gr}(k,m)$ of complex Grassmannians.
For distinct positive principal angles, exactly $k$ of which are
supercritical, there are exactly $2^k$ minimizing transporters.

\begin{corollary}[When real transport attains the complex minimum]
\label{cor:schatten-real-parity}
Let $P,Q$ be equal-rank real orthogonal projections whose principal
angles are all less than $\pi/2$. The minimum of $\|I-W\|_p$ over real
orthogonal transporters equals the minimum over complex unitary
transporters if and only if every principal angle greater than
$\vartheta_p$ has even multiplicity. If one such multiplicity is odd,
the real minimum is strictly larger.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose a real cosine--sine decomposition. Complexification preserves the
principal angles and the displacement singular values. By
\cref{prop:schatten-all-acute-minimizers}, a complex minimizer on a
supercritical angle space of multiplicity $m$ has multiplicity factor
\[
 \exp(i\delta S)=\cos\delta\,I+i\sin\delta\,S,
 \qquad S=S^*=S^{-1},\qquad 0<\delta<\pi/2.
\]
This factor is real precisely when $S=iJ$ for a real matrix $J$.
The conditions on $S$ then give $J^T=-J$ and $J^2=-I$.
Such an orthogonal complex structure exists on $\mathbb R^m$ precisely
when $m$ is even: necessity follows by taking determinants of $J^2=-I$,
and sufficiency follows from a direct sum of quarter-turn matrices.
Below and at the cutoff, the multiplicity factor is the identity.
Thus a real complex-minimizing transporter exists exactly under the
stated parity condition. Both feasible sets are compact and nonempty,
so failure to attain the complex minimum forces a strict gap between
the two minimum values.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The sharp exponent-dependent angle cutoff]
\label{cor:schatten-canonical-cutoff}
For an acute complex pair with largest principal angle $\alpha$ (taken
to be zero when the principal-angle list is empty), the
canonical direct rotation minimizes the Schatten displacement if and
only if $\alpha\leq\vartheta_p$. The same inequality is sufficient over
the real field. It is the largest cutoff that works uniformly over
real dimensions: whenever $\vartheta_p<\theta<\pi/2$, a real pair with
two principal angles equal to $\theta$ has a strictly cheaper
orientation-preserving transport.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The complex assertion follows by summing
\cref{prop:schatten-scalar-diagram}. A real transporter is a complex
transporter after complexification, with unchanged singular values, so
the sufficient condition also holds over the real field. For sharpness,
realify a complex planar scalar-phase improvement. Every displacement
singular value is then repeated twice, both real principal angles are
$\theta$, and the real determinant is the squared modulus of the complex
determinant, hence one.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A sharp Schatten comparison constant]
\label{cor:schatten-sharp-ratio}
For a principal-plane direct rotation $R$ of a finite-dimensional real
or complex pair, and every admissible transporter $W$,
\begin{equation}
 \|I-R\|_p\leq 2^{1/p-1/2}\|I-W\|_p.
 \label{eq:schatten-sharp-ratio}
\end{equation}
The constant is attained by a right-angle plane in either field and is
the optimal uniform constant for acute pairs as well.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a scalar-phase plane, put
$x=2\sin((\theta+\delta)/2)$ and
$y=2\sin((\theta-\delta)/2)$. Then
\[
 x^p+y^p\geq(x^2+y^2)^{p/2}
 \geq\bigl(2(2\sin(\theta/2))^2\bigr)^{p/2},
\]
because $x^2+y^2=4-4\cos\theta\cos\delta
\geq4-4\cos\theta$. Consequently
$2f_p(\theta)\leq2^{1-p/2}m_p(\theta)$.
Sum this inequality and apply \cref{thm:schatten-independent-planes}.
Complexification gives the real inequality. At a right angle the direct
rotation has displacement singular values $(\sqrt2,\sqrt2)$, while a
reflection has $(2,0)$. Their norm ratio is $2^{1/p-1/2}$. Real
reflections on planes with angles tending to $\pi/2$ show that the same
constant is necessary for acute pairs.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The nearest subspace-preserving unitary}

For a specified orthogonal projection $P$, let
$\mathcal C(P)=\{V\in U(n):VP=PV\}$. Given a unitary $U$, finding its
nearest element of $\mathcal C(P)$ enforces exact subspace preservation
with the smallest possible change to the full unitary.
This is the block-diagonal approximation formulation of the unitary
distortion problem in \citet[Section~3]{bolotnikov2001minimal}.

\begin{corollary}[Exact unitary rounding]
\label{cor:schatten-unitary-rounding}
Let $\theta_j$ be the principal angles between $\operatorname{ran}P$
and $U\operatorname{ran}P$, and let $\alpha$ be their largest value,
taken to be zero for an empty list. Then
\begin{equation}
 \min_{V\in\mathcal C(P)}\|U-V\|_p^p=\sum_j m_p(\theta_j),\qquad
 \min_{V\in\mathcal C(P)}\|U-V\|_p
          \leq2^{1-1/p}\|[P,U]\|_p.
 \label{eq:schatten-unitary-rounding}
\end{equation}
The constant in the second inequality is optimal. If the pair is acute,
the blockwise unitary polar factor of
$PUP+(I-P)U(I-P)$ is nearest precisely when
\begin{equation}
 \|[P,U]\|_\infty\leq\frac{2\sqrt{p-1}}{p}.
 \label{eq:schatten-polar-rounding-cutoff}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Set $Q=UPU^*$. The correspondence $W=UV^*$, with inverse $V=W^*U$,
identifies $\mathcal C(P)$ with $\mathcal U(P,Q)$ and satisfies
$\|U-V\|_p=\|I-W\|_p$. This proves the exact formula.
The singular values of $P-Q$ are $\sin\theta_j$, each twice, and
$(P-Q)U=[P,U]$. The endpoint phase gives
$m_p(\theta)\leq(2\sin\theta)^p$. Summing yields
\[
 \sum_j m_p(\theta_j)\leq2^{p-1}\|[P,U]\|_p^p.
\]
At a right angle in dimension two, the minimum distance is $2$ and
the commutator norm is $2^{1/p}$, so the constant is necessary.

In an acute cosine--sine decomposition, write $U=L R_\Theta M$ with
$L,M$ block diagonal relative to $P$. The diagonal cosine blocks are
positive definite, so their blockwise unitary polar factor is $LM$.
The direct rotation from $P$ to $Q$ is $L R_\Theta L^*$, and hence its
associated rounded unitary is $LM$. The optimality criterion follows
from \cref{cor:schatten-canonical-cutoff}, because
$\|[P,U]\|_\infty=\sin\alpha$ and
$\sin\vartheta_p=2\sqrt{p-1}/p$.
\end{proof}

This is also a rounding problem for a two-level Hamiltonian. If
$H=E_0I+\Delta(I-P)$ with $\Delta>0$, the sharp estimate becomes
\[
 \min_{\substack{V\in U(n)\\VH=HV}}\|U-V\|_p
       \leq\frac{2^{1-1/p}}{\Delta}\|[H,U]\|_p.
\]
Rounding approximate symmetries to ground-space-preserving operators is
studied by \citet{chubb2017approximate}. For the two-level Hamiltonian
above, the principal-angle formula determines the smallest possible
Schatten displacement to a unitary commuting with $H$ and gives its sharp
dimension-independent bound.

\subsection{Computing the optimizer and resolving the transition}

In the supercritical regime define
\[
 \eta_p(\theta)=\arccos\frac{p\cos\theta}{2-p}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:schatten-log-ratio-derivative} shows that
$D_{p,\theta}$ increases strictly on
$(\eta_p(\theta),\theta)$ and that its unique positive zero lies in
this interval. Bisection of this scalar equation therefore computes the
global plane optimizer with a certified choice of branch. Together with
\cref{thm:schatten-independent-planes}, this reduces the matrix problem
to a cosine--sine decomposition and one scalar root for each supercritical
principal angle.

At $p=3/2$ that root is explicit. Writing $c=\cos\theta$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{3/2}(\theta)=\arccos\frac{c}{1-2c},\qquad
       \arccos(1/3)<\theta\leq\pi/2,
 \label{eq:schatten-three-halves-phase}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 m_{3/2}(\theta)^{2/3}=
 \begin{cases}
  2^{2/3}\,2\sin(\theta/2),&0\leq\theta\leq\arccos(1/3),\\
  2(1+c)^{1/3}(1-2c)^{1/6},&\arccos(1/3)\leq\theta\leq\pi/2.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:schatten-three-halves-value}
\end{equation}
To verify these formulas above the transition, put
$x=2\sin((\theta+\delta)/2)$ and
$y=2\sin((\theta-\delta)/2)$. Squaring the positive stationary
amplitudes gives $x-x^3/4=y-y^3/4$. Since $x>y$, this is equivalent to
$x^2+xy+y^2=4$. The identities
\[
 x^2+y^2=4-4c\cos\delta,\qquad
 xy=2(\cos\delta-c)
\]
give $(1-2c)\cos\delta=c$ and hence
\cref{eq:schatten-three-halves-phase}. Moreover,
\[
 x+y=\frac{2(1-c)}{\sqrt{1-2c}},\qquad
 \sqrt{xy}=\frac{2c}{\sqrt{1-2c}}.
\]
Using $x^{3/2}+y^{3/2}
=\sqrt{x+y+2\sqrt{xy}}\,(x+y-\sqrt{xy})$ proves
\cref{eq:schatten-three-halves-value}. Continuity includes the two
displayed boundary angles.

\begin{proposition}[A hyperbolic parametrization of the optimal phase]
\label{prop:schatten-hyperbolic-phase}
Let $1<p<2$, put $\beta=(2-p)/p$, and suppose
$\vartheta_p<\theta<\pi/2$. There is a unique $u>0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \cos\theta=\frac{\sinh(\beta u)}{\sinh u}.
 \label{eq:schatten-hyperbolic-angle}
\end{equation}
The optimal phase and minimum power sum are
\begin{equation}
 \cos\delta_p(\theta)=\frac{\cosh(\beta u)}{\cosh u},\qquad
 m_p(\theta)=2^{p+1}\cosh u
     \left(\frac{\sinh(2(p-1)u/p)}{\sinh(2u)}\right)^{p/2}.
 \label{eq:schatten-hyperbolic-value}
\end{equation}
The ratio in \cref{eq:schatten-hyperbolic-angle} strictly decreases
from $\beta$ to zero, giving a monotone equation for every supercritical
acute angle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=\theta-\delta_p(\theta)$, $b=\theta+\delta_p(\theta)$,
$x=\sin(a/2)$, $y=\sin(b/2)$, and $q=p-1$.
Then $0<x<y<1$ and the stationary balance reads
$x^q\sqrt{1-x^2}=y^q\sqrt{1-y^2}$.
With $r=x/y=e^{-t}$, $t>0$, it gives
\[
 y^2=\frac{1-r^{2q}}{1-r^{2p}}
      =e^t\frac{\sinh(qt)}{\sinh(pt)},\qquad
 x^2=e^{-t}\frac{\sinh(qt)}{\sinh(pt)}.
\]
The cosine addition identities yield
\[
 \cos\theta=\frac{\sinh t-\sinh(qt)}{\sinh(pt)}
           =\frac{\sinh((2-p)t/2)}{\sinh(pt/2)},
 \qquad
 \cos\delta_p(\theta)=\frac{\sinh t+\sinh(qt)}{\sinh(pt)}
           =\frac{\cosh((2-p)t/2)}{\cosh(pt/2)}.
\]
Set $u=pt/2$ and sum $2^p(x^p+y^p)$ to obtain the formulas.
For uniqueness, $v\coth v$ strictly increases on $(0,\infty)$:
its derivative has numerator $\sinh v\cosh v-v>0$, since that
numerator vanishes at zero and has derivative $2\sinh^2v>0$.
Consequently
\[
 \frac{d}{du}\log\frac{\sinh(\beta u)}{\sinh u}
      =\beta\coth(\beta u)-\coth u<0.
\]
The endpoint limits are $\beta$ and zero.
\end{proof}

For $p=2n/(n+1)$, $n\geq2$, this scalar equation is polynomial.
Let $T_n,U_{n-1}$ be the Chebyshev polynomials characterized by
$T_n(\cosh v)=\cosh(nv)$ and
$U_{n-1}(\cosh v)=\sinh(nv)/\sinh v$ for $v>0$.
Putting $z=\cosh(u/n)>1$ gives
\[
 U_{n-1}(z)=\frac1{\cos\theta},\qquad
 \cos\delta_p(\theta)=\frac{z}{T_n(z)}.
\]
There is exactly one root $z>1$ when $\cos\theta<1/n$.
In particular, $n=2$ gives another elementary closed form.

\begin{corollary}[The explicit four-thirds minimum]
\label{cor:schatten-four-thirds}
For $p=4/3$ the scalar critical angle is $\pi/3$. Above it,
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{4/3}(\theta)=\arccos\frac{\cos\theta}{1-2\cos^2\theta},
 \qquad
 m_{4/3}(\theta)=2^{4/3}(1-2\cos^2\theta)^{1/3}.
 \label{eq:schatten-four-thirds-value}
\end{equation}
Below it, $\delta_{4/3}=0$ and
$m_{4/3}(\theta)=2(2\sin(\theta/2))^{4/3}$.
The formulas agree at $\pi/3$ and extend continuously to $\pi/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here $\beta=1/2$. Write $c=\cos\theta$ and $z=\cosh(u/2)$.
Then $c=1/(2z)$ and
$\cos\delta=z/(2z^2-1)=c/(1-2c^2)$.
Since $\sinh(2u)=4\sinh(u/2)z\cosh u$, the minimum becomes
\[
 m_{4/3}(\theta)=2\left(\frac{\cosh u}{z^2}\right)^{1/3}
               =2^{4/3}(1-2c^2)^{1/3}.
\]
The remaining assertions follow from the scalar classification and continuity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Shape of the minimum value]
\label{prop:schatten-value-convexity}
The function $m_p$ is strictly convex on $[0,\vartheta_p]$ and strictly
concave on $[\vartheta_p,\pi/2]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Below the transition $m_p=2f_p$, and
\cref{eq:schatten-amplitude-derivative} gives the strict convexity.
For $\vartheta_p<\sigma<\pi/2$, let $a<b$ be its two optimizing
eigenphase magnitudes. Thus $a<\vartheta_p<b$, $a+b=2\sigma$, and
$f_p'(a)=f_p'(b)=s>0$. The derivative of $f_p(t)-st$ is positive on
$(a,b)$ and negative on $(b,\pi)$. Consequently
\[
 f_p(t)\leq f_p(b)+s(t-b),\qquad a\leq t\leq\pi,
\]
with equality only at $t=b$. For $a\leq\theta\leq\pi/2$, choose the
admissible phase $\delta=\theta-a$. This gives
\begin{equation}
 m_p(\theta)\leq f_p(a)+f_p(2\theta-a)
       \leq m_p(\sigma)+2s(\theta-\sigma),
 \label{eq:schatten-balanced-support}
\end{equation}
where the second inequality is strict unless $\theta=\sigma$.
In particular, every interior point $\sigma$ of the supercritical
interval has a strict affine upper support over that whole interval.
Taking $\sigma$ to be a strict convex combination of two distinct
angles proves strict concavity, including the endpoints.
\end{proof}

The transition also determines the smoothness of the minimum value.
Fix $p$ and write $c_p=2\sin(\vartheta_p/2)$ and
$\varepsilon=2-p-p\cos\theta$. Expanding the logarithmic ratio at
$(\vartheta_p,0)$ gives
\[
 D_{p,\theta}(\delta)
 =\frac{\delta}{\sin\vartheta_p}
   \left[-\varepsilon+\frac{2-p}{6}\delta^2
       +O(\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon\delta^2+\delta^4)\right].
\]
The implicit function theorem applied after dividing by $\delta$ and
using $\delta^2$ as the variable yields
\begin{equation}
 \delta_p(\theta)^2
    =\frac{6\varepsilon}{2-p}+O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 m_p(\theta)=2f_p(\theta)
    -\frac{3p c_p^{p-2}}{2(2-p)}\varepsilon^2
    +O(\varepsilon^3),
 \label{eq:schatten-transition-asymptotics}
\end{equation}
as $\theta\downarrow\vartheta_p$ from above. In particular, the
nonzero optimizing phase grows as the square root of the excess angle.
The minimum value is continuously differentiable at the transition, but
its one-sided second derivatives are
\[
 m_p''(\vartheta_p-)=0,\qquad
 m_p''(\vartheta_p+)
   =-\frac{3p^3 c_p^{p-2}\sin^2\vartheta_p}{2-p}<0.
\]
Thus the quotient displacement cost loses twice differentiability at an
acute angle determined by $p$, before the geometric right-angle boundary.

\subsection{Schatten-class perturbations in a Hilbert space}

The finite-dimensional formula also gives an attained minimum in the
Schatten ideal. Let $\mathcal S_p$ denote the Schatten class on a separable
complex Hilbert space $\mathcal H$.

\begin{theorem}[An optimum differing by finite rank from direct rotation]
\label{thm:schatten-ideal-minimum}
Let $P,Q$ be orthogonal projections with $P-Q\in\mathcal S_p$ and
\[
 \dim(\operatorname{ran}P\cap\ker Q)
   =\dim(\ker P\cap\operatorname{ran}Q).
\]
List their positive principal angles with multiplicity as
$(\theta_j)_{j\geq1}$, using a finite list when appropriate. Then
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\substack{W\text{ unitary},\ WPW^*=Q\\ I-W\in\mathcal S_p}}
       \|I-W\|_p^p=\sum_j m_p(\theta_j).
 \label{eq:schatten-ideal-minimum}
\end{equation}
For any principal-plane direct rotation $R$, including a choice of pairing
on the right-angle defect spaces, an attaining $W_*$ can be chosen with
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{rank}(W_*-R)
       \leq 2\,\#\{j:\theta_j>\vartheta_p\}<\infty.
 \label{eq:schatten-finite-correction}
\end{equation}
In particular, $R$ is optimal if and only if all principal angles are at
most $\vartheta_p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The decomposition of two projections \citep{halmos1969two}, together with
compactness of $P-Q$, gives orthogonal principal planes for all nonzero
angles and a fixed complement. The defect-space hypothesis allows the
right-angle spaces to be paired. Since the nonzero singular values of
$P-Q$ are $\sin\theta_j$, each twice, we have
$\sum_j\sin^p\theta_j<\infty$ and $\theta_j\to0$ if the list is infinite.
The direct rotation $R$ is the identity on the fixed complement and has
two displacement singular values $2\sin(\theta_j/2)$ on each plane.
The inequality $2\sin(\theta/2)\leq\sqrt2\sin\theta$ on
$[0,\pi/2]$ implies $I-R\in\mathcal S_p$.

First suppose the angle list is infinite, and let $R_N$ be $R$ on its
first $N$ principal planes and the identity elsewhere. Then
$R_N-R\to0$ in $\mathcal S_p$. For any feasible $W$, put $Z=R^*W$.
It commutes with $P$ and satisfies $Z-I\in\mathcal S_p$. The compact
normal operator $Z-I$ has an orthonormal eigenbasis off its kernel.
Because $Z$ commutes with $P$, its finite-dimensional nontrivial
eigenspaces admit bases in $\operatorname{ran}P$ or $\ker P$.
Truncating these eigenspaces gives unitaries $Z_N$ commuting with $P$,
with $Z_N-I$ of finite rank and $Z_N-Z\to0$ in $\mathcal S_p$.

Set $W_N=R_N Z_N$ and $Q_N=R_N P R_N^*$. The ranges of $R_N-I$ and
$Z_N-I$ lie in a common finite-dimensional $P$-invariant space $M_N$.
Both unitaries preserve $M_N$ and equal the identity on its orthogonal
complement. Their product transports $P|_{M_N}$ to $Q_N|_{M_N}$.
The latter pair has the first $N$ positive principal angles of $P,Q$,
with any remaining angles zero. Thus
\cref{thm:schatten-independent-planes} gives
\[
 \|I-W_N\|_p^p\geq\sum_{j=1}^N m_p(\theta_j).
\]
Unitary invariance and the ideal norm inequality yield
\[
 \|W_N-W\|_p
  \leq\|R_N-R\|_p+\|Z_N-Z\|_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
Passing to the limit proves the lower bound. For a finite angle list,
keep $R_N=R$ and use the same approximation of $Z$.

Choose $W_*$ by the independently minimizing phases on the principal
planes and the identity on the fixed complement. Its cost is the sum
in \cref{eq:schatten-ideal-minimum}, bounded above by the finite cost of
$R$. Only finitely many angles exceed the positive number
$\vartheta_p$. Every other plane uses phase zero, proving
\cref{eq:schatten-finite-correction}. The optimality criterion follows
from the scalar classification and its strict improvement above the
transition.
\end{proof}
