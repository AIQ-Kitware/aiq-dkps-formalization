\begin{abstract}
We determine the exact cost of transporting one spectral subspace onto
another by an ambient unitary for a closed two-parameter family of
spectral displacement penalties
$\rho_{p,\tau}(s)=(\tau^2+s^2)^{p/2}-\tau^p$,
$1\le p\le2$ and $\tau\ge0$. For equal-rank subspaces of a finite-dimensional
complex Hilbert space, the unrestricted minimum is the sum of scalar
principal-angle phase minima. We derive a sharp phase-transition surface
characterizing global optimality of canonical direct rotation, including
the nuclear, intermediate Schatten, smoothed, and quadratic boundaries.
For arbitrary complete complex Hilbert spaces, we prove finite-cost
feasibility and attainment exactly when the projection difference belongs
to $S_p$ at zero smoothing or $S_2$ at positive smoothing, and the crossed
defect dimensions agree. The attained value is the corresponding
principal-angle series.

Over the reals, the unsmoothed finite-dimensional problem for
$1\le p<2$ has an exact weighted-matching solution. At the nuclear
endpoint the real minimum equals the complex value
$2\sum_j\sin\theta_j$ precisely when all strictly acute positive angles
have even multiplicity. An intrinsic signed-root criterion extends
attainment of this complex lower bound to trace-class projection pairs
on arbitrary real Hilbert spaces. Complete optimizer classifications
and local-to-global results are proved in specified Schatten and
Charbonnier subfamilies; no all-optimizer classification is asserted for
the full two-parameter family.

The analysis grew from a Lean~4 formalization of the 1970 Davis--Kahan
perturbation paper. Of 29 reviewed result-level statements, 28 are proved
at their reviewed source scope, including unbounded self-adjoint and
operator-ideal formulations. The printed Proposition~4.4 is refuted by a
real four-dimensional counterexample at every positive acute angle.
We distinguish the original direct-rotation and minimal-distortion
results from the exact transport formulas and classifications established
here; all reported mathematical results are formally verified.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Davis--Kahan theory; spectral perturbation;
direct rotations; Schatten norms; spectral penalties; phase transitions; operator ideals; formal verification; Lean.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Let $P$ and $Q$ be orthogonal projections on a Hilbert space.  A
unitary $W$ transporting $\operatorname{ran}P$ to
$\operatorname{ran}Q$ satisfies $WPW^*=Q$.  Such a transporter is
rarely unique.  The canonical direct rotation is distinguished by the
principal-angle geometry of the pair, but its optimality depends on how
the displacement $I-W$ is measured.  This paper studies exact
minimization of spectral displacement penalties over the full unitary
or orthogonal transport fiber, allowing arbitrary coupling between
principal-angle sectors.

The setting originates in classical subspace geometry
\citep{dixmier1948position,davis1958separation,halmos1969two} and the
direct-rotation and perturbation theory of Davis and Kahan
\citep{davis1970rotation,kato1976perturbation}.  Davis established
Hilbert--Schmidt and finite-dimensional Schatten-$p$ optimality for
$p\ge2$ in 1958 \citep[Theorems~7.3--7.4]{davis1958separation}.
The phase-based failure of optimality under some smaller-exponent
costs is also classical.  Bolotnikov, Li, and Rodman subsequently
formulated the ambient unitary minimal-distortion problem and solved
substantial parts of it, including the Frobenius case and Q-norm
results \citep{bolotnikov2001minimal}.  These antecedents determine the
historical scope of the present optimization results; the comparison
is developed in \cref{sec:related}.

Our main objective is the spectral family
\[
 \rho_{p,\tau}(s)=(\tau^2+s^2)^{p/2}-\tau^p,
 \qquad 1\le p\le2,\quad\tau\ge0.
\]
It contains the nuclear and quadratic endpoints, intermediate Schatten
powers, and the smooth Charbonnier penalty.  We determine the global
minimum over \emph{all} ambient complex unitary transporters as a sum
of independent scalar phase minima, and obtain an explicit threshold
for when canonical direct rotation attains that minimum.  The same
family admits an infinite-dimensional version with an exact finite-cost
ideal condition and balanced-defect requirement.  These results are
stated together in \cref{sec:unified-transport}.

The scalar field changes the feasible spectral geometry.  In a real
Hilbert space, phase choices that are possible on an individual complex
principal plane may require coupling distinct real planes.  The exact
finite real Schatten problem below exponent two reduces to a weighted
matching of principal angles.  At the nuclear endpoint, the real
and complex minima agree exactly under an even-multiplicity condition
on strictly acute positive angles.  Its infinite-dimensional
signed-root formulation explains the compatibility obstruction on
actual spectral subspaces; the real theory is treated in
\cref{sec:family-real,sec:nuclear-endpoint}.  The paper does not claim
a complete real smoothed-power transport formula.

The original motivation was a formal audit of the Davis--Kahan 1970
paper.  Davis--Kahan perturbation estimates bound how far invariant
subspaces move when a self-adjoint operator is perturbed, using spectral
separation and residuals \citep{davis1970rotation,wedin1972perturbation}.
The transporter optimization studied here is a different question,
connected to perturbation theory by projections and principal angles.
In particular, the optimal phase formulas do not themselves provide
new spectral-gap or residual bounds.

We formalized the Davis--Kahan arguments in Lean~4 using Mathlib
\citep{demoura2021lean4,mathlib2020}.  The formalization covers 29
reviewed result-level statements: 28 have proved counterparts at
the reviewed source scope, including real and complex Hilbert spaces,
unbounded self-adjoint operators, ordered half-infinite spectral gaps,
and where-defined unitarily invariant operator-ideal norms.  The
printed Proposition~4.4 fails: a real four-dimensional counterexample
exists even for arbitrarily small positive principal angles.  It
realifies the phase mechanism already described by Davis and Kahan
\citep[Example~4.2]{davis1970rotation}.  The source proof contains an
invalid Ky Fan comparison; we state the valid remaining conclusions,
including a sharp direct-rotation approximation factor, in
\cref{sec:prop44,sec:sharp-displacement}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{A common complex optimization theorem.} We prove the exact
 unrestricted minimum and an attaining unitary for the full closed
 $(p,\tau)$ family, for every finite-dimensional complex equal-rank
 subspace pair. A single phase-transition surface characterizes
 canonical global optimality over the entire closed parameter range
 (\cref{thm:family-finite,thm:family-canonical}).
 \item \textbf{The operator-ideal lift.} On any complete complex Hilbert
 space, including nonseparable spaces, finite-cost transport is possible
 precisely under the relevant Schatten ideal condition and equality of
 crossed-defect dimensions. We prove the infinite principal-angle
 minimum series and its attainment
 (\cref{thm:family-infinite}).
 \item \textbf{Real-field compatibility.} We establish exact unrestricted
 finite-dimensional real matching for unsmoothed $1\le p<2$ costs,
 the nuclear real--complex parity criterion, and the signed-root
 equality-attainment criterion on general trace-class projection pairs.
 These results identify where the complex phase construction cannot be
 realized by independent real planes (\cref{sec:family-real,sec:nuclear-endpoint}).
 \item \textbf{Optimizer structure in specific regimes.} Separate verified
 theorems classify all complex global minimizers in the strict Schatten,
 nuclear, and Charbonnier cases. The strict Schatten and Charbonnier
 problems also admit local-to-global theorems and quantitative or
 topological results under their stated hypotheses
 (\cref{sec:schatten-optimization,sec:smoothed-transport}).
 \item \textbf{Formalized classical foundations and a source correction.}
 We formalize the original Davis--Kahan theorem family, refute its
 printed Proposition~4.4, identify the failed proof step, and recover
 its valid norm conclusions. The full-scope sine-theta result includes
 unbounded operators and where-defined ideal norms
 (\cref{sec:sine-theta}).
\end{enumerate}

The detailed source-to-theorem correspondence for the 1970 paper is in
Appendix~\ref{app:dk-inventory}. The mathematical provenance of the
new transport statements and their classical dependencies is recorded
in Appendix~\ref{app:transport-provenance}.

\section{Related work and mathematical provenance}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Principal angles, direct rotation, and perturbation bounds}
\label{sec:related-classical}

The relative-position theory of two closed Hilbert subspaces predates
the Davis--Kahan perturbation estimates.  Dixmier developed the
operator-theoretic structure of pairs of closed subspaces
\citep{dixmier1948position}; Davis explicitly credited that structural
work in his treatment of angle, sine, cosine, and separation operators
\citep[Introduction]{davis1958separation}.  Halmos later gave an
influential account of the geometry of two subspaces
\citep{halmos1969two}.  Direct rotations and the polar-decomposition
methods used here are classical; Kato's perturbation theory provides
another standard treatment \citep{kato1966perturbation,kato1976perturbation}.

Davis's 1958 Theorem~7.2 establishes an extremal inequality for the
squared displacement compressed to the source subspace.
Theorem~7.3 proves Hilbert--Schmidt optimality and Theorem~7.4 gives
finite-dimensional Schatten-$p$ optimality for $p\ge2$
\citep{davis1958separation}.  Davis also discusses failure of the
analogous assertion for $p<2$ near orthogonality.  These results
precede Davis and Kahan's 1970 work and are acknowledged as classical
optimality statements here.

Davis and Kahan's 1970 paper derives perturbation bounds for spectral
subspaces, including the sine-theta and tangent theorems
\citep{davis1970rotation}.  Their Example~4.2 contains the complex
phase mechanism behind nuclear nonminimality.  Their printed real
Proposition~4.4 asserts a stronger small-angle extremal property,
which the real four-dimensional example in \cref{sec:prop44} refutes.
The squared-displacement conclusion and the direct-rotation
construction remain valid.  Qiu, Zhang, and Li later restated the
small-angle full-norm assertion in Section~5 of their Grassmann-space
paper \citep{qiu2006grassmann}; Zhang and Qiu subsequently recognized
trace-norm nonminimality explicitly
\citep{zhang2010canonicalangles}.  We claim a formalized contradiction
of the printed proposition and sharp replacement statements, without
claiming to have discovered the original complex phase mechanism.

Wedin's singular-subspace perturbation bounds and subsequent matrix
and Sylvester-equation treatments concern the stability of spectral
subspaces under changes of an operator
\citep{wedin1972perturbation,stewart1990matrix,grubisic2005sylvester,grubisic2010tan}.
They provide perturbation-theoretic context for the source formalization.
The present transport objective fixes two subspaces and minimizes the
ambient displacement of a unitary relating them; it does not involve
a spectral-gap assumption or a residual.

\subsection{Minimal distortion on a unitary transport fiber}
\label{sec:related-minimal-distortion}

Bolotnikov, Li, and Rodman formulated the ambient constrained problem
of minimizing $\|I-W\|$ over unitary maps transporting one or more
prescribed subspaces \citep[Problem~1.1]{bolotnikov2001minimal}.
Their paper gives the full Frobenius minimum and its minimizing set,
proves Q-norm results under the structural assumptions in their
Theorem~3.1, and includes a failure example for $1\le p<2$
\citep[Lemma~3.3]{bolotnikov2001minimal}.  Their Q-norm class
includes Schatten-$p$ norms for $p\ge2$.  The paper's special-case and
norm-dependent results are the closest direct predecessors of our
optimization problem.  Our closed-family theorem supplies an attained
principal-angle minimum over \emph{every} ambient transporter for all
$1\le p\le2$ and $\tau\ge0$, together with the explicit canonical
phase-transition threshold and an operator-ideal attainment lift.
The separately verified real matching and complete optimizer
classifications address different aspects of the same problem.
The cited 2001 results do not state these full-family formulas or the
real multiplicity criterion; historical priority of equivalent
formulations under other terminology remains a separate question.

The lower bounds use established singular-value majorization and
unitarily invariant norm theory \citep{bhatia1997matrix}.
The complex spectral-gradient and second-variation arguments belong to
the wider theory of differentiating spectral functions
\citep{lewis1996derivatives,lewis2001twice}.  Local-to-global results
for spectral or singular-value \emph{orbits} also appear in work of
Massey, Rios, and Stojanoff \citep{massey2018local}; their orbit
constraint is different from $WPW^*=Q$.  Our local-to-global claims
are limited to the transport problems and penalties for which they
have been proved in \cref{sec:schatten-optimization,sec:smoothed-transport}.

\subsection{Grassmann metrics and Procrustes formulations}
\label{sec:related-objectives}

The principal-angle metrics of Qiu, Zhang, and Li and the angular
inequalities studied by Zhang and Qiu are intrinsic functions of the
subspaces \citep{qiu2006grassmann,zhang2007angular,zhang2010canonicalangles}.
The length of a curve on a Grassmann manifold, a norm of a logarithm,
a projection-distance norm, and the extrinsic chordal transport cost
$\|I-W\|$ are distinct optimization objectives.  Work on Schatten
unitary groups develops rectifiable Finsler distance and minimal-curve
theorems \citep{andruchow2010finsler,larotonda2026shortcurves}; these
results are relevant geometric context without identifying their
geodesic objectives with the present one.

Weighted Procrustes and Parseval-frame approximation results
\citep{contino2017weighted,corach2014procrustes} likewise involve
closely related matrix factorizations, but their feasible sets are
weighted bounded-operator solutions or frame constraints.  The
stabilizer condition in~\eqref{eq:transport-fiber} must be retained
when comparing minima.  A reduction from one formulation to another
would need to preserve that condition and the chosen displacement
penalty; no such general equivalence is assumed here.

\subsection{Smoothing, Schatten ideals, and defect index}
\label{sec:related-ideals}

The generalized Charbonnier form $(\tau^2+s^2)^a$ is a standard
regularization penalty in imaging
\citep{sun2010secrets,lobos2026smooth}.  Subtracting its value at zero
normalizes the present operator cost.  The novelty claim concerns exact
optimization \emph{subject to unitary subspace transport}, not the
invention of this scalar penalty or its use in regularization.

For infinite-dimensional projections, relative index and balanced
crossed defects are classical concerns: Avron, Seiler, and Simon give
index formulas and a unitary equivalence criterion for pairs of
projections \citep{avron1994index}.  The existence of a transporter
with finite Schatten displacement requires additional ideal control;
the classical index obstruction is therefore explicitly credited.
Our contribution in \cref{thm:family-infinite} is the exact
$S_p$-versus-$S_2$ finite-cost boundary, the attained minimum for the
whole smoothed-power family, and the angle-series formula under the
specified ideal and defect conditions.  The real infinite-dimensional
nuclear equality criterion combines this background with the
signed-root compatibility construction.  A general real positive-
smoothing formula and an all-optimizer classification for every family
parameter are outside the proved scope.

\subsection{Prior formalization work and attribution}
\label{sec:related-formal}

The Lean development uses Lean~4 and Mathlib
\citep{demoura2021lean4,mathlib2020}.  Its continuous functional
calculus and basic Hilbert-space constructions reuse accepted
foundations \citep{dedecker2025cfc}.  Parts of the projection-valued-
measure, Borel spectral calculus, and self-adjoint infrastructure were
ported, adapted, or generalized from the Spectra development
\citep{bornemann2026spectra}.  Reusable results are being contributed
to Tau Ceti \citep{tauceti2026}.  The Davis--Kahan source theorems,
where previously established, are described as formalizations of
classical mathematics.  New transport statements, the printed-source
counterexample, and source-level verification are identified separately
in Appendix~\ref{app:transport-provenance}.

\section{Source coverage and the sine-theta theorem}
\label{sec:coverage}

The formalization covers \DKResultCount{} result-level statements in Davis
and Kahan's paper: the Section~2 sine and tangent inequalities, the geometry
and extremal properties of direct rotations, the bounded and unbounded
Sylvester estimates, the generalized Section~6 perturbation bounds, and the
spectral branch-selection theorems. \DKProvedCount{} have checked Lean proofs
with their reviewed source hypotheses; Proposition~4.4 is refuted as printed.
Appendix~\ref{app:dk-inventory} specifies the counting rule and gives the
complete result table, and Appendix~\ref{app:source-review} states the
comparison procedure.

Appendix~\ref{app:notation} collects the symbols used in the sine-theta
and direct-rotation arguments.

These results depend on spectral and operator-ideal constructions that cannot
be reduced to finite matrices without changing the assumptions of the source
statements.

\subsection{The full-scope sine-theta theorem}
\label{sec:sine-theta}

\subsubsection{Trial and exact subspaces}

Davis and Kahan distinguish a reference operator $\sym{A}$ from a perturbed operator
$\sym{A}+\sym{H}$.  For the formal statement we write $\sym{Acurrent}$ for the perturbed full-space
operator, corresponding to the source's $\sym{A}+\sym{H}$.

Let $\sym{Eambient}$ be the ambient Hilbert space.  Let $\sym{A0}$ be a self-adjoint operator on a
trial-coordinate space $\sym{Ftrial}$, and let
\[
  \sym{E0}:\sym{Ftrial}\to \sym{Eambient}
\]
be an isometric coordinate map for the trial subspace.  The residual compares
applying the ambient operator after embedding with applying the trial operator
before embedding:
\begin{equation}
  \sym{R} = \sym{Acurrent}\sym{E0}-\sym{E0}\sym{A0}.
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
When the operators are unbounded, \cref{eq:residual} is asserted on
$\operatorname{dom}(\sym{A0})$ and $\sym{R}$ is the bounded residual that agrees there.
The Lean predicate \texttt{IsTrialResidual} packages the isometry, the required
domain inclusion, and this residual identity.

Let $\sym{F0}:\sym{Hexact}\to \sym{Eambient}$ be isometric coordinates for the desired exact spectral
subspace and let $\sym{F1}:\sym{Gunwanted}\to \sym{Eambient}$ be isometric coordinates for its orthogonal
complement.  A self-adjoint operator $\sym{Lambda1}$ on $\sym{Gunwanted}$ describes the
complementary spectral block through
\[
  \sym{Acurrent}\sym{F1} = \sym{F1}\sym{Lambda1}
\]
on its domain.  The predicate \texttt{IsExactSpectralDecomposition} also records
orthogonality and completeness of the two coordinate ranges.

\subsubsection{The sine operator and its rectangular representative}

The source angle operator acts on the trial-coordinate space.  If
$\sym{Pexact}=\sym{F0}\like{F0}{F_0^*}$ is the orthogonal projection onto the desired exact subspace, then
\[
  \cos\sym{Theta0} = (\like{E0}{E_0^*}\sym{Pexact}\sym{E0})^{1/2},
  \qquad
  \sym{sinTheta0} = (I-\like{E0}{E_0^*}\sym{Pexact}\sym{E0})^{1/2}.
\]
The analytic proof uses the rectangular complementary block
\begin{equation}
  \sym{S}=(I-\sym{Pexact})\sym{E0}.
  \label{eq:rectangular-sine}
\end{equation}
The source-facing Lean theorem is stated on a positive operator on the
trial-coordinate space.  The rectangular block has codomain $\sym{Eambient}$, while the
positive sine has codomain $\sym{Ftrial}$.  Their relationship is
\[
  \sym{S}^*\sym{S}
  = \like{E0}{E_0^*}(I-\sym{Pexact})\sym{E0}
  = I-\like{E0}{E_0^*}\sym{Pexact}\sym{E0},
\]
so
\begin{equation}
  |\sym{S}|=(\sym{S}^*\sym{S})^{1/2}=\sym{sinTheta0}.
  \label{eq:modulus-sine}
\end{equation}
The Lean API constructs the directed angle on trial coordinates by functional
calculus and defines its sine by applying $\sin$ through the same calculus.
Under the isometry hypotheses, Lean proves that this operator is exactly the
modulus $|\sym{S}|$ in \cref{eq:modulus-sine}.  A polar decomposition $\sym{S}=\sym{PolarU}|\sym{S}|$ and
the ideal laws then imply
\[
  \sym{NormN}(\sym{S})=\sym{NormN}(\sym{sinTheta0})
\]
whenever the norm is defined.  The rectangular theorem is the analytic proof
engine; the selected source-facing theorem transports its conclusion to the
positive sine operator.

\subsubsection{Gap geometry and the inequality}

Fix $\sym{delta}>0$. The separation hypothesis has the following alternatives.
For some $\sym{betaEndpoint}\leq\sym{alphaEndpoint}$, one of the two spectra
$\sigma(\sym{A0})$ and $\sigma(\sym{Lambda1})$ lies in $[\sym{betaEndpoint},\sym{alphaEndpoint}]$ and the other
lies in
\[
 (-\infty,\sym{betaEndpoint}-\sym{delta}]\;\cup\;[\sym{alphaEndpoint}+\sym{delta},\infty).
\]
Alternatively, for some real $b$, the operator forms satisfy either
\[
 \sym{A0}\geq (b+\sym{delta})I,\quad \sym{Lambda1}\leq bI,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \sym{A0}\leq bI,\quad \sym{Lambda1}\geq (b+\sym{delta})I.
\]
Each form inequality is asserted on the domain of the corresponding operator;
for example, $\sym{A0}\geq aI$ means
$\langle \sym{A0}x,x\rangle\geq a\|x\|^2$ for every
$x\in\operatorname{dom}(\sym{A0})$. For self-adjoint operators the source's ordered
spectral half-line hypotheses imply these form bounds. The Lean predicate
\texttt{FormBoundedSylvesterGap} records precisely the finite-interval
alternative, in either spectral order, and these two ordered form alternatives.

Both blocks may be unbounded in an ordered configuration. For instance,
on $\ell^2(\mathbb N_{\geq1})$, take diagonal operators
$\sym{A0}e_n=(n+\sym{delta})e_n$ and $\sym{Lambda1}e_n=-ne_n$, on the domains where their
images are square summable. They satisfy the first ordered configuration
with $b=0$, while neither spectrum lies in a finite interval. This example
explains why retaining the half-infinite alternatives changes the theorem's
applicability.

The source also writes a common-domain equality for the unbounded trial
configuration.  That equality discharges the forward domain condition consumed
by \texttt{IsTrialResidual}.  Source correspondence is checked from the combined
hypotheses, with this equality supplying the required domain condition.

Appendix~\ref{app:lean-guide} explains the general Lean syntax in the
following theorem signature; the geometric and spectral hypotheses have been
defined above.

\begin{theorem}[Sine-theta estimate]
\label{thm:sine-theta}
Let the scalar field be $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, let $\sym{Eambient}$ be separable,
and let $\sym{Acurrent}$, $\sym{A0}$, and $\sym{Lambda1}$ be self-adjoint operators on the
Hilbert spaces above. Suppose the isometries, domain inclusions, residual
identity, exact complementary decomposition, and one of the three gap
configurations hold. Let $\sym{NormN}$ be a supported normalized symmetric
operator-ideal norm. Whenever $\sym{sinTheta0}$ and $\sym{R}$ belong to its domain,
\begin{equation}
 \sym{delta}\,\sym{NormN}(\sym{sinTheta0})\leq \sym{NormN}(\sym{R}).
 \label{eq:dk-sine}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The two ideal-membership hypotheses express the ``where-defined'' restriction.
The proof first estimates the rectangular map $\sym{S}=(I-\sym{F0}\like{F0}{F_0^*})\sym{E0}$ and then
uses \cref{eq:modulus-sine} and modulus invariance of the ideal gauge. The
ambient Hilbert-space instances are omitted from the displayed Lean signature.
The theorem contains all three separation configurations encoded by its gap
hypothesis.  The ordered half-infinite cases have distinct hypotheses from
the finite interval/exterior specialization, so source correspondence is checked
against the complete family of gap hypotheses.

\subsubsection{Why the Sylvester estimate gives the angle bound}

Set $\sym{X}=\like{F1}{F_1^*}\sym{E0}$. The exact complementary decomposition gives
$\sym{S}=\sym{F1}\sym{X}$, and multiplication by the isometry $\sym{F1}$ preserves the supported
singular-value gauges. Self-adjointness, the domain hypotheses, and the
residual identity yield the Sylvester relation
\[
 \sym{Lambda1} \sym{X}-\sym{X}\sym{A0}=\like{F1}{F_1^*}\sym{R}
\]
on $\operatorname{dom}(\sym{A0})$, with the left side represented by the bounded
right side. The separated Sylvester estimate gives
\[
 \sym{delta} \sym{NormN}(\sym{X})\leq \sym{NormN}(\like{F1}{F_1^*}\sym{R})\leq \sym{NormN}(\sym{R}).
\]
Combining this with $\sym{NormN}(\sym{X})=\sym{NormN}(\sym{S})=\sym{NormN}(\sym{sinTheta0})$ proves the conclusion.
For unbounded operators, deriving the domain compatibility for $\sym{X}$ is part of
the argument; the displayed relation is not an everywhere-defined composition
of unbounded maps. The constant-one estimate uses the specified interval or
ordered gap geometry, rather than an arbitrary assertion that the two spectra
are disjoint.


The rank-one equality example in \cref{fig:sine-theta-rank-one} takes
$\sym{Acurrent}=\operatorname{diag}(0,\sym{delta})$, $\sym{A0}=0$, and the trial unit vector
$\like{v}{v_\theta}=(\cos\sym{theta},\sin\sym{theta})$.  Then the complementary component
has length $\sin\sym{theta}$ and the residual has length $\sym{delta}\sin\sym{theta}$.
This geometric picture is a finite-dimensional specialization of the
preceding theorem, whose domain and operator-ideal hypotheses remain as
stated above.

\section{Mathematical architecture of the Davis--Kahan development}
\label{sec:dk-rest}

The proofs draw on three closely related areas: the geometry of two
subspaces, symmetric operator ideals and majorization, and Sylvester
estimates for spectrally separated operators.

\subsection{Direct rotations and the geometry of two subspaces}

Let $\sym{Psource}$ and $\sym{Qtarget}$ be the orthogonal projections onto closed subspaces $\sym{Usource}$ and
$\sym{Vtarget}$.  Davis and Kahan organize their geometry through the operators obtained
from $\sym{Psource}$, $\sym{Qtarget}$, and their complements.  In the acute case, the compression
$\sym{Psource}\sym{Qtarget}\sym{Psource}$ has no kernel on $\sym{Usource}$ and determines the cosine of the angle operator;
the complementary compression determines its sine.  Polar decomposition then
supplies the partial isometries that pair the corresponding principal
subspaces.  Combining these pieces gives the direct rotation, an orthogonal or
unitary operator $\sym{Rrotation}$ satisfying $\sym{Rrotation}(\sym{Usource})=\sym{Vtarget}$ and rotating along the principal
planes.

This construction explains several results that look separate when stated only
at theorem level.  Existence and uniqueness in the acute case, the
principal-square-root characterization, commutation with the angle operators,
and reversal symmetry all express compatibility among the same projection and
polar-decomposition data.  The nonacute theory records the additional kernel
conditions needed when principal angles reach $\pi/2$.  For compact angle
operators, the resulting decomposition reduces to the familiar sequence of
principal angles; the general Hilbert-space statements do not require such a
discrete spectrum.

Section~8 returns to this geometry from the spectral side.  Perturbation
smallness and spectral repulsion rule out the wrong branch and place the two
spectral subspaces in the acute regime where the direct rotation is available.
In the formal development this branch-selection argument uses the same
unbounded spectral calculus as the perturbation theorems.  Acuteness therefore
follows from the spectral hypotheses, with no separate matrix-level assumption.

\subsection{Approximation numbers, majorization, and unitarily invariant norms}

Davis and Kahan formulate many extremal statements simultaneously for broad
classes of unitarily invariant norms.  In finite dimensions such a norm can be
described by a symmetric gauge function of the singular values.  The
infinite-dimensional formalization uses approximation numbers as the singular
value sequence of a compact operator and symmetric norming functions to encode
the gauge.  Ky Fan partial sums then provide the bridge from pointwise or
majorization statements to norm inequalities.

This layer is essential in Section~4.  If $\sym{Rrotation}$ is the direct rotation and $\sym{Wcompetitor}$
is any unitary carrying $\sym{Usource}$ onto $\sym{Vtarget}$, the restricted displacement has
singular-value extremality strong enough to imply
unitarily invariant norm minimality.  Davis and Kahan also prove a
majorization theorem for the squared full displacement
$(I-\sym{Wcompetitor})^*(I-\sym{Wcompetitor})$.  Squaring changes the relevant singular values from
$s_j(I-\sym{Wcompetitor})$ to $s_j(I-\sym{Wcompetitor})^2$, so a majorization statement at the squared level
does not automatically imply the corresponding statement for the unsquared
trace norm.  Proposition~4.4 attempts precisely that stronger passage. The
counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:prop44} shows where it fails; the
$\sym{NormQ}$-norm assertion and quantitative bound in
Section~\ref{sec:sharp-displacement} give two valid conclusions.

The same operator-ideal machinery is used in the perturbation inequalities.
For the sine theorem, the statement $\sym{delta}\,\sym{NormN}(\sym{S})\leq \sym{NormN}(\sym{R})$ is meaningful for
any supported symmetric operator ideal once both $S$ and the residual belong to
that ideal.  The formal theorem therefore keeps the source's ``where-defined''
convention and does not build an accidental finite-dimensional compactness
assumption into the statement.

\subsection{Sylvester separation and unbounded operators}

The analytic engine behind the sine and tangent estimates is the Sylvester map
\[
  \sym{X} \longmapsto AX-XB.
\]
Under the interval/exterior or ordered spectral-gap configurations used above,
Davis and Kahan obtain lower bounds of the form
\[
  \delta\,\sym{NormN}(\sym{X}) \leq \sym{NormN}(AX-XB).
\]
The perturbation theorems result by identifying the appropriate off-diagonal or
trial-subspace operator $\sym{X}$ and rewriting its Sylvester residual in terms of the
original perturbation.

The unbounded case forces domain information into this argument.  Expressions
such as $AX-XB$ are only meaningful on the appropriate domains, and the
spectral separation may be encoded by a finite interval/exterior configuration
or by an ordered pair of half-lines.  The reusable development represents
unbounded operators as domain-carrying linear partial maps, develops the
self-adjoint and semibounded interfaces needed by the proof, and connects
spectral hypotheses to a form-bounded Sylvester gap.  Strong spectral cutoffs
reduce parts of the argument to bounded operators; convergence of the relevant
approximation numbers then permits passage back to the unbounded setting.

Section~6 reuses this engine after weakening the trial-coordinate assumptions.
Reflection pinching and direct-sum norm comparisons control the resulting block
operators, while the Sylvester estimate supplies the spectral-gap factor.  This
produces the generalized sine and tangent theorems and the pairwise-gap
square-norm estimate at the scope stated in the source.

\subsection{Formal foundations and ancestry}

Several of the preceding objects had no sufficiently general interface in the
available Lean libraries when this work began.  The development therefore adds
a focused theory of principal angles and projections, approximation numbers and
symmetric operator ideals, polar decomposition and direct rotations, Borel
spectral calculus, unbounded self-adjoint partial maps, and real/complex
transfer.  Complexification is used to prove analytic results once when the
complex spectral theorem is the natural tool and then descend the conclusions
to real Hilbert spaces where Davis and Kahan state them as well.

Mathlib supplies the general algebraic, topological, Hilbert-space, and
continuous-functional-calculus foundation \citep{mathlib2020,dedecker2025cfc}.
Parts of the projection-valued-measure, Borel-calculus, and self-adjoint
infrastructure were copied, adapted, or generalized from Spectra
\citep{bornemann2026spectra}.  Reusable declarations from this project are
organized under the \texttt{TauCeti.*} namespaces and are being prepared for
integration with Tau Ceti \citep{tauceti2026}.  The paper-specific
Davis--Kahan package contains the source statements, examples, and the
Proposition~4.4 counterexample; Appendix~\ref{app:source-review} records the
statement-level review procedure.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For every $1\le p\le2$ and $\tau\ge0$, the complex spectral transport
problem with penalty $\rho_{p,\tau}$ has an attained unrestricted
finite-dimensional minimum given by the sum of principal-angle phase
minima.  A single closed critical-cosine formula specifies exactly when
a canonical direct rotation minimizes that cost.  The theorem includes
the nuclear, intermediate Schatten, Charbonnier, and Frobenius regimes;
it does not require competing transporters to preserve principal planes.

In arbitrary complete complex Hilbert spaces, finite-cost transport is
characterized by membership of the projection difference in $S_p$ at
zero smoothing or $S_2$ at positive smoothing, together with balanced
crossed defects.  Under these conditions the same phase-minimum series
is finite and attained.  This ideal change reflects the behavior of
the penalty near zero.  The relative-index obstruction and the
quadratic direct-rotation case have classical predecessors
\citep{davis1958separation,avron1994index}.

The scalar field affects which complex-optimal phase patterns are
available.  The finite-dimensional real unsmoothed Schatten minimum
below exponent two is an attained weighted matching of principal
angles.  At the nuclear endpoint, equality with the complex minimum
has a sharp even-multiplicity criterion for strictly acute positive
angles.  The signed-root equality certificate extends that criterion
to trace-class real Hilbert-space projection pairs with balanced
defects.  Separate verified results classify every complex optimizer
for the strict unsmoothed Schatten, nuclear, and Charbonnier regimes,
and establish local-to-global and stability properties under their
specific hypotheses.  An all-optimizer classification for arbitrary
$(p,\tau)$ and a general real smoothed-power matching formula remain
outside the results proved here.

The companion Lean formalization proves 28 of 29 reviewed source
statements from Davis and Kahan (1970), including their unbounded
self-adjoint sine-theta result and its operator-ideal hypotheses.
Their printed Proposition~4.4 fails for real subspaces at arbitrarily
small positive principal angles.  Our four-dimensional counterexample
uses the complex phase mechanism already present in that source; the
formal development identifies the invalid proof inequality and
establishes valid replacements.  The new spectral transport theorems
are distinguished from this historical correction and from the
classical minimal-distortion problem \citep{bolotnikov2001minimal}.

The mathematical setting and attribution are summarized in
\cref{sec:related,tab:related-comparison}, with formal theorem evidence
and source correspondence in the appendices.  The present optimization
results do not assert improved spectral-gap perturbation constants or
downstream algorithmic rates; those require separate links between the
ambient transport cost and the error quantity used in an application.

\section{Reading the Lean signatures}
\label{app:lean-guide}
\label{app:reading-lean}

A Lean theorem declaration has the form
\[
  \texttt{theorem name (parameters) (hypotheses) : conclusion := proof}.
\]
Parameters introduce the mathematical objects under discussion.  A hypothesis
such as \texttt{(hA : IsSelfAdjoint A)} supplies the corresponding fact;
bundled predicates such as \texttt{IsTrialResidual} and
\texttt{IsExactSpectralDecomposition} collect related assumptions.  Braces mark
arguments normally inferred by Lean, while square brackets request typeclass
instances supplying ambient algebraic, analytic, or topological structure.  The
proposition after the final colon is checked by Lean's kernel
\citep{demoura2021lean4}.

In \Cref{lst:dk-sintheta-headline}, \texttt{RCLike} is the Mathlib abstraction
covering $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$.  A type
\texttt{ContinuousLinearMap K F E} is a continuous linear map.  A type
\texttt{LinearPMap K E E} is a linear partial map whose
domain is a submodule; this is the representation used for possibly unbounded
operators.  \texttt{N.Mem T} states that the ideal gauge associated with $\sym{NormN}$ is
finite on $T$, and \texttt{N.gaugeReal T} is the corresponding real value.  The
suffix \texttt{Real} refers to the codomain of the gauge and not to the scalar
field.
