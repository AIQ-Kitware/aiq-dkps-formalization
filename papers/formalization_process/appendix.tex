\section{Formalization procedure}
\label{app:system}

\Cref{sec:system} summarizes the workflow.  Source transcriptions, prose
summaries, result records, and review judgments were kept outside individual
model conversations so work could continue across sessions.  The formalization
used a pinned Lean/Mathlib environment and reusable local foundations; exact
dependency revisions and helper commands are supplied with the artifact rather
than repeated here.  Mechanical checks established the Lean declarations and
their types, while correspondence judgments were recorded separately.  Source
review included inherited section assumptions and the fields of structures
occurring in a theorem type, because both can restrict a claim without changing
its concluding inequality.

Source review compared each tracked Davis--Kahan result with the elaborated Lean
statement and definitions that affected its meaning.  Recurring questions were
the scalar field, dimension, bounded or unbounded operator scope, norm class,
gap conditions, constants, directionality, and the identity of angle and
residual operators.  Mechanical checks established properties of the formal
artifact; they did not determine whether our reading of the source was correct.

ChatGPT and Claude were used for implementation, mathematical discussion,
search, and review.  Human supervision consisted mainly of monitoring progress,
supplying framing, asking models to explain inconsistencies, and requesting
additional or independent reviews.  The humans directing the formalization were
not Davis--Kahan subject-matter experts, so disagreements about source meaning
were often investigated by probing the models in different ways rather than by
an independent expert reading of the theorem.

\section{Sine-angle norms and Formalization 1}
\label{app:formalization1}

The coordinate spaces in the Lean signatures are \texttt{E} for the ambient
space, \texttt{F} for the trial coordinates, \texttt{H} for the desired exact
coordinates, and \texttt{G} for the complementary exact coordinates.  The maps
$E_0,F_0,F_1$ embed the last three spaces isometrically into the first.  In
finite dimensions these maps can be viewed as matrices with orthonormal
columns.  An adjoint, written $^*$ in mathematics and \texttt{.adjoint} in
Lean, is then the conjugate transpose.  The notation $\circ_L$ composes bounded
linear maps, with the right-hand map applied first.  Thus the expression
inside \texttt{N.gaugeReal} in \cref{lst:current-sintheta} first embeds a
trial vector and then removes its component in the desired exact subspace.

\paragraph{Why this map represents the sine of the angle.}
Recall $Q=F_0F_0^*$ and $S=(I-Q)E_0$.  The map $Q$ is an orthogonal
projection because $F_0$ is isometric.  For one-dimensional trial and exact
subspaces, choose coordinates in which the exact subspace is the horizontal
axis and the trial unit vector is $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, with
$0\le\theta\le\pi/2$.  Then
\[
  S(1)=(0,\sin\theta),\qquad |S|(1)=\sin\theta.
\]
The two outputs have the same length and lie in different spaces: $S$ maps
trial coordinates into the ambient space, while $|S|$ acts on trial coordinates.

For general subspaces, isometry gives $E_0^*E_0=I$, and projection gives
$(I-Q)^2=I-Q$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  S^*S=E_0^*(I-Q)E_0=I-E_0^*QE_0.
  \label{eq:sine-gram}
\end{equation}
The operator $E_0^*QE_0$ is positive and bounded by the identity.  Put
$C=(E_0^*QE_0)^{1/2}$.  Davis--Kahan define the angle on trial coordinates
by $\Theta_0=\arccos C$; their equation~(1.16) uses the same positive
square root in its coordinate notation.  Applying the identity
$\sin(\arccos t)=\sqrt{1-t^2}$ on $0\le t\le1$ through functional calculus
and using \cref{eq:sine-gram} gives
\[
  \sin\Theta_0=(I-C^2)^{1/2}=(S^*S)^{1/2}=|S|.
\]
In finite dimensions, the eigenvalues of $|S|$ are the singular values of $S$,
namely the sines of the principal angles.  The positive-square-root identity
also applies in infinite dimensions, without a discrete list of eigenvectors.

\paragraph{Why the norms agree.}
Polar decomposition writes $S=U|S|$, where $U$ preserves lengths on the closure
of the range of $|S|$ and is zero on its orthogonal complement.  This
\emph{partial isometry} and its adjoint are contractions, and $|S|=U^*S$.
Davis--Kahan's compatibility law says that composing with a contraction cannot
increase the chosen norm.  Applying it to these two factorizations yields
\[
  N(S)\le N(|S|),\qquad N(|S|)\le N(S).
\]
This proves \cref{eq:sine-norm-representation}.  It also preserves the domain
on which the norm is defined.  The argument needs neither compactness nor an
extension of $U$ to a unitary operator.  Davis--Kahan state the resulting
identity $N((I-Q)E_0)=N(\sin\Theta_0)$ in their Section~1
\citep{davis1970rotation}.

The exact decomposition also gives $I-Q=F_1F_1^*$, so
$S=F_1(F_1^*E_0)$.  Removing or restoring the isometric factor $F_1$
preserves the norm.  This connects $S$ to the complementary coordinate block
$F_1^*E_0$ used in the source proof.

\paragraph{The equality hypothesis in Formalization 1.}
The parameter \texttt{sinTheta}\(_0\) names $S$.  The hypothesis
\texttt{hSinTheta}\(_0\) fixes it to $(I-F_0F_0^*)E_0$, so the supplied
coordinate maps determine its value.  This is a choice of name for a
bounded map and imposes no additional condition on those subspaces.  Its
modulus is the positive operator $\sin\Theta_0$ used in the source.
\Cref{lst:current-sintheta} substitutes the map directly into the conclusion.

The historical gap hypothesis \texttt{hspectral} requires one block to have
spectrum in a finite interval.  For example, on $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ let $D$
be the self-adjoint diagonal operator with entries $1,2,3,\ldots$.  The blocks
$A_0=-D$ and $\Lambda_1=D$ have a gap of two, although neither has bounded
spectrum.  They fit the source's half-infinite configuration and cannot satisfy
that finite-interval hypothesis.  Taking $A=(-D)\oplus D$, the first-summand
inclusion for $E_0=F_0$, and the second-summand inclusion for $F_1$ gives such
a source instance with $R=S=0$.

The two bracketed analytic assumptions in \cref{lst:sintheta-mismatch}
supply a minimax lower bound for singular values and a Ky Fan norm estimate
for unbounded Sylvester equations.

\section{Reading Formalization 2}
\label{app:formalization2}

\paragraph{The norm expression.}
The theorem quantifies over a normalized symmetric operator-ideal family $N$.
Such a family assigns compatible norms to operators between Hilbert spaces.
Its underlying gauge takes values in $[0,+\infty]$, with $+\infty$ marking
operators outside the norm's domain.  Thus \texttt{N.Mem T} asserts that this
gauge is finite on $T$, and \texttt{N.gaugeReal T} converts that finite value
to a real number.  The suffix \texttt{Real} refers to the value of the norm;
the operator itself may be real or complex.

In \cref{lst:current-sintheta}, the two membership premises are
$S\in\operatorname{dom}N$ and $R\in\operatorname{dom}N$.  The body of the
left-hand gauge is precisely the map fixed by \texttt{hSinTheta}\(_0\) in
\cref{lst:sintheta-mismatch}.  The conclusion therefore reads
\[
  \delta\,N(S)\le N(R),
\]
which is \cref{eq:sin-theta} by the norm identity proved in
\cref{app:formalization1}.  The membership premises ensure that each
real-valued gauge is being used on its finite domain.

The record includes the rank-one normalization and contraction compatibility
specified in Davis--Kahan Section~1.  It also includes the where-defined
Ky Fan comparison cited there: inequalities for every Ky Fan norm imply the
corresponding inequality for the chosen norm when both quantities exist.
The sine-theta theorem uses that comparison through the norm record.  It does
not independently derive it from unitary invariance alone.

\paragraph{The gap hypothesis.}
\texttt{FormBoundedSylvesterGap A0 Lambda1 delta} has three constructors.
One expresses finite interval/exterior separation, allowing either block to
occupy the interval.  The other two express ordered half-infinite separation
by quadratic-form bounds: one block is bounded above by $c$ and the other
bounded below by $c+\delta$, in either order.  For example, the first bound
means $\langle A_0x,x\rangle\le c\|x\|^2$ for every $x$ in its domain.
For self-adjoint operators, spectral containment in the corresponding
half-lines implies these form bounds.  This supplies the source assumptions
required to use either ordered constructor.

\paragraph{The domain hypotheses.}
The operators $A,A_0,\Lambda_1$ are self-adjoint and may be unbounded.
The maps $E_0,F_0,F_1,R$ are bounded.  Davis--Kahan's common-domain assumption
for $AE_0$ and $E_0A_0$ is
\[
  \{x:E_0x\in\operatorname{dom}A\}=\operatorname{dom}A_0.
\]
It implies the forward inclusion in \texttt{IsTrialResidual}; self-adjointness
of $A_0$ supplies density of that domain.  The same predicate states the
residual identity there.  The exact-decomposition predicate supplies the
orthogonal coordinate maps and the intertwining relation for $\Lambda_1$.
These conditions require domain compatibility only where the unbounded
operators are applied.
