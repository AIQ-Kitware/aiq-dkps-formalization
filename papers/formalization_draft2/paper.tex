\begin{abstract}
We report a source-faithful \Lean{} 4 formalization of Yu, Wang, and Samworth's
widely used statistical variant of the Davis--Kahan theorem.  The formalization
uses a paper-keyed source census: a progress-and-audit instrument constructed
from the source rather than from the Lean tree, so that missing formalizations
remain visible.  The current census has \YWSCensusEntryCount{} tracking entries,
covering named results, equations, identities, examples, source conventions,
and explicitly labeled extensions beyond the printed paper.  All
\YWSCensusVerifiedCount{} entries currently resolve to declarations in the
default build; this is a verification statement about the census, not a claim
that YWS contains that many named theorems.  Treating the source as an object to audit rather than
merely a theorem to restate exposed two printed source defects in YWS and, in a
deeper audit of the 1970 source, an explicit finite-dimensional counterexample
to Davis--Kahan Proposition 4.4.

The effort also required a substantial prerequisite layer of operator theory,
principal-angle geometry, singular-value machinery, unitarily invariant norms,
and spectral perturbation infrastructure that was not available at the required
interface in Mathlib.  These results are open source, are reused by a formalized
contemporary statistical theorem family, and are being organized for contribution
through an operator-theory roadmap in \TauCeti{}.

Finally, we study what this development cost and how human--LLM collaboration
actually occurred.  The repository contains a partially observed resource ledger
and retained interaction traces.  At the pinned analysis snapshot, the current
ledger records at least \LedgerTurns{} model turns, \LedgerOutputTokens{} output
tokens, and \LedgerBillableInputTokens{} tokens in its billable-input accounting
measure.  These are strict measured lower bounds: \CommitsWithoutExactAccounting{}
of \HistoryCommitCount{} commits lack exact commit-level accounting, and
\CommitsBeforeInstrumentation{} commits precede the instrumentation commit.
We therefore distinguish measured commit-attributed usage, measured but
commit-unattributed session usage, and genuinely unmeasured work, and we treat
extrapolation as a missing-data problem rather than reporting a single total.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Davis--Kahan sine-theta theorem is a standard tool for turning perturbations
of a symmetric operator into perturbation bounds for eigenspaces
\citep{davis1970rotation,chen2021spectral}.  In statistics, the variant of
\citet{yu2015davis} is particularly useful because it replaces an awkward
sample-and-population separation condition with a population eigengap and gives
aligned-frame and singular-subspace forms suited to statistical estimation.
A one-line citation to these results conceals substantial mathematical
infrastructure: spectral subspaces, principal angles, singular-value comparison,
orthogonal alignment, unitarily invariant norms, and the interfaces connecting
finite-dimensional matrix statements to operator theory.

This paper asks what happens when that entire chain is made executable in a
proof assistant.  Our main object is not an isolated convenient ``Davis--Kahan
lemma,'' but the published YWS paper as a source document.  The resulting
formalization serves three roles: a verification of a central statistical
perturbation result, an audit that can expose defects in the literature, and a
reusable mathematical layer for later statistical formalizations.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The intended contribution list is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A source-faithful formalization of YWS at the published theorem numbering
        and generality, tracked by a paper-keyed census whose
        \YWSCensusEntryCount{} entries make absent, corrected, generalized, and
        deliberately extra material visible.
  \item Machine-checked diagnoses and repairs of two printed YWS source defects:
        the false polynomial identity in equation (4) and the rank-boundary
        convention in Theorem 3.
  \item A source audit of \DK{} Proposition 4.4, including an explicit
        $\mathbb{R}^4$ trace-norm counterexample, a machine-checked localization
        of the proof defect to the printed equation (4.3), and a repaired theorem
        surface.
  \item A demonstration that the resulting spectral machinery is reusable in a
        contemporary end-to-end statistical theorem family (Quench), rather than
        being an isolated verification artifact.
  \item A quantitative and qualitative study of the human--LLM development
        process, with explicit treatment of incomplete telemetry, session-level
        backfill, chat-interface work, and extrapolation uncertainty.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Paper thesis.}
The broader claim we aim to support is that source-faithful formalization can be
\emph{executable scholarship for mathematical statistics}: it both verifies and
audits the literature, while converting prerequisite work into an amortizable
formal substrate for subsequent results.

\paragraph{The importance of the population gap in statistics.}
The change made by Yu, Wang, and Samworth is small syntactically but important
for statistical use.  In a typical application, $\Sigma$ is a population
matrix determined by the statistical model, while $\widehat{\Sigma}$ is a
random estimator.  Consequently, the population eigengap
\[
  \Delta
  =
  \min\{
    \lambda_{r-1}(\Sigma)-\lambda_r(\Sigma),
    \lambda_s(\Sigma)-\lambda_{s+1}(\Sigma)
  \}
\]
is a deterministic property of the model, whereas gaps involving eigenvalues
of $\widehat{\Sigma}$ are themselves random quantities.  A perturbation theorem
whose separation hypothesis mixes the population and sample spectra therefore
creates an additional probabilistic obligation: before using the eigenspace
bound, one must first prove that the random sample eigenvalues remain suitably
separated.

Theorem~2 of Yu, Wang, and Samworth removes that intermediate dependence.  Once
a population gap $\Delta>0$ is assumed, a bound on
$\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma$ can be converted directly into a bound on the
estimated eigenspace.  This gives the modular form routinely needed in
statistical arguments:
\[
  \text{population separation}
  \;+\;
  \text{stochastic matrix-estimation error}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \text{eigenspace-estimation error}.
\]
The denominator is supplied by assumptions on the population model, while
  concentration or random-matrix arguments control the numerator.
This distinction highlights the importance of semantic alignment: a
  formalization that silently introduced a sample-eigengap assumption could
  still prove a valid perturbation theorem, but it would miss much of the
  statistical content of the Yu--Wang--Samworth result.

\subsection{The Davis--Kahan headline theorem as an auditable Lean statement}
\label{sec:dk-headline}

The first headline result in Section~2 of \citet{davis1970rotation} is the
sine-theta theorem.  In the paper's coordinate setup, let $E_0$ parameterize a
trial subspace with restricted operator $A_0$, let $F_0$ parameterize the exact
comparison subspace, let $F_1$ parameterize its orthogonal complement with
$AF_1=F_1\Lambda_1$, and let
\[
  R=AE_0-E_0A_0,
  \qquad
  \sin\Theta_0=(I-F_0F_0^*)E_0.
\]
If, for some $\beta\leq\alpha$ and $\delta>0$, either
\[
  \operatorname{spec}(A_0)\subset[\beta,\alpha],
  \qquad
  \operatorname{spec}(\Lambda_1)\cap
    (\beta-\delta,\alpha+\delta)=\varnothing,
\]
or the same condition holds with $A_0$ and $\Lambda_1$ interchanged, then for
every unitarily invariant norm
\begin{equation}
  \delta\,\lVert\sin\Theta_0\rVert\leq\lVert R\rVert.
  \label{eq:dk-sintheta-headline}
\end{equation}
The source states this in the real/complex, infinite-dimensional setting and
allows the relevant self-adjoint operators to be unbounded when the residual is
bounded.  Our headline declaration retains that scope.  In the Lean listings
below, the declaration text through its opening \texttt{:= by} is copied
verbatim from the corresponding \texttt{.lean} source file; only the
\texttt{listings} glyph substitutions needed for pdf\LaTeX{} rendering are
applied.  Imports, namespace/open commands, ambient section variables, and proof
bodies remain available in the source file but are not repeated here.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Canonical Lean surface for the Davis--Kahan sine-theta theorem.},
  label={lst:dk-sintheta-headline}]
theorem sinTheta_headline
    [ContinuousLinearMap.HasMinMaxLowerBoundEverywhere.{u, v} \KField]
    [HasUnboundedSylvesterKyFan.{u, v} \KField]
    (N : UnitaryInvariantNorm)
    (A : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := E))
    (A₀ : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := F))
    (Λ₁ : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := G))
    (E₀ : F →L[\KField] E)
    (F₀ : H →L[\KField] E)
    (F₁ : G →L[\KField] E)
    (sinTheta₀ : F →L[\KField] E)
    (R : F →L[\KField] E)
    (hSinTheta₀ :
      sinTheta₀ =
        (ContinuousLinearMap.id \KField E - F₀ ∘L F₀.adjoint) ∘L E₀)
    (hA : A.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (hA₀ : A₀.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (hΛ₁ : Λ₁.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (htrial : IsTrialResidual A A₀ E₀ R)
    (hexact : IsExactSpectralDecomposition A Λ₁ F₀ F₁)
    {β α δ : ℝ}
    (hβα : β ≤ α)
    (hδ : 0 < δ)
    (hspectral :
      (LinearPMap.realSpectrum A₀.toLinearPMap ⊆ Set.Icc β α ∧
          LinearPMap.realSpectrum Λ₁.toLinearPMap ⊆
            {x : ℝ | x ≤ β - δ ∨ α + δ ≤ x}) ∨
        (LinearPMap.realSpectrum Λ₁.toLinearPMap ⊆ Set.Icc β α ∧
          LinearPMap.realSpectrum A₀.toLinearPMap ⊆
            {x : ℝ | x ≤ β - δ ∨ α + δ ≤ x}))
    (hR : N.Mem R) :
    δ * N.gauge sinTheta₀ ≤ N.gauge R := by
\end{lstlisting}

\Cref{lst:dk-sintheta-headline} is both the presentation-facing theorem and the
declaration selected by our semantic-alignment audit.  The reader can therefore
read the proposition after the final colon first---it is the source inequality
in \cref{eq:dk-sintheta-headline}---and then scan upward to see how each named
object is tied to the source setup.  In particular, \texttt{sinTheta\textsubscript{0}}
is an explicit parameter rather than an opaque local definition, and the
hypothesis \texttt{hSinTheta\textsubscript{0}} identifies it literally with
$(I-F_0F_0^*)E_0$.  The two predicates \texttt{IsTrialResidual} and
\texttt{IsExactSpectralDecomposition} package the domain-heavy unbounded
operator bookkeeping; the audit expands both through characteristic theorems,
so they do not hide extra mathematical assumptions.  The bracketed typeclass
arguments are library-level capabilities for the generic real/complex scalar
setting, rather than additional hypotheses on the operators appearing in the
source statement.

The underlying proof theorem additionally certifies that \texttt{sinTheta0}
belongs to the norm ideal.  That fact is mathematically implicit when the source
writes a finite unitarily invariant norm, but it is kept explicit in the lower
formal layer because the implementation totalizes the real-valued gauge outside
its ideal.  The headline declaration above projects that stronger certificate
to the literal source inequality rather than changing its mathematical content.

\section{A Source-Faithful Formalization of YWS}
\label{sec:yws}

The package \texttt{YuWangSamworth2015} is organized around the published
Biometrika numbering rather than around whichever auxiliary theorem was easiest
to expose in Lean.  Formalization progress is tracked by a \emph{paper-keyed
source census}: its rows are created from claims and audit obligations in the
source document, not discovered by enumerating declarations already present in
the Lean tree.  Consequently a missing formalization remains a visible row
instead of disappearing from a coverage report.

The current census contains \YWSCensusEntryCount{} entries.  They are not
\YWSCensusEntryCount{} named theorems: the rows include theorem conclusions,
lemmas, corollaries, equations, identities, examples, a definition, source
scope/convention checks, and exposition/audit material.  It also records
\YWSCensusExplicitAdditionCount{} items explicitly marked as additions beyond
the printed paper, so that repository extensions are not accidentally
attributed to YWS.  All \YWSCensusVerifiedCount{} entries currently have
\texttt{verification=proved\_in\_build}.  The independent census status field
records whether a row matches the source exactly, is generalized or
specialized, is a compiled correction to a defective source statement, or is
not source proof debt.

\subsection{The population-gap theorem}

Let $\Sigma,\widehat\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}$ be symmetric, with
population eigenvalues $\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_p$.  For
$1\leq r\leq s\leq p$, let $d=s-r+1$ and let $V,\widehat V\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}$
contain orthonormal eigenvectors at the ordered indices $r,\ldots,s$.  Define
\[
  \Delta=
  \min\{\lambda_{r-1}-\lambda_r,\lambda_s-\lambda_{s+1}\},
  \qquad
  \lambda_0=+\infty,\quad \lambda_{p+1}=-\infty.
\]
The main contribution of \citet{yu2015davis}, Theorem~2, states that if
$\Delta>0$, then
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\rVert_{\mathrm F}
  \leq
  \frac{2\min\{\sqrt d\,\lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
                    \lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm F}\}}
       {\Delta},
  \label{eq:yws-sintheta}
\end{equation}
and there exists $\widehat O\in O(d)$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\widehat V\widehat O-V\rVert_{\mathrm F}
  \leq
  \frac{2^{3/2}\min\{\sqrt d\,\lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
                         \lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm F}\}}
       {\Delta}.
  \label{eq:yws-aligned}
\end{equation}
Only the population spectrum enters $\Delta$; the supplied sample eigenframe may
therefore be chosen arbitrarily inside a repeated sample eigenspace.

The Lean statement in \cref{lst:yws-theorem2-sintheta} is again the same
canonical declaration used by the semantic-alignment audit.  Lean uses
zero-based indices, so \texttt{r <= s}, \texttt{s < p}, and
\texttt{d = s - r + 1} encode the same consecutive block.  The sine quantity
\texttt{sinThetaNorm} is tied to its concrete realization by the equality
hypothesis immediately above the claim.  The right-hand side literally uses the
local source spellings \texttt{\textbardbl SigmaHat - Sigma\textbardbl\_op}
and \texttt{\textbardbl SigmaHat - Sigma\textbardbl\_F}; their exact
notation context is reproduced in \cref{app:lean-listing-context}.  Thus a
reader who starts after the final colon sees essentially
\cref{eq:yws-sintheta}.  The predicates \texttt{IsEigenvectorBlock} and
\texttt{SourcePopulationGap} are expanded in the generated audit; the latter
pins \texttt{Delta} to the exact source denominator (with an explicit full-space
endpoint convention), rather than merely assuming an arbitrary lower bound.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Canonical Lean surface for the first conclusion of YWS Theorem 2.},
  label={lst:yws-theorem2-sintheta}]
theorem theorem2_sinTheta
    {p d r s : Nat}
    (Sigma SigmaHat :
      EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p) →ₗ[Real] EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hSigma : Sigma.IsSymmetric) (hSigmaHat : SigmaHat.IsSymmetric)
    (hr : r ≤ s) (hs : s < p) (hd : d = s - r + 1)
    (V Vhat : Fin d → EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hV : IsEigenvectorBlock Sigma hSigma hr hs hd V)
    (hVhat : IsEigenvectorBlock SigmaHat hSigmaHat hr hs hd Vhat)
    (sinThetaNorm : Real)
    (hSinThetaNorm :
      sinThetaNorm =
        sinThetaFrobenius (Submodule.span Real (Set.range V))
          (Submodule.span Real (Set.range Vhat)))
    (Delta : Real) (hDelta : 0 < Delta)
    (hgap : SourcePopulationGap Sigma hSigma r s Delta) :
    sinThetaNorm ≤
      2 * min
        (Real.sqrt d * ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_op)
        ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_F / Delta := by
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Aligned frames and rectangular singular subspaces}

The second conclusion is represented just as literally.  In

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Canonical Lean surface for the aligned-frame conclusion of YWS Theorem 2.},
  label={lst:yws-theorem2-aligned}]
theorem theorem2_alignedFrame
    {p d r s : Nat}
    (Sigma SigmaHat :
      EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p) →ₗ[Real] EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hSigma : Sigma.IsSymmetric) (hSigmaHat : SigmaHat.IsSymmetric)
    (hr : r ≤ s) (hs : s < p) (hd : d = s - r + 1)

    (hV : IsEigenvectorBlock Sigma hSigma hr hs hd V)
    (hVhat : IsEigenvectorBlock SigmaHat hSigmaHat hr hs hd Vhat)

\section{Formalization as Source Audit}
\label{sec:audit}

\subsection{Two defects in YWS}

The formalization does not silently repair source defects.  First, printed
equation (4) is a false polynomial identity: it is missing a square, and the
repository contains both a concrete counterexample to the printed formula and a
proof of the corrected identity.  Second, the printed convention
$\sigma^2_{\operatorname{rank}(A)+1}:=-\infty$ in Theorem 3 is incorrect at the
rank boundary.  When $s=\operatorname{rank}(A)$ it makes a denominator infinite
and can force a zero upper bound even when the sample and population singular
subspaces are orthogonal.  The intended theorem is repaired by the ambient-index
boundary convention used in the paper's own Gram-matrix proof.

$\|v-u\|^2=4/5$.  The two sides of the identity as printed are therefore
\[
(2\langle v,u\rangle)^2(1-\langle v,u\rangle^2)
   = \frac{576}{625},
\qquad
\frac14\|v-u\|^2
  (2-\|v-u\|^2)
  (4-\|v-u\|^2)
   = \frac{96}{125},
\]
so they are unequal.  The correction is exactly one square:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:yws-equation4-corrected}
(2\langle v,u\rangle)^2(1-\langle v,u\rangle^2)
=
\frac14\|v-u\|^2
  (2-\|v-u\|^2)^2
  (4-\|v-u\|^2).
\end{equation}
The counterexample is machine checked by
\path{yuWangSamworth_equation4_printed_counterexample}, while
\path{yuWangSamworth_equation4} proves
\cref{eq:yws-equation4-corrected} for arbitrary real unit vectors; both
declarations are in
\path{YuWangSamworth2015/Symmetric/AngleIdentity.lean}.

The rank-boundary defect in Theorem~3 also occurs in the smallest nontrivial
dimension.  Let
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both matrices have rank one.  Taking $r=s=1$, their population and sample top
right singular subspaces are the perpendicular coordinate axes, and hence
\[
\|\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\|_F=1.
\]
Under the printed conventions
$\sigma_0^2:=+\infty$ and
$\sigma_{\operatorname{rank}(A)+1}^2:=-\infty$, however, both boundary
separations are infinite:
\[
\sigma_0^2-\sigma_1^2=+\infty,
\qquad
\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2=+\infty.
\]
Thus the printed denominator is infinite and its asserted upper bound collapses
to zero, contradicting the displayed value above.  This concrete $2\times2$
example is formalized as
\path{yuWangSamworth_theorem3_printed_rankBoundary_refutation_euclidean}
in \path{YuWangSamworth2015/Rectangular/RankBoundary.lean}.

The correction is to take the boundary in the ambient Gram spectrum, as the
proof of Theorem~3 itself does.  For right singular subspaces of
$A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}$, extend the singular values by
$\sigma_j(A)=0$ for
$\operatorname{rank}(A)<j\le q$ and place the terminal convention at
$\sigma_{q+1}^2:=-\infty$; for left singular subspaces the corresponding
ambient endpoint is $p+1$.  Consequently the theorem uses a finite
$\Delta>0$ satisfying
\[
\Delta
\le
\min\left\{
  \sigma_{r-1}^2-\sigma_r^2,\,
  \sigma_s^2-\sigma_{s+1}^2
\right\},
\]
with the singular values interpreted at the appropriate ambient dimension.
For the example above, the lower boundary is therefore the genuine Gram
eigenvalue gap
\[
\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2=1-0=1,
\]
rather than $+\infty$.  The corrected boundary reduction is formalized by
\path{singularBoundaryGap_of_rank_le}; the corrected right- and left-singular
subspace statements are
\path{yuWangSamworth_rightSingularSubspace_block_le} and
\path{yuWangSamworth_leftSingularSubspace_block_le}, respectively, in
\path{YuWangSamworth2015/Rectangular/SingularBlock.lean}.

\subsection{Davis--Kahan Proposition 4.4 is false as printed}

A separate audit of the 1970 source produced an explicit four-dimensional
counterexample to Proposition 4.4 for the trace norm.  With two principal angles
$\pi/4$, the direct rotation has trace displacement
$4\sqrt{2-\sqrt{2}}$, while an admissible orthogonal competitor has trace
displacement $2\sqrt{2}$.  Thus the claimed minimizing property fails inside the
stated $\Theta\leq\pi/3$ regime.

The same witness also localizes a specific defect in the published proof.  This
should not be confused with \emph{Proposition 4.3}, which is a separate and valid result: the defect is the
paper's displayed \emph{equation (4.3)} inside the proof of Proposition 4.4.
Writing $K=I-W$ and taking the two principal-plane projectors
$\Omega_1E=\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_3\}$ and
$\Omega_2E=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$, the source passes from its valid
Ky Fan variational identity to the asserted lower bound
\[
  \lVert K\rVert_4 \geq
  \lVert K\Omega_1\rVert_2+\lVert K\Omega_2\rVert_2.
\]
For the counterexample, however,
\[
  \lVert K\rVert_4=2\sqrt2
  \quad\text{while}\quad
  \lVert K\Omega_1\rVert_2=\lVert K\Omega_2\rVert_2=2,
\]
so the asserted inequality would require $2\sqrt2\geq4$.  The Lean theorem
\texttt{davisKahanEquation4\_3\_\hspace{0pt}refuted} machine-checks these block values and
the resulting contradiction.
The underlying issue is that orthogonality of the domain planes does not imply
orthogonality of their images under $K$, so their singular-value sums need not
add.  On this witness, the later blockwise calculation where the $\pi/3$ bound
is consumed behaves as claimed; the invalid step is the earlier summation
across blocks.

This proof defect explains where the published argument for Proposition 4.4
breaks, but the theorem-level refutation does not depend on that diagnosis: the
strict trace-norm comparison above directly falsifies Proposition 4.4.  By
contrast, Proposition 4.3 concerns the \emph{squared} displacement
$(I-W)^*(I-W)$ and survives.  It is proved in the finite-dimensional formalization
as \texttt{directRotation\_displacementSquare\_uiNorm} and at the paper-facing
Ky Fan level as \texttt{proposition4\_3\_squaredDisplacement\_kyFan}.  The
counterexample illustrates the distinction: the relevant partial sums of
squared singular values are dominated even though the corresponding partial
sums of singular values are not.

The repository also contains a repaired result for the $Q$-norm class.  We
separately investigated whether the trace-norm failure inside the advertised
short-angle regime had already been identified in the mathematical literature.
The search, last updated 14 August 2026, included exact theorem-number and
$\pi/3$ queries; direct rotation combined with trace, nuclear, and Schatten-norm
terminology; the concrete angle pattern $(\pi/4,\pi/4)$ versus $(\pi/2,0)$ and
its displacement costs; erratum/correction/counterexample terminology; publisher
records; and citation snowballing.

That search found important nearby prior work but not the same correction.
Bolotnikov, Li, and Rodman showed by 2001 that canonical completion can fail for
Schatten $p<2$ norms, including the trace norm, but their explicit negative
example is the orthogonal-subspace endpoint $\theta=\pi/2$, outside the
Davis--Kahan hypothesis $\theta_{\max}\leq\pi/3$ \citep{bolotnikov2001minimal}.
Qiu, Zhang, and Li later repeated the Davis--Kahan short-angle minimality claim
rather than flagging it as false \citep{qiu2006grassmann}.  We also found no
SIAM-recorded correction attached to the original article.  We therefore do
not make an unconditional priority claim: the narrower historical conclusion is
that we found no indexed or widely recognized correction, or equivalent
counterexample, showing failure of Proposition~4.4 within its stated real
short-angle regime.  Non-digitized material, subscriber-only citation databases,
and private correspondence remain limitations, and this search should be
repeated immediately before submission.  The standalone technical note and
historical-search supplement preserve the fuller semantic-alignment and search
audit.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Resource accounting begins after substantial development, and transcript
        retention limits how much of the earlier period can be reconstructed.
  \item Chat-interface work is not recoverable from the local harness ledger;
        historical attribution depends on separate evidence and author review.
        Missing chat logs also limit reconstruction of initiative and steering,
        not merely token totals.
  \item Pending transcript spans can be broad.  Timestamp overlap identifies
        candidate commits but does not justify a per-commit token allocation.
  \item Missingness is not random, so extrapolated totals are model-dependent and
        should be presented with sensitivity analyses rather than as ground truth.
        Some model variants have no direct measured calibration in the ledger.
  \item Dollar costs depend on historically applicable model- and token-class
        rates.  Measured token counts remain primary evidence; monetary totals
        should be reported only with sourced rates and effective dates.
  \item Path-based component labels overlap and are only a first approximation
        to mathematical dependency-level cost attribution.
  \item Formal verification establishes the Lean statement.  Claims that a
        formal result corrects a prose source require a separate semantic-alignment
        argument, which we make explicit in the YWS and Davis--Kahan audits.
  \item The AI-formalization literature is changing rapidly; related-work and
        priority claims need a final contemporaneous search.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The paper's intended conclusion is not that Davis--Kahan can be ``prompted into
existence.''  The development history instead shows the cost of making a
classical statistical citation executable at source-level fidelity.  That cost
produced more than sine-theta theorem statements: it exposed defects in the
literature, forced foundational mathematical interfaces into existence, enabled
a downstream statistical formalization, and created open-source infrastructure
that can be amortized by later work.

The accounting analysis complements the mathematical contribution by making the
development process falsifiable and inspectable.  Where telemetry exists we
report measurements; where it is only session-level we preserve that ambiguity;
and where it is missing we model it explicitly as missing data rather than
retroactively treating an incomplete ledger as a complete project bill.

\section{Lean listing context}
\label{app:lean-listing-context}

The theorem declarations printed in the main text are literal source text: we
do not shorten qualifiers, rename binders, or substitute presentation-only Lean
syntax.  What the listings omit is the surrounding module context that a reader
would see by opening the referenced source file.  In particular,
\texttt{DavisKahan1970.sinTheta\_headline} is declared in a module with
\texttt{open TauCeti}; consequently the source itself spells the proposed
Tau-Ceti extension \texttt{TauCeti.LinearPMap.realSpectrum} as
\texttt{LinearPMap.realSpectrum}.  The underlying \texttt{LinearPMap} partial
linear-map type is Mathlib's; the real-spectrum API used here is part of the
operator-theory layer currently proposed for Tau Ceti.  Likewise,
\texttt{UnitaryInvariantNorm} is the public source-facing norm interface in the
Davis--Kahan development.  These namespace and import choices are context, not
text silently removed from the displayed theorem declaration.

For the YWS headline statements, the application package introduces the
following reducible abbreviation and local notations so that the two
perturbation norms have the same visible roles as in the printed theorem:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Exact local norm context used by the YWS headline declarations.},
  label={lst:yws-frobenius-abbrev}]
noncomputable abbrev frobeniusNorm {p : Nat}
    (A : EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p) →ₗ[Real] EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p)) : Real :=
  UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm.frobenius Real (EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p)) A

local notation "‖" A "‖_op" => ‖LinearMap.toContinuousLinearMap A‖
local notation "‖" A "‖_F" => frobeniusNorm A
\end{lstlisting}
Because \texttt{frobeniusNorm} is an \texttt{abbrev} and the two notations
expand directly to their displayed right-hand sides, this context introduces no
additional mathematical hypothesis.  It only gives the compiling theorem the
source spellings \texttt{\textbardbl A\textbardbl\_op} and
\texttt{\textbardbl A\textbardbl\_F}.
