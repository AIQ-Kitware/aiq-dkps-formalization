\begin{abstract}
We formalize in Lean~4 all \FormalizationDKResultCount{} mathematical results
established by Davis and Kahan in their 1970 paper: the four unnumbered
Section~2 theorems and every named theorem, proposition, lemma, and corollary.
Twenty-eight are proved at the scope stated in the source; Proposition~4.4 is
false as printed and is formally refuted and repaired.  We also formalize the
theorem-level claims of Theorems~1--3 of Yu, Wang, and Samworth (YWS), including
both conclusions of Theorem~2 and the right-, left-, and aligned-frame forms of
Theorem~3.  Formal comparison with the published arguments exposes two printed
YWS defects.

Reproducing these results at their stated scope required substantial supporting
theory absent from the available Lean library.  The resulting development spans
\FormalizationBasicTheoryCount{} areas of spectral geometry and operator theory,
including principal angles, singular-subspace geometry, unitarily invariant
norms, Borel spectral calculus, unbounded self-adjoint operators, direct
rotations, operator angles, and Sylvester equations.

We also report lower bounds on large language model (LLM) resource use as
  baselines for comparing formalization efficiency and extrapolate them to
  obtain an order-of-magnitude estimate of project-wide resource usage.
These records do not capture the full formalization cost, but commit-level
  provenance enables comparisons on individual proof developments.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Davis--Kahan sine-theta theorem is a standard tool for converting
perturbations of a self-adjoint operator into perturbation bounds for its
spectral subspaces \citep{davis1970rotation,chen2021spectral}.  In statistics,
the Yu--Wang--Samworth (YWS) variant \citep{yu2015davis} is widely used
because its separation condition depends only on the population spectrum and
its conclusions include aligned-frame and singular-subspace bounds suited to
estimation problems.  On 17 August 2026, OpenAlex recorded
\OpenAlexDavisKahanCitations{} and \OpenAlexYuWangSamworthCitations{} citing works
for Davis--Kahan and YWS, respectively, while Google Scholar recorded
\GoogleScholarDavisKahanCitations{} and \GoogleScholarYuWangSamworthCitations{}
citations.

The population-gap formulation in YWS is especially useful in statistical
arguments.  If $\Sigma$ is a population matrix and $\widehat\Sigma$ is a random
estimator, a gap defined from the spectrum of $\Sigma$ is fixed by the model,
while a gap involving $\widehat\Sigma$ is random and requires an additional
probabilistic argument.  YWS Theorem~2 converts a bound on
$\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma$ directly into an eigenspace bound under a population gap.
This yields the common pattern
\[
  \text{population separation}
  \;+\;
  \text{matrix-estimation error}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \text{eigenspace-estimation error}.
\]

\paragraph{Lean and formalization.}
Lean~4 is an interactive theorem prover based on dependent type theory
\citep{demoura2021lean4}.  Mathematical definitions, theorem statements, and
proofs are represented as typed expressions that are checked by Lean's kernel;
Mathlib supplies a large shared library of formalized mathematics
\citep{mathlib2020}.  In this work, formalizing a source theorem means encoding
its mathematical objects and hypotheses in Lean and constructing a proof of the
corresponding proposition at the scope asserted by the source.  The formalization
was developed with large language model (LLM) assistance through the ChatGPT
chat interface, Claude Code, and Codex, with repeated comparison of the Lean
statements against the papers.  Appendix~\ref{app:formalization-process}
describes the process.

Davis--Kahan and YWS provide useful targets for this kind of formalization for
two related reasons.  First, translating the papers into checked statements and
proofs provides a detailed audit of hypotheses, boundary conventions, and
intermediate identities; in this project that process exposed errors in both
sources.  Second, the published results rely on a broad layer of projection
geometry, principal angles, singular-subspace comparison, unitarily invariant
norms, spectral calculus, real/complex transfer, direct rotations, operator
angles, and Sylvester equations.  Formalizing the papers therefore required
building reusable mathematical infrastructure beyond the final perturbation
inequalities.  We also retained lower-bound LLM resource measurements so that
the computational scale of the formalization, and possible improvements in
artificial intelligence (AI) formalization workflows, can be studied
quantitatively.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formalize all \FormalizationDKResultCount{} mathematical results
        established in \citet{davis1970rotation}.  Twenty-eight are proved at the
        scope stated in the source, including the infinite-dimensional and
        unbounded-operator cases where asserted; Proposition~4.4 is refuted and
        repaired.  For \citet{yu2015davis}, we formalize the theorem-level claims
        of Theorems~1--3 and their supporting arguments.  The precise coverage is
        given in Appendix~\ref{app:source-coverage}.
  \item Formalization exposes two printed defects in YWS and the failure of
        Davis--Kahan Proposition~4.4.  We prove corrected YWS statements, give an
        explicit $\mathbb{R}^4$ counterexample to Proposition~4.4, identify the
        failing step in its proof, and prove a repaired Davis--Kahan result.
  \item We develop the missing spectral, geometric, and operator-theoretic
        foundations required by these proofs.  \Cref{sec:foundations} summarizes
        \FormalizationBasicTheoryCount{} theory areas and credits the portions
        adapted from prior formal work.
  \item We preserve lower-bound model-resolved resource measurements for the
        formalization, quantify their coverage of the Git history, and give
        revisable operational-energy estimates and coarse project-level
        extrapolations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Main Results in Mathematics and Lean}
\label{sec:main-results}

\textbf{We formalize every one of the \FormalizationDKResultCount{} mathematical
results established in the Davis--Kahan paper.}  Twenty-eight are proved at the
scope asserted by Davis and Kahan; Proposition~4.4 is false as printed, and we
formalize both its refutation and a repaired result.  We also
formalize the theorem-level claims of YWS Theorems~1--3, including both
conclusions of Theorem~2 and the right-, left-, and aligned-frame conclusions of
Theorem~3.

This section exhibits the central perturbation statements beside their Lean
signatures.  The examples show how the mathematical hypotheses and conclusions
are represented without reproducing the full formal development.  The complete
source correspondence and the precise coverage conventions are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:source-coverage}.

\subsection{The Davis--Kahan Sine Theta Theorem in Lean}
\label{sec:dk-headline}

The first headline result in Section~2 of \citet{davis1970rotation} is the
sine-theta theorem.  In the paper's coordinate setup, let $E_0$ parameterize a
trial subspace with restricted operator $A_0$, let $F_0$ parameterize the exact
comparison subspace, let $F_1$ parameterize its orthogonal complement with
$AF_1=F_1\Lambda_1$, and let
\[
  R=AE_0-E_0A_0,
  \qquad
  \sin\Theta_0=(I-F_0F_0^*)E_0.
\]
If, for some $\beta\leq\alpha$ and $\delta>0$, either
\[
  \operatorname{spec}(A_0)\subset[\beta,\alpha],
  \qquad
  \operatorname{spec}(\Lambda_1)\cap
    (\beta-\delta,\alpha+\delta)=\varnothing,
\]
or the same condition holds with $A_0$ and $\Lambda_1$ interchanged, then for
every unitarily invariant norm
\begin{equation}
  \delta\,\lVert\sin\Theta_0\rVert\leq\lVert R\rVert.
  \label{eq:dk-sintheta-headline}
\end{equation}
The source states this in the real/complex, infinite-dimensional setting and
allows the relevant self-adjoint operators to be unbounded when the residual is
bounded.  The Lean signature retains that scope.  In \Cref{lst:dk-sintheta-headline},
$\Bbbk$ is the real or complex scalar field.  The types $E,F,G,H$ are complete
Hilbert spaces over $\Bbbk$: $E$ is the ambient space, while $F,H,G$ carry the
trial, exact, and complementary exact coordinates, respectively.  The symbol
$N$ names the unitarily invariant norm, and the two square-bracketed assumptions
provide the min--max and unbounded Sylvester estimates used by the proof.
Appendix~\ref{app:reading-lean} summarizes the general Lean notation used in the
formalizations.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Lean signature for the Davis--Kahan sine-theta theorem.
  \texttt{IsTrialResidual} supplies the residual-domain conditions,
  \texttt{IsExactSpectralDecomposition} supplies the exact-subspace conditions,
  and \texttt{N.Mem R} states that the residual belongs to the norm ideal.},
  label={lst:dk-sintheta-headline}]
theorem sinTheta_headline
    [ContinuousLinearMap.HasMinMaxLowerBoundEverywhere.{u, v} \KField]
    [HasUnboundedSylvesterKyFan.{u, v} \KField]
    (N : UnitaryInvariantNorm)
    (A : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := E))
    (A₀ : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := F))
    (Λ₁ : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := G))
    (E₀ : F →L[\KField] E)
    (F₀ : H →L[\KField] E)
    (F₁ : G →L[\KField] E)
    (sinTheta₀ : F →L[\KField] E)
    (R : F →L[\KField] E)
    (hSinTheta₀ :
      sinTheta₀ =
        (ContinuousLinearMap.id \KField E - F₀ ∘L F₀.adjoint) ∘L E₀)
    (hA : A.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (hA₀ : A₀.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (hΛ₁ : Λ₁.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (htrial : IsTrialResidual A A₀ E₀ R)
    (hexact : IsExactSpectralDecomposition A Λ₁ F₀ F₁)
    {β α δ : ℝ}
    (hβα : β ≤ α)
    (hδ : 0 < δ)
    (hspectral :
      (LinearPMap.realSpectrum A₀.toLinearPMap ⊆ Set.Icc β α ∧
          LinearPMap.realSpectrum Λ₁.toLinearPMap ⊆
            {x : ℝ | x ≤ β - δ ∨ α + δ ≤ x}) ∨
        (LinearPMap.realSpectrum Λ₁.toLinearPMap ⊆ Set.Icc β α ∧
          LinearPMap.realSpectrum A₀.toLinearPMap ⊆
            {x : ℝ | x ≤ β - δ ∨ α + δ ≤ x}))
    (hR : N.Mem R) :
    δ * N.gauge sinTheta₀ ≤ N.gauge R := by
\end{lstlisting}

The central statistical result of YWS, Theorem~2, has two conclusions.  For the
next two subsections, let $\Sigma,\widehat\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}$ be
symmetric, with population eigenvalues
$\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_p$.  For $1\leq r\leq s\leq p$, set
$d=s-r+1$ and let $V,\widehat V\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}$ contain orthonormal
eigenvectors at the ordered indices $r,\ldots,s$.  Define
\[
  \Delta=
  \min\{\lambda_{r-1}-\lambda_r,\lambda_s-\lambda_{s+1}\},
  \qquad
  \lambda_0=+\infty,\quad \lambda_{p+1}=-\infty.
\]
Only the population spectrum enters $\Delta$, so the sample eigenframe may be
chosen arbitrarily inside a repeated sample eigenspace.

\subsection{Yu--Wang--Samworth Sine-Angle Bound}
\label{sec:yws-sintheta}

The first conclusion of YWS Theorem~2 states that, when $\Delta>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\rVert_{\mathrm F}
  \leq
  \frac{2\min\{\sqrt d\,\lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
                    \lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm F}\}}
       {\Delta}.
  \label{eq:yws-sintheta}
\end{equation}
In \Cref{lst:yws-theorem2-sintheta}, \texttt{IsEigenvectorBlock} packages the
ordered orthonormal eigenvector conditions, \texttt{SourcePopulationGap}
encodes the population-gap condition, and \texttt{hSinThetaNorm} identifies the
auxiliary scalar \texttt{sinThetaNorm} with the left side of
\cref{eq:yws-sintheta}.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Lean signature for the sine-angle conclusion (2) of YWS Theorem~2.},
  label={lst:yws-theorem2-sintheta}]
theorem theorem2_sinTheta
    {p d r s : Nat}
    (Sigma SigmaHat :
      EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p) →ₗ[Real] EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hSigma : Sigma.IsSymmetric) (hSigmaHat : SigmaHat.IsSymmetric)
    (hr : r ≤ s) (hs : s < p) (hd : d = s - r + 1)
    (V Vhat : Fin d → EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hV : IsEigenvectorBlock Sigma hSigma hr hs hd V)
    (hVhat : IsEigenvectorBlock SigmaHat hSigmaHat hr hs hd Vhat)
    (sinThetaNorm : Real)
    (hSinThetaNorm :
      sinThetaNorm =
        sinThetaFrobenius (Submodule.span Real (Set.range V))
          (Submodule.span Real (Set.range Vhat)))
    (Delta : Real) (hDelta : 0 < Delta)
    (hgap : SourcePopulationGap Sigma hSigma r s Delta) :
    sinThetaNorm ≤
      2 * min
        (Real.sqrt d * ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_op)
        ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_F / Delta := by
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Yu--Wang--Samworth Aligned-Frame Bound}
\label{sec:yws-aligned}

The second conclusion of YWS Theorem~2 states that there exists an orthogonal
matrix $\widehat O\in O(d)$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\widehat V\widehat O-V\rVert_{\mathrm F}
  \leq
  \frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\min\{\sqrt d\,\lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
                         \lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm F}\}}
       {\Delta}.
  \label{eq:yws-aligned}
\end{equation}
\Cref{lst:yws-theorem2-aligned} uses the same hypotheses as
\cref{lst:yws-theorem2-sintheta}; its conclusion constructs the alignment in
\cref{eq:yws-aligned} and proves the stated Frobenius bound.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Lean signature for the aligned-frame conclusion (3) of YWS Theorem~2.},
  label={lst:yws-theorem2-aligned}]
theorem theorem2_alignedFrame
    {p d r s : Nat}
    (Sigma SigmaHat :
      EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p) →ₗ[Real] EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hSigma : Sigma.IsSymmetric) (hSigmaHat : SigmaHat.IsSymmetric)
    (hr : r ≤ s) (hs : s < p) (hd : d = s - r + 1)
    (V Vhat : Fin d → EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hV : IsEigenvectorBlock Sigma hSigma hr hs hd V)
    (hVhat : IsEigenvectorBlock SigmaHat hSigmaHat hr hs hd Vhat)
    (Delta : Real) (hDelta : 0 < Delta)
    (hgap : SourcePopulationGap Sigma hSigma r s Delta) :
    ∃ O : Matrix (Fin d) (Fin d) Real,
      O ∈ Matrix.orthogonalGroup (Fin d) Real ∧
        Real.sqrt (∑ i, ‖(∑ j, O j i • Vhat j) - V i‖ ^ 2) ≤
          2 * Real.sqrt 2 *
            min
              (Real.sqrt d * ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_op)
              ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_F / Delta := by
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Yu--Wang--Samworth Rectangular Extension}
\label{sec:yws-rectangular}

YWS Theorem~3 extends the same population-gap principle to singular subspaces of
rectangular matrices.  For $D=\widehat A-A$ and a block of $d$ consecutive
singular values, the right-singular-subspace bound has numerator
\[
  2(2\sigma_1+\lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}})
  \min\{\sqrt d\,\lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm F}\},
\]
and denominator given by the corresponding population gap between squared
singular values; the same bound holds for left singular subspaces, with an
aligned-frame conclusion as well.  The formal proof follows the argument in the
paper through the Gram operators $A^*A$ and $AA^*$.  The boundary convention in
the printed theorem requires the correction described in \cref{sec:audit}.

\section{Formalization as Source Audit}
\label{sec:audit}

Formalization audits a mathematical source at the level of individual
hypotheses, boundary conventions, and intermediate identities.  Errors that can
pass through an informal argument may become immediate proof obligations or
small counterexamples.  The two papers here provide examples involving an
algebraic identity, a boundary convention, and an extremal claim.  All
counterexamples and corrected statements discussed in this section are verified
in Lean; Appendix~\ref{app:source-coverage} gives the corresponding declarations.

\subsection{Two Defects in YWS}

Printed equation~(4) is a false polynomial identity: it is missing a square.  We
prove both a concrete counterexample to the printed formula and the corrected
identity.  The printed convention
$\sigma^2_{\operatorname{rank}(A)+1}:=-\infty$ in Theorem~3 is incorrect at the
rank boundary.  When $s=\operatorname{rank}(A)$ it makes a denominator infinite
and can force a zero upper bound even when the sample and population singular
subspaces are orthogonal.  The intended theorem is repaired by the ambient-index
boundary convention used in the paper's own Gram-matrix proof.

For equation~(4), a two-dimensional numerical example already exposes the
error.  Take
\[
u=(1,0),\qquad v=(3/5,4/5).
\]
Then $\|u\|=\|v\|=1$, $\langle v,u\rangle=3/5$, and
$\|v-u\|^2=4/5$.  The two sides of the identity as printed are therefore
\[
(2\langle v,u\rangle)^2(1-\langle v,u\rangle^2)
   = \frac{576}{625},
\qquad
\frac14\|v-u\|^2
  (2-\|v-u\|^2)
  (4-\|v-u\|^2)
   = \frac{96}{125},
\]
so they are unequal.  The correction is exactly one square:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:yws-equation4-corrected}
(2\langle v,u\rangle)^2(1-\langle v,u\rangle^2)
=
\frac14\|v-u\|^2
  (2-\|v-u\|^2)^2
  (4-\|v-u\|^2).
\end{equation}
The same formal development proves the corrected identity for arbitrary real
unit vectors.

The rank-boundary defect in Theorem~3 also occurs in the smallest nontrivial
dimension.  Let
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both matrices have rank one.  Taking $r=s=1$, their population and sample top
right singular subspaces are the perpendicular coordinate axes, and hence
\[
\|\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\|_F=1.
\]
Under the printed conventions
$\sigma_0^2:=+\infty$ and
$\sigma_{\operatorname{rank}(A)+1}^2:=-\infty$, however, both boundary
separations are infinite:
\[
\sigma_0^2-\sigma_1^2=+\infty,
\qquad
\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2=+\infty.
\]
Thus the printed denominator is infinite and its asserted upper bound collapses
to zero, contradicting the displayed value above.

The correction is to take the boundary in the ambient Gram spectrum, as the
proof of Theorem~3 itself does.  For right singular subspaces of
$A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}$, extend the singular values by
$\sigma_j(A)=0$ for
$\operatorname{rank}(A)<j\le q$ and place the terminal convention at
$\sigma_{q+1}^2:=-\infty$; for left singular subspaces the corresponding
ambient endpoint is $p+1$.  Consequently the theorem uses a finite
$\Delta>0$ satisfying
\[
\Delta
\le
\min\left\{
  \sigma_{r-1}^2-\sigma_r^2,\,
  \sigma_s^2-\sigma_{s+1}^2
\right\},
\]
with the singular values interpreted at the appropriate ambient dimension.
For the example above, the lower boundary is therefore the Gram eigenvalue gap
\[
\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2=1-0=1,
\]
in place of $+\infty$.  The formalization uses this ambient boundary in the
corrected right- and left-singular-subspace statements.

\subsection{Davis--Kahan Proposition 4.4 Is False as Printed}

For Davis--Kahan Proposition~4.4, we prove an explicit four-dimensional
counterexample for the trace norm.  With two principal angles
$\pi/4$, the direct rotation has trace displacement
$4\sqrt{2-\sqrt{2}}$, while an admissible orthogonal competitor has trace
displacement $2\sqrt{2}$.  Thus the claimed minimizing property fails inside the
stated $\Theta\leq\pi/3$ regime.

The same witness also localizes a specific defect in the published proof.
Proposition~4.3 is a separate valid result.  The defective step is the displayed
\emph{equation (4.3)} inside the proof of Proposition~4.4.
Writing $K=I-W$ and taking the two principal-plane projectors
$\Omega_1E=\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_3\}$ and
$\Omega_2E=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$, the source passes from its valid
Ky Fan variational identity to the asserted lower bound
\[
  \lVert K\rVert_4 \geq
  \lVert K\Omega_1\rVert_2+\lVert K\Omega_2\rVert_2.
\]
For the counterexample, however,
\[
  \lVert K\rVert_4=2\sqrt2
  \quad\text{while}\quad
  \lVert K\Omega_1\rVert_2=\lVert K\Omega_2\rVert_2=2,
\]
so the asserted inequality would require $2\sqrt2\geq4$.
The two domain planes are orthogonal, while their images under $K$ overlap;
the corresponding singular-value sums therefore fail to add.  On this witness,
the later blockwise calculation where the $\pi/3$ bound is consumed behaves as
claimed; the invalid step is the earlier summation across blocks.

The strict trace-norm comparison directly falsifies Proposition~4.4,
independently of the diagnosis of its printed proof.  Proposition~4.3 concerns
the squared displacement $(I-W)^*(I-W)$ and remains valid.  The
finite-dimensional formalization proves that result both at the unitarily
invariant norm level and in the source's Ky Fan formulation.  On the counterexample,
the relevant partial sums of squared singular values satisfy the required
domination while the corresponding partial sums of singular values fail it.

We also prove a repaired result for the $Q$-norm class and searched for prior
treatments of the trace-norm failure inside the advertised
short-angle regime.  Bolotnikov, Li, and Rodman showed by 2001 that canonical
completion can fail for Schatten $p<2$ norms, including the trace norm, but their
explicit negative example is the orthogonal-subspace endpoint $\theta=\pi/2$,
outside the Davis--Kahan hypothesis $\theta_{\max}\leq\pi/3$
\citep{bolotnikov2001minimal}.  Qiu, Zhang, and Li later repeated the
Davis--Kahan short-angle minimality claim \citep{qiu2006grassmann}.  To the best
of our knowledge, no published correction or equivalent counterexample
identifies failure of Proposition~4.4 within its stated real short-angle regime.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Large formal developments such as Flyspeck and the Liquid Tensor Experiment
illustrate how source-driven verification can also produce reusable library
infrastructure \citep{hales2017kepler,scholze2022liquid}; Mathlib provides the
shared Lean foundation on which the present work builds \citep{mathlib2020}.
LLM-assisted formalization has progressed from natural-language
translation and sketch-guided proving \citep{wu2022autoformalization,jiang2023dsp}
to long-horizon Lean workflows \citep{zhang2026leanmarathon}.  Recent work also
formalizes statistical learning theory and related spectral results in Lean
\citep{zhang2026slt,zhang2026sltrepo}.

Several current systems suggest different ways to organize AI-assisted Lean
work.  MathCode maintains a persistent Lean environment and reusable theorem
library, while AutoLean uses compile-repair loops with semantic evaluation and
replanning \citep{mathcode2026,autolean2026}.  Use of AI during
formalization and the resource demands of long-running math agents have
also been studied directly \citep{collins2026humanai,anthropic2026zeta}.  The
resource record in \cref{sec:process-study} provides a historical baseline for
comparing such workflow choices on parts of this formalization.

\section{Remarks}
\label{sec:remarks}

At the outset, the authors carrying out the formalization had effectively no
prior experience with formal theorem proving.  The primary author had also not
studied most of the spectral and operator theory used here beyond basic linear
algebra and set theory.  AI assistance supported both learning the necessary
mathematics and constructing the Lean development.

Formalization itself became part of that learning process: making hypotheses,
definitions, and proof obligations explicit provided a structured way to study
unfamiliar material.  By repeatedly comparing the Lean statements with the
source papers, reviewing them with humans and other models, and checking the
proofs in Lean, we learned enough to evaluate the correspondence with
confidence.  The scale of the resulting formalization is striking given that
starting point.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formalized all \FormalizationDKResultCount{} mathematical results established
in Davis--Kahan and the theorem-level claims of YWS Theorems~1--3.  The process
exposed errors in both sources and required substantial new Lean theory across
spectral geometry, unitarily invariant norms, functional calculus, unbounded
self-adjoint operators, direct rotations, operator angles, and Sylvester
equations.  These foundations are reusable beyond the two papers, with prior
formal work credited where it supplied or shaped part of the implementation.

The observed portion of the project also required substantial computational
resources.  Because a subset of the measurements is linked to individual Git
commits, selected pre-commit states can serve as a historical baseline for
experiments with alternative workflows such as MathCode and AutoLean
\citep{mathcode2026,autolean2026}.  Comparing those workflows while controlling
the model, available context, stopping criteria, and acceptance criteria
would help quantify how far the computational cost of large formalization
projects can be reduced while retaining comparable theorem coverage and proof
checking.

\section{Reading the Lean Signatures}
\label{app:reading-lean}

A Lean theorem declaration has the form
\[
  \texttt{theorem name (parameters) (hypotheses) : conclusion := proof}.
\]
Parameters introduce the mathematical objects under discussion.  A hypothesis
such as \texttt{(hA : A.IsSelfAdjoint)} supplies a proof of the proposition
\texttt{A.IsSelfAdjoint}; bundled predicates such as
\texttt{IsTrialResidual} and \texttt{IsExactSpectralDecomposition} collect
several related assumptions behind a single proposition.  Braces mark arguments
that Lean normally infers, while square brackets request typeclass instances
that provide ambient algebraic or analytic structure.  The proposition after
the final colon is the theorem's conclusion; the term after \texttt{:=} is its
proof \citep{demoura2021lean4}.

\paragraph{Notation used in the displayed formalizations.}
The scalar type $\Bbbk$ in \Cref{lst:dk-sintheta-headline} is constrained by
\texttt{RCLike}, the Mathlib abstraction covering $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$.
The notation \texttt{Fin n} is an $n$-element index type, and \texttt{Nat} and
\texttt{Real} are the natural and real numbers.  In Lean, a type of the form
\lstinline[style=leanpaper]!F →L[\KField] E! is a continuous linear map and
\lstinline[style=leanpaper]!E →ₗ[\KField] E! is a linear map.
\texttt{ClosedOperator} is a possibly unbounded closed linear operator;
\texttt{.IsSelfAdjoint} asserts self-adjointness; \texttt{.adjoint} is the
adjoint; and \lstinline[style=leanpaper]!F₀ ∘L F₀.adjoint! denotes composition
with an adjoint continuous linear map.  In the YWS formalizations,
\texttt{EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p)} is $\mathbb{R}^p$,
\texttt{Matrix (Fin d) (Fin d) Real} is a real $d\times d$ matrix,
\texttt{Matrix.orthogonalGroup} is $O(d)$, \texttt{\ensuremath{\bullet}} is
scalar multiplication, and \texttt{\ensuremath{\sum}} is finite summation.
For the Davis--Kahan norm, \texttt{N : UnitaryInvariantNorm} specifies the norm,
\texttt{N.Mem T} asserts that $T$ lies in its norm ideal, and \texttt{N.gauge T}
is its value on $T$.  Standard logical and set symbols retain their usual
mathematical meanings.

Lean treats proofs of the same proposition as definitionally equal.  Thus a
hypothesis contributes the fact that its proposition holds, without creating a
mathematical distinction between different proof terms.  This proof-irrelevance
principle is also why later theorems can use a proved proposition without
depending on the internal form of its proof \citep{demoura2021lean4}.
