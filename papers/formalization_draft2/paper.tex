\begin{abstract}
We report a source-faithful \Lean{} 4 formalization of Yu, Wang, and Samworth's
widely used statistical variant of the Davis--Kahan theorem, together with the
Davis--Kahan results needed to support and audit that development.  Comparing
the formal statements with the published sources exposed two printed defects in
YWS and produced an explicit finite-dimensional counterexample to Davis--Kahan
Proposition 4.4.

The development required a substantial prerequisite layer.  A compiler-backed
inventory of the formalized YWS and Davis--Kahan result surfaces identifies
\FormalizationBasicTheoryCount{} basic theory areas, including principal-angle
and singular-subspace geometry, Borel spectral calculus, unbounded
self-adjoint operator theory, unitarily invariant norms, direct rotations,
operator angles, and Sylvester equations.  We report the scope of these local
theories qualitatively and record formalization provenance so that adapted or
ported material receives explicit credit.

We also quantify the resources used by the formalization.  At the pinned
analysis snapshot, the retained ledger records at least \LedgerTurnsScientific{}
model turns, \LedgerOutputTokensScientific{} output tokens, and
\LedgerBillableInputTokensScientific{} tokens in its billable-input accounting
measure.  Exact commit-level telemetry is unavailable for about
\CommitsWithoutExactAccountingScientific{} commits, and some earlier or
chat-interface work has no equivalent telemetry.  Under versioned public-price
and operational-serving assumptions, the measured usage has API-equivalent
value on the order of \MeasuredApiEquivalentCostScientific{}~USD and central
modeled operational emissions on the order of
\ModeledOperationalCarbonScientific{}~kg~CO$_2$e.  The ledger measurements are
preserved separately from the valuation assumptions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Davis--Kahan sine-theta theorem is a standard tool for turning perturbations
of a symmetric operator into perturbation bounds for eigenspaces
\citep{davis1970rotation,chen2021spectral}.  In statistics, the variant of
\citet{yu2015davis} is particularly useful because it replaces an awkward
sample-and-population separation condition with a population eigengap and gives
aligned-frame and singular-subspace forms suited to statistical estimation.
Formalizing these statements at their published scope required spectral
subspaces, principal angles, singular-value comparison, orthogonal alignment,
unitarily invariant norms, and interfaces between finite-dimensional matrix
statements and operator theory.

We formalize the published YWS paper as a source document and develop the
operator-theoretic prerequisites needed to state and prove its results at their
published scope.  The development verifies the statistical perturbation results,
identifies source defects through formal audit, and supplies reusable
spectral-perturbation foundations.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A source-faithful formalization of YWS at the published theorem numbering
        and generality, with explicit dispositions for defective printed
        statements and clearly separated extensions beyond the source.
  \item Machine-checked diagnoses and repairs of two printed YWS source defects:
        the false polynomial identity in equation (4) and the rank-boundary
        convention in Theorem 3.
  \item A source audit of \DK{} Proposition 4.4, including an explicit
        $\mathbb{R}^4$ trace-norm counterexample, a machine-checked localization
        of the proof defect to the printed equation (4.3), and a repaired theorem
        surface.
  \item A compiler-backed account of the prerequisite formalization, classifying
        project-local named-declaration dependencies into
        \FormalizationBasicTheoryCount{} basic mathematical theory areas with
        explicit scope and provenance assessments.
  \item A resource-accounting study of the formalization, including
        model-resolved token measurements and revisable estimates of API-equivalent
        cost and operational energy and emissions.
\end{enumerate}

The formalization is used here as verification, source audit, and reusable
mathematical infrastructure.  The breadth of the prerequisite layer provides an
additional reason to formalize these classical results: source-scale proofs force
interfaces that can support later work in spectral perturbation and operator
theory.  Resource accounting records the computational expenditure associated
with producing that infrastructure and permits economic and environmental
estimates to be revised when better measurements or models become available.

\paragraph{Population-gap formulation in statistics.}
Yu, Wang, and Samworth change little notation while changing how the
perturbation bound enters a statistical argument.  In a typical application, $\Sigma$ is a population
matrix determined by the statistical model, while $\widehat{\Sigma}$ is a
random estimator.  Consequently, the population eigengap
\[
  \Delta
  =
  \min\{
    \lambda_{r-1}(\Sigma)-\lambda_r(\Sigma),
    \lambda_s(\Sigma)-\lambda_{s+1}(\Sigma)
  \}
\]
is a deterministic property of the model, whereas gaps involving eigenvalues
of $\widehat{\Sigma}$ are themselves random quantities.  A perturbation theorem
whose separation hypothesis mixes the population and sample spectra therefore
creates an additional probabilistic obligation: before using the eigenspace
bound, one must first prove that the random sample eigenvalues remain suitably
separated.

Theorem~2 of Yu, Wang, and Samworth removes that intermediate dependence.  Once
a population gap $\Delta>0$ is assumed, a bound on
$\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma$ can be converted directly into a bound on the
estimated eigenspace.  This gives the modular form routinely needed in
statistical arguments:
\[
  \text{population separation}
  \;+\;
  \text{stochastic matrix-estimation error}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \text{eigenspace-estimation error}.
\]
The denominator is supplied by assumptions on the population model, while
  concentration or random-matrix arguments control the numerator.
Introducing a sample-eigengap assumption would change the statistical content
even if the resulting perturbation theorem were valid.  The displayed YWS theorem also depends on a
broader collection of spectral, geometric, and operator-theoretic foundations;
\cref{sec:foundations} summarizes those foundations and their formalization
provenance.

\subsection{Reusable mathematical foundations}

A second motivation comes from the prerequisite structure of the source
results.  Formalizing Davis--Kahan at the breadth of the 1970 paper and YWS at
its statistical scope requires a connected collection of projection geometry,
principal angles, singular-subspace comparison, unitarily invariant norms,
operator ideals, spectral calculus, real/complex transfer, direct rotations,
and Sylvester-type arguments.  The resulting interfaces have scope beyond the
individual source theorems and provide reusable components for later
formalizations in spectral perturbation and operator theory.

The prerequisite work therefore produces outputs beyond the source theorems.  A
later development can inherit definitions, spectral constructions, geometric
lemmas, and proof interfaces whose construction absorbed much of the work here.
\Cref{sec:foundations} gives the compiler-backed inventory and scoped coverage
assessment for these foundations.

\subsection{The Davis--Kahan headline theorem in Lean}
\label{sec:dk-headline}

The first headline result in Section~2 of \citet{davis1970rotation} is the
sine-theta theorem.  In the paper's coordinate setup, let $E_0$ parameterize a
trial subspace with restricted operator $A_0$, let $F_0$ parameterize the exact
comparison subspace, let $F_1$ parameterize its orthogonal complement with
$AF_1=F_1\Lambda_1$, and let
\[
  R=AE_0-E_0A_0,
  \qquad
  \sin\Theta_0=(I-F_0F_0^*)E_0.
\]
If, for some $\beta\leq\alpha$ and $\delta>0$, either
\[
  \operatorname{spec}(A_0)\subset[\beta,\alpha],
  \qquad
  \operatorname{spec}(\Lambda_1)\cap
    (\beta-\delta,\alpha+\delta)=\varnothing,
\]
or the same condition holds with $A_0$ and $\Lambda_1$ interchanged, then for
every unitarily invariant norm
\begin{equation}
  \delta\,\lVert\sin\Theta_0\rVert\leq\lVert R\rVert.
  \label{eq:dk-sintheta-headline}
\end{equation}
The source states this in the real/complex, infinite-dimensional setting and
allows the relevant self-adjoint operators to be unbounded when the residual is
bounded.  Our headline declaration retains that scope.  The Lean listing reproduces the source declaration through the beginning of
its proof, with only the glyph substitutions needed for pdf\LaTeX{} rendering.
Imports, ambient section variables, and the proof body are omitted.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Canonical Lean surface for the Davis--Kahan sine-theta theorem.},
  label={lst:dk-sintheta-headline}]
theorem sinTheta_headline
    [ContinuousLinearMap.HasMinMaxLowerBoundEverywhere.{u, v} \KField]
    [HasUnboundedSylvesterKyFan.{u, v} \KField]
    (N : UnitaryInvariantNorm)
    (A : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := E))
    (A₀ : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := F))
    (Λ₁ : ClosedOperator (\KField := \KField) (E := G))
    (E₀ : F →L[\KField] E)
    (F₀ : H →L[\KField] E)
    (F₁ : G →L[\KField] E)
    (sinTheta₀ : F →L[\KField] E)
    (R : F →L[\KField] E)
    (hSinTheta₀ :
      sinTheta₀ =
        (ContinuousLinearMap.id \KField E - F₀ ∘L F₀.adjoint) ∘L E₀)
    (hA : A.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (hA₀ : A₀.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (hΛ₁ : Λ₁.IsSelfAdjoint)
    (htrial : IsTrialResidual A A₀ E₀ R)
    (hexact : IsExactSpectralDecomposition A Λ₁ F₀ F₁)
    {β α δ : ℝ}
    (hβα : β ≤ α)
    (hδ : 0 < δ)
    (hspectral :
      (LinearPMap.realSpectrum A₀.toLinearPMap ⊆ Set.Icc β α ∧
          LinearPMap.realSpectrum Λ₁.toLinearPMap ⊆
            {x : ℝ | x ≤ β - δ ∨ α + δ ≤ x}) ∨
        (LinearPMap.realSpectrum Λ₁.toLinearPMap ⊆ Set.Icc β α ∧
          LinearPMap.realSpectrum A₀.toLinearPMap ⊆
            {x : ℝ | x ≤ β - δ ∨ α + δ ≤ x}))
    (hR : N.Mem R) :
    δ * N.gauge sinTheta₀ ≤ N.gauge R := by
\end{lstlisting}

\Cref{lst:dk-sintheta-headline} is the presentation-facing declaration selected
by the semantic-alignment audit.  Its conclusion is exactly
\cref{eq:dk-sintheta-headline}.  The sine-angle operator is explicitly tied to
$(I-F_0F_0^*)E_0$, while bundled residual and exact-spectral-decomposition
hypotheses collect the domain bookkeeping required for the unbounded setting.
The audit expands those bundled hypotheses through their characteristic
properties.  The bracketed typeclass arguments supply library capabilities for
the generic real/complex scalar setting and add no operator hypothesis beyond
the published theorem.

A lower formal layer also certifies membership in the relevant norm ideal.  The
source leaves that condition implicit when it writes a finite unitarily
invariant norm; the displayed declaration presents the published inequality
while retaining the stronger certificate internally.

\section{A Source-Faithful Formalization of YWS}
\label{sec:yws}

\subsection{The population-gap theorem}

Let $\Sigma,\widehat\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}$ be symmetric, with
population eigenvalues $\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_p$.  For
$1\leq r\leq s\leq p$, let $d=s-r+1$ and let $V,\widehat V\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}$
contain orthonormal eigenvectors at the ordered indices $r,\ldots,s$.  Define
\[
  \Delta=
  \min\{\lambda_{r-1}-\lambda_r,\lambda_s-\lambda_{s+1}\},
  \qquad
  \lambda_0=+\infty,\quad \lambda_{p+1}=-\infty.
\]
The main contribution of \citet{yu2015davis}, Theorem~2, states that if
$\Delta>0$, then
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\rVert_{\mathrm F}
  \leq
  \frac{2\min\{\sqrt d\,\lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
                    \lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm F}\}}
       {\Delta},
  \label{eq:yws-sintheta}
\end{equation}
and there exists $\widehat O\in O(d)$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\widehat V\widehat O-V\rVert_{\mathrm F}
  \leq
  \frac{2^{3/2}\min\{\sqrt d\,\lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},
                         \lVert\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert_{\mathrm F}\}}
       {\Delta}.
  \label{eq:yws-aligned}
\end{equation}
Only the population spectrum enters $\Delta$; the supplied sample eigenframe may
therefore be chosen arbitrarily inside a repeated sample eigenspace.

The Lean statement in \cref{lst:yws-theorem2-sintheta} is the canonical
declaration used by the semantic-alignment audit.  Its indexing convention
represents the same consecutive eigenvector block as the paper, the sine-angle
quantity is equated to its principal-angle realization, and the population-gap
hypothesis expands to the denominator in \cref{eq:yws-sintheta}, including the
full-space endpoint convention.  The symbols $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}$
and $\lVert\cdot\rVert_F$ in the listing denote the operator and Frobenius
norms.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=leanpaper,
  caption={Canonical Lean surface for the first conclusion of YWS Theorem 2.},
  label={lst:yws-theorem2-sintheta}]
theorem theorem2_sinTheta
    {p d r s : Nat}
    (Sigma SigmaHat :
      EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p) →ₗ[Real] EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hSigma : Sigma.IsSymmetric) (hSigmaHat : SigmaHat.IsSymmetric)
    (hr : r ≤ s) (hs : s < p) (hd : d = s - r + 1)
    (V Vhat : Fin d → EuclideanSpace Real (Fin p))
    (hV : IsEigenvectorBlock Sigma hSigma hr hs hd V)
    (hVhat : IsEigenvectorBlock SigmaHat hSigmaHat hr hs hd Vhat)
    (sinThetaNorm : Real)
    (hSinThetaNorm :
      sinThetaNorm =
        sinThetaFrobenius (Submodule.span Real (Set.range V))
          (Submodule.span Real (Set.range Vhat)))
    (Delta : Real) (hDelta : 0 < Delta)
    (hgap : SourcePopulationGap Sigma hSigma r s Delta) :
    sinThetaNorm ≤
      2 * min
        (Real.sqrt d * ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_op)
        ‖SigmaHat - Sigma‖_F / Delta := by
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Aligned frames and rectangular singular subspaces}

The second conclusion of YWS Theorem~2 gives the aligned-frame bound in

companion Lean declaration retains that existential orthogonality condition and
the expanded Frobenius distance; its source surface is reproduced in
\cref{app:yws-aligned-listing}.

The rectangular singular-subspace theorem is derived from the symmetric theory

the left and right singular spaces.  The reusable Gram, singular-value, and

\section{Formalization as Source Audit}
\label{sec:audit}

\subsection{Two defects in YWS}

The formalization records source defects explicitly.  First, printed
equation (4) is a false polynomial identity: it is missing a square, and the
repository contains both a concrete counterexample to the printed formula and a
proof of the corrected identity.  Second, the printed convention
$\sigma^2_{\operatorname{rank}(A)+1}:=-\infty$ in Theorem 3 is incorrect at the
rank boundary.  When $s=\operatorname{rank}(A)$ it makes a denominator infinite
and can force a zero upper bound even when the sample and population singular
subspaces are orthogonal.  The intended theorem is repaired by the ambient-index
boundary convention used in the paper's own Gram-matrix proof.

For equation~(4), a two-dimensional numerical example already exposes the
error.  Take
\[
u=(1,0),\qquad v=(3/5,4/5).
\]
Then $\|u\|=\|v\|=1$, $\langle v,u\rangle=3/5$, and
$\|v-u\|^2=4/5$.  The two sides of the identity as printed are therefore
\[
(2\langle v,u\rangle)^2(1-\langle v,u\rangle^2)
   = \frac{576}{625},
\qquad
\frac14\|v-u\|^2
  (2-\|v-u\|^2)
  (4-\|v-u\|^2)
   = \frac{96}{125},
\]
so they are unequal.  The correction is exactly one square:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:yws-equation4-corrected}
(2\langle v,u\rangle)^2(1-\langle v,u\rangle^2)
=
\frac14\|v-u\|^2
  (2-\|v-u\|^2)^2
  (4-\|v-u\|^2).
\end{equation}
Both the numerical counterexample and the corrected identity for arbitrary
real unit vectors are machine checked.  The corresponding declarations and
source locations are recorded in \cref{app:source-coverage}.

The rank-boundary defect in Theorem~3 also occurs in the smallest nontrivial
dimension.  Let
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0\\0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both matrices have rank one.  Taking $r=s=1$, their population and sample top
right singular subspaces are the perpendicular coordinate axes, and hence
\[
\|\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\|_F=1.
\]
Under the printed conventions
$\sigma_0^2:=+\infty$ and
$\sigma_{\operatorname{rank}(A)+1}^2:=-\infty$, however, both boundary
separations are infinite:
\[
\sigma_0^2-\sigma_1^2=+\infty,
\qquad
\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2=+\infty.
\]
Thus the printed denominator is infinite and its asserted upper bound collapses
to zero, contradicting the displayed value above.  This concrete $2\times2$ example is machine checked; the exact declaration is
listed in \cref{app:source-coverage}.

The correction is to take the boundary in the ambient Gram spectrum, as the
proof of Theorem~3 itself does.  For right singular subspaces of
$A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}$, extend the singular values by
$\sigma_j(A)=0$ for
$\operatorname{rank}(A)<j\le q$ and place the terminal convention at
$\sigma_{q+1}^2:=-\infty$; for left singular subspaces the corresponding
ambient endpoint is $p+1$.  Consequently the theorem uses a finite
$\Delta>0$ satisfying
\[
\Delta
\le
\min\left\{
  \sigma_{r-1}^2-\sigma_r^2,\,
  \sigma_s^2-\sigma_{s+1}^2
\right\},
\]
with the singular values interpreted at the appropriate ambient dimension.
For the example above, the lower boundary is therefore the genuine Gram
eigenvalue gap
\[
\sigma_1^2-\sigma_2^2=1-0=1,
\]
in place of $+\infty$.  The corrected boundary reduction and the corresponding
right- and left-singular-subspace statements are machine checked; exact Lean
names are given in \cref{app:source-coverage}.

\subsection{Davis--Kahan Proposition 4.4 is false as printed}

A separate audit of the 1970 source produced an explicit four-dimensional
counterexample to Proposition 4.4 for the trace norm.  With two principal angles
$\pi/4$, the direct rotation has trace displacement
$4\sqrt{2-\sqrt{2}}$, while an admissible orthogonal competitor has trace
displacement $2\sqrt{2}$.  Thus the claimed minimizing property fails inside the
stated $\Theta\leq\pi/3$ regime.

The same witness also localizes a specific defect in the published proof.
Proposition~4.3 is a separate valid result.  The defective step is the displayed
\emph{equation (4.3)} inside the proof of Proposition~4.4.
Writing $K=I-W$ and taking the two principal-plane projectors
$\Omega_1E=\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_3\}$ and
$\Omega_2E=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$, the source passes from its valid
Ky Fan variational identity to the asserted lower bound
\[
  \lVert K\rVert_4 \geq
  \lVert K\Omega_1\rVert_2+\lVert K\Omega_2\rVert_2.
\]
For the counterexample, however,
\[
  \lVert K\rVert_4=2\sqrt2
  \quad\text{while}\quad
  \lVert K\Omega_1\rVert_2=\lVert K\Omega_2\rVert_2=2,
\]
so the asserted inequality would require $2\sqrt2\geq4$.  The block values and
the resulting contradiction are machine checked.
The two domain planes are orthogonal, while their images under $K$ overlap;
the corresponding singular-value sums therefore fail to add.  On this witness, the later blockwise calculation where the $\pi/3$ bound
is consumed behaves as claimed; the invalid step is the earlier summation
across blocks.

The strict trace-norm comparison directly falsifies Proposition~4.4,
independently of the diagnosis of its printed proof.  Proposition~4.3 concerns
the squared displacement $(I-W)^*(I-W)$ and remains valid.  The
finite-dimensional formalization proves that result both at the unitarily
invariant norm level and at the paper-facing Ky Fan level.  On the counterexample,
the relevant partial sums of squared singular values satisfy the required
domination while the corresponding partial sums of singular values fail it.

The repository also contains a repaired result for the $Q$-norm class.  We
searched for prior treatments of the trace-norm failure inside the advertised
short-angle regime.  Bolotnikov, Li, and Rodman showed by 2001 that canonical
completion can fail for Schatten $p<2$ norms, including the trace norm, but their
explicit negative example is the orthogonal-subspace endpoint $\theta=\pi/2$,
outside the Davis--Kahan hypothesis $\theta_{\max}\leq\pi/3$
\citep{bolotnikov2001minimal}.  Qiu, Zhang, and Li later repeated the
Davis--Kahan short-angle minimality claim \citep{qiu2006grassmann}.  To the best
of our knowledge, no published correction or equivalent counterexample
identifies failure of Proposition~4.4 within its stated real short-angle regime.

The finite-dimensional foundations in \cref{tab:formalized-theories} include

singular-subspace machinery, spectral-gap reasoning, and unitarily invariant

The full Davis--Kahan development uses a broader range of foundations, including unitarily invariant norm and operator-ideal
machinery, polar decomposition and direct rotations, two-projection geometry,
operator angles and double-angle constructions, and Sylvester/separated-intertwiner
equations.  These topics are already part of the mathematical architecture of the
1970 paper \citep{davis1970rotation}; later operator-theoretic developments provide
useful comparison points for direct-rotation minimality and angle/Sylvester
methods \citep{bolotnikov2001minimal,qiu2006grassmann,grubisic2005sylvester,grubisic2010tan}.

The infinite-dimensional DK results also required a spectral-analysis layer that
is mostly absent from the displayed YWS theorem: projection-valued measures and
bounded Borel functional calculus, closed and unbounded self-adjoint spectral
theory, spectral restrictions and resolvents, and transfer between real and
complex Hilbert spaces.  The classical mathematical reference point is Kato's
operator perturbation theory \citep{kato1976perturbation}.  On the formal side,
Mathlib already supplies a substantial continuous functional-calculus foundation
\citep{mathlib2020,dedecker2025cfc}; the compiler trace here records the additional
project-local interfaces and constructions that the source-faithful DK development
actually depends on.

\subsection{Degree of formalization}

Coverage is described with four scoped labels: \emph{focused reusable slice},
\emph{substantial focused subsystem}, \emph{substantial reusable subsystem}, and
\emph{broad partial development}.  Field-level percentages lack a natural denominator, and declaration counts are
sensitive to API design.  The labels therefore describe the local theory:
a narrow reusable interface, a coherent cluster around one problem, a
substantial independently reusable subsystem, or a broader development with
known gaps.  The declaration graph and module inventory provide quantitative
evidence for those reviewed labels.

Final named-declaration dependence and historical development can differ.  Some
auxiliary results support API construction without remaining on a traced path to
a source result, while a small declaration may be reused by many results.  The
compiler graph records the first quantity conservatively; the scope labels
summarize the second at the level of mathematical theory.  Both layers are
retained so that future evidence can revise the qualitative assessment without
changing the recorded dependency snapshot.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Large formal developments provide precedents for source-driven mathematical
verification and reusable library construction, including Flyspeck, the Liquid
Tensor Experiment, and Mathlib itself
\citep{hales2017kepler,scholze2022liquid,mathlib2020}.  LLM-assisted
formalization has progressed from natural-language autoformalization and
sketch-guided proving \citep{wu2022autoformalization,jiang2023dsp} to
long-horizon Lean workflows \citep{zhang2026leanmarathon}.  Recent statistical
formalization develops empirical-process infrastructure in Lean
\citep{zhang2026slt}; its public repository also contains later Davis--Kahan
extensions \citep{zhang2026sltrepo}.

Human use of AI during formalization has also become an object of study
\citep{collins2026humanai}.  The Anthropic Riemann-zeta case study reports
resource usage and multi-agent development traces for a research mathematics
task \citep{anthropic2026zeta}.  Recent formalization agents expose alternative
workflow choices that can be compared experimentally.  MathCode combines a
persistent Lean environment with reusable theorem libraries, planning, and
parallel subgoal work \citep{mathcode2026}; AutoLean provides logged
formalization and proving loops with compiler feedback, semantic evaluation, and
replanning \citep{autolean2026}.  Our resource study contributes a longitudinal
measured-usage record from a source-audited spectral-perturbation formalization.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The YWS and Davis--Kahan formalization includes the published theorem surfaces
and the spectral, geometric, and operator-theoretic interfaces needed to prove
them.  Formal source audit identified defects in both sources, while the
prerequisite development produced reusable foundations for later formal work.
That reusable layer is part of the scientific return from formalizing these
sources at their published breadth.

The resource analysis records substantial computational expenditure for the
observed portion of the project.  Token measurements are stored independently of
commit-level attribution and post-hoc valuation, so revised price or emissions
assumptions can be applied to the same ledger as empirical estimates improve.

The commit-linked portion of the ledger also suggests a direct experiment for
future work.  Selected pre-commit repository states can be paired with the
formalization obligations solved by the corresponding commits and rerun under
alternative agent workflows such as MathCode and AutoLean
\citep{mathcode2026,autolean2026}.  The original commit-associated token usage
provides an imperfect historical baseline.  Comparisons would need to control
model choice, available context, stopping criteria, and semantic acceptance, and
commit attribution alone cannot establish causal per-task cost.  Such a study
could test whether persistent Lean sessions, theorem retrieval, planning and
replanning, or other orchestration choices reduce token and API expenditure for
comparable mathematical coverage and proof quality.

Our hope is that the reusable foundations developed here reduce the work
required by later formalizations.  This project still required substantial
computational expenditure, and the preserved record leaves a concrete open
question: how much of that expenditure can future workflows avoid while reaching
comparable source fidelity, mathematical coverage, and proof checking?
