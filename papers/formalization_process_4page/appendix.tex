\section{Data and reproducibility}
\label{app:data}

Using LLM-assisted web search, we collected the \PractitionerAccountCount{}
public first-person accounts used in this paper.  Each row retains the public
source URL, bibliography key, observation used here, a qualification note, and
the categorical fields used for the descriptive counts.  The checked-in source
notes render these rows as per-account records so each observation can be
checked against its public source.  The collection is not necessarily
representative, so we make no prevalence estimates from it.

\texttt{make sources} validates the account schema, identifiers, citation keys,
and URLs and regenerates the paper macros.  The monthly Lean-publication series
and selected Git timeline are kept separately.  For the publication series, a
networked survey captured the exact Papers With Lean statistics page and
\texttt{site\_papers.json} corpus on 6 September 2026 and recorded hashes of both.
Grouping the corpus by its \texttt{published} calendar month reproduced every
frozen January 2025--July 2026 chart value and the live August value.  The
tracked CSV retains the partial September count for provenance; the figure below
shows complete months only, through August 2026.

The build records SHA-256 digests and Git revisions for the manuscript inputs
and generated evidence in \texttt{generated/materials\_manifest.json}.  For
\cref{lst:scope-mismatch}, it extracts the historical bounded Theorem~8.2
signature from commit \texttt{59f37a20} and the unbounded source-facing endpoint
from \texttt{064df8d3}; the full signatures, paths, line ranges, and digests are
retained in the generated semantic-alignment candidate notes.  The prompt
history used while drafting this paper is preserved verbatim in
\texttt{brainstorm.md}; retained pasted text written by another agent is labeled
as such.

The interface consolidates evidence that had previously been inspected through
source files, LLM reviews, and small Lean queries.  A reader learning Lean may
still need help understanding structures, predicates, coercions, and typeclass
assumptions in a signature.  Lean Compass's theorem-centered semantic dependency
view suggests a useful extension: make those definitions inspectable from the
statement while omitting proof-only dependencies \citep{yanahama2026leanatlas}.

\toprule
Term & Meaning here \\
\midrule
Lean kernel & The small checker that validates an elaborated proof term against its formal type. \\
Lean signature & The theorem declaration: parameters and hypotheses followed by the proposition being proved. \\
Elaboration & Lean's resolution of notation, implicit arguments, coercions, namespaces, and typeclass inference into a fully typed term. \\
Self-adjoint operator & An operator equal to its adjoint; for real finite-dimensional matrices this corresponds to a symmetric matrix. \\
Spectral subspace & A subspace associated with a selected part of an operator's spectrum; eigenspaces are the finite-matrix example. \\
Residual & A quantity measuring how far a trial subspace or frame fails to satisfy the exact invariant relation; here $R=AE_0-E_0A_0$. \\
Spectral gap & Separation between the target spectral region and competing spectrum. \\
$\sin\Theta$ & An operator whose singular values encode sines of principal angles between subspaces. \\
Unitarily invariant norm & A norm unchanged by unitary changes of coordinates; examples include spectral, Frobenius/Hilbert--Schmidt, and trace norms in finite dimensions. \\
Semantic alignment & Agreement between a formal declaration and the mathematical claim intended by its source, including inherited context and scope. \\
