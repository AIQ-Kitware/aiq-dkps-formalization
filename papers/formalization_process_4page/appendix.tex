\label{app:data}

Using LLM-assisted web search, we collected the \PractitionerAccountCount{}
public first-person project/workflow accounts used in this paper.  They are
supported by \PractitionerSourceCount{} practitioner-source records, including
\PractitionerSupplementalSourceCount{} supplemental or corroborating sources.
The account table is separate from the source table: additional sources for one
project do not increase the project-account denominator.  Accounts known to
share a practitioner carry the same practitioner-overlap cluster; the
\PractitionerAccountCount{} project accounts form
\PractitionerOverlapClusterCount{} such clusters.  This cluster count is not a
count of unique practitioners.  Each account retains the observation and coding
qualifications used here, while each source retains its URL, bibliography key,
source role, and objective provenance fields.  The collection is not
necessarily representative, so we make no prevalence estimates from it.

The screening log records the sources in the preexisting snapshot and candidates
reviewed from 8 September 2026 onward, with their disposition and a reason.
Useful follow-up sources are marked \texttt{supplemental\_source} instead of
being counted as new accounts; \PractitionerHoldCount{} screened candidates are
currently marked \texttt{hold} pending stronger first-person workflow evidence.
The log does not reconstruct every candidate encountered in earlier searches
and is not an exhaustive search record.

\texttt{make sources} validates the account, source, and screening schemas,

\section{Formalization procedure details}
\label{app:system}

\Cref{sec:system} summarizes the working procedure.  At the manuscript snapshot,
the workspace uses Lean \texttt{v4.34.0-rc1}; the Lake manifest pins Mathlib at
\texttt{72f9c607bb3a} and Tau Ceti at \texttt{1b39d420ac84}.  The ordinary
\texttt{lake build} checks the reusable \texttt{ForTauCeti} package, the
Davis--Kahan library used by this paper, and the other formalization targets in
the workspace.

\paragraph{Records kept during the formalization.}
The supplementary repository retains the information needed to resume and audit
work without relying on a particular chat transcript:
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0.2em}
\item the \textbf{source-order transcription}, used to read the source claims in
  order and distribute them for review;
\item the \textbf{source-atom record}, which records finer-grained statements and
  surrounding mathematical material for source-coverage checks;
\item the \textbf{29-result register}, which records each counted source result,
  its intended scope, the Lean evidence used for it, and its review state;
\item the \textbf{registered Lean-evidence record}, used by the checkers to map a
  source result to the declaration that is claimed to formalize it;
\item the \textbf{audit packet}, which places the source text, our interpretation,
  and the resolved Lean evidence together for result-by-result review; and
\item the \textbf{Lean source tree and \texttt{ForTauCeti/}}, containing the
  checked implementations and reusable foundations developed during the work.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Working procedure.}
Before implementation, we recorded the source passage, inherited assumptions,
and intended scope of a target.  After Lean accepted an implementation, we
registered the declaration used as evidence for that target.  The project
checkers then resolved that declaration in the pinned workspace and regenerated
its compiler-derived type and consistency checks.  The audit packet placed those
results beside the source context.  The resulting correspondence judgment was
stored with the target for later work.

\paragraph{Mechanical checks.}
The ordinary build checks the Lean libraries in the pinned environment.  We also
run separate checks for the statement map, source coverage, and the 29-result
register.  The result-level checker resolves registered declarations against the
Lean project and can query compiler-derived theorem types rather than relying on
copied signatures.  It also rejects stale or internally inconsistent evidence.
Axiom and dependency inspection is available through \texttt{leanq}.  These
checks establish that the recorded formal artifact exists and has the recorded
formal properties; source correspondence is reviewed separately.

\paragraph{Statement review.}
For each tracked result, review starts from the source passage and inherited
context and then examines the elaborated Lean type together with definitions
that affect the hypotheses or conclusion.  For Davis--Kahan, the recurring
questions were the scalar field, dimension, bounded or unbounded operator scope,
norm class, gap conditions, constants, directionality, and which angle or
residual operator a definition represents.  When these disagreed with the
source, we revised the formalization even if Lean had already proved the encoded
proposition.  The script
\texttt{scripts/render\_davis\_kahan\_1970\_audit\_packet.py} renders the
result-by-result audit packet.  The interface in \cref{app:review-page} was added
later to place the same source, interpretation, and Lean evidence in one view.

\paragraph{Division of labor.}
LLMs proposed Lean code, searched for existing foundations, diagnosed compiler
failures, and assisted with source/signature comparisons.  Human authors chose
the source results claimed by the paper and adjudicated ambiguous source
readings.  Review by another model was useful for finding possible problems, but
acceptance of a source correspondence did not follow from a fixed number of
model review rounds.

\section{Davis--Kahan background and Formalization 1}
\label{app:formalization1}

Davis and Kahan compare a candidate subspace with an exact invariant
subspace of a self-adjoint operator \citep{davis1970rotation}.  In finite
dimensions, let $A=A^*$ (i.e., $A$ is self-adjoint) and let $E_0$ have
orthonormal columns spanning the candidate subspace $V$.  The
\emph{candidate-space compression} is
\begin{equation}
  A_0=E_0^*AE_0.
  \label{eq:dk-candidate-compression}
\end{equation}
This expression can be read from right to left: $E_0$ embeds candidate
coordinates into the ambient space, $A$ acts there, and $E_0^*$ projects the
result back to candidate coordinates.  Thus $A_0$ is the operator seen after
applying $A$ and discarding the component outside $V$.  Writing
$P_V=E_0E_0^*$ for the orthogonal projector onto $V$, define the
\emph{candidate residual}
\begin{equation}
  R=AE_0-E_0A_0=(I-P_V)AE_0.
  \label{eq:dk-candidate-residual}
\end{equation}
Thus $R$ is exactly the component of $AE_0$ outside $V$, and $R=0$ precisely
when $V$ is invariant under $A$.  The historical signature allows any bounded
operator \texttt{M} on $V$, not just the compression in
\cref{eq:dk-candidate-compression}; its residual is
$A\circ\iota_V-\iota_V\circ M$, where $\iota_V:V\hookrightarrow E$ is inclusion.
Choosing $M$ to be the corresponding candidate-space compression recovers
the residual in \cref{eq:dk-candidate-residual}.

For the exact subspace $U$, the assumption \texttt{Reduces A U} says that both
$U$ and $U^\perp$ are invariant.  In Formalization~1, the spectrum of $A|_U$
lies in $[a,b]$, while the spectrum of $A|_{U^\perp}$ lies outside
$(a-\delta,b+\delta)$ for $\delta>0$.  This gap separates the two exact spectral
parts.  It is an assumption on the operator being compared, rather than a
consequence of the residual being small.

For two subspaces of the same finite dimension with orthonormal coordinate
maps $E_0$ and $F_0$, their principal angles $\theta_j\in[0,\pi/2]$ satisfy
$\cos\theta_j=\sigma_j(F_0^*E_0)$.  Equivalently, they pair directions in the two
subspaces by their overlap.  The directed sine measures the part of a candidate
direction outside the exact subspace; its matrix representative is
$(I-P_U)E_0$.  The doubled-angle quantity applies $\sin(2\theta_j)$ to the
angles, with the candidate-to-exact orientation fixed by the source.  The bound
\[
  \delta\,N(\sin 2\Theta_0)\le 2N(R)
\]
therefore controls the doubled-angle quantity by the residual relative to the
gap.  A small doubled sine alone does not imply a small angle:
$\sin(2\theta)$ vanishes at both $0$ and $\pi/2$.  Recovering a small-angle bound
requires an appropriate angle branch, such as $0\le\theta\le\pi/4$.

Formalization~1 states the estimate for complex Hilbert spaces.  Its ambient
Hilbert-space assumptions are omitted from the displayed code.  In the syntax
shown there, \texttt{E }$\mathrel{\to}\!\mathtt{L}[\mathbb C]$\texttt{ E}
means a bounded continuous complex-linear map $E\to E$.  It elaborates to
\texttt{ContinuousLinearMap (RingHom.id Complex) E E}; this bounded,
complex-linear type is part of the historical scope mismatch.  The following
table uses the same surface syntax as the displayed formalization.

The conclusion asserts that the doubled-angle block belongs to the same ideal
as the residual and then bounds its gauge.  These norm and spectral conditions
are part of the statement.  The historical scope mismatch is visible in the
fixed complex field and bounded type of $A$.  They exclude the source's real
and unbounded settings, even when all the displayed hypotheses hold.
