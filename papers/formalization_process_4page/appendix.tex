The page makes the checked declaration easy to retrieve, but a reader learning
Lean may still need help understanding the vocabulary inside that declaration.
For example, the meaning of \texttt{ClosedOperator}, \texttt{IsSelfAdjoint},
coercions, and typeclass assumptions can be spread across project code and
Mathlib.  Lean Compass's theorem-centered semantic dependency view suggests a
useful direction for this interface: make those definitions inspectable from
the statement while keeping proof-only dependencies out of the way
\citep{yanahama2026leanatlas}.

\section{A stopping model for repeated review}
\label{app:stopping}

A prospective review process could make the statistical idea in the main text
precise.  Suppose discoveries of new source-correspondence defects follow a
Poisson process with rate $\lambda$ per unit of standardized review exposure.
If $k$ defects are found during exposure $E$ and
$\lambda\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(a,b)$ under the rate parameterization, then
\[
  \lambda\mid k,E\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(a+k,b+E),
\]
and the posterior predictive probability of no discovery in an additional
exposure $h$ is
\[
  \Pr\{K(E+h)-K(E)=0\mid k,E\}
  =\left(\frac{b+E}{b+E+h}\right)^{a+k}.
\]
This gives a stopping statistic for future discoveries under the assumed
process.  A probability that the formal theorem is semantically correct would
also require a model connecting remaining defects to their chance of being
detected.  The historical project lacks standardized exposure, so we do not
fit these parameters to calendar time or to the existing Git events.
