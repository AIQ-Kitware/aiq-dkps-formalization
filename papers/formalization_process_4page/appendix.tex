\section{Data and reproducibility}
\label{app:data}

Using LLM-assisted web search, we collected the \PractitionerAccountCount{}
public first-person accounts used in this paper.  Each row retains the public
source URL, bibliography key, observation used here, a qualification note, and
the categorical fields used for the descriptive counts.  The checked-in source
notes render these rows as per-account records so each observation can be
checked against its public source.  The collection is not necessarily
representative, so we make no prevalence estimates from it.

\texttt{make sources} validates the account schema, identifiers, citation keys,
and URLs and regenerates the paper macros.  Figure~\ref{fig:lean-activity}
reproduces the Papers With Lean public monthly series for January 2025 through
August 2026 from a 6 September 2026 snapshot.  Grouping the captured corpus by
its \texttt{published} month reproduced the public chart; the partial September
count is excluded from the figure.

\section{Reading Formalization 1}
\label{app:formalization1}

Formalization~1 is a historical bounded-complex version of the directed
$\sin 2\Theta$ residual estimate.  In the notation of the source, $U$ is the
separated exact subspace and $V$ is the trial subspace.  The inclusion
\texttt{V.subtypeL} plays the role of $E_0$, while the operator \texttt{M} on
$V$ plays the role of $A_0$.  Consequently
\texttt{residual A V.subtypeL M} is exactly the operator
$AE_0-E_0A_0$.  The two spectral hypotheses place the spectrum of $A$ on $U$
inside $[a,b]$ and the spectrum on $U^\perp$ at least $\delta$ away.  The
conclusion says that the directed $\sin 2\Theta$ object belongs to the same norm
ideal as the residual and satisfies
$\delta N(\sin 2\Theta) \le 2N(R)$.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Reader's guide to the principal Lean structures in Formalization~1.}
\label{tab:formalization1-glossary}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.34\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
\toprule
Lean expression & Mathematical role \\
\midrule
\texttt{ContinuousLinearMap Complex E E}
  & A bounded continuous complex-linear operator $E\to E$.  In Formalization~1
    this is the ambient self-adjoint operator $A$; restricting it to this type
    is part of the historical scope mismatch. \\
\texttt{IsSelfAdjoint A}
  & $A=A^*$, the self-adjointness assumption. \\
\texttt{Submodule Complex E}
  & A complex linear subspace of the ambient Hilbert space.  Here $U$ is the
    separated exact/reducing subspace and $V$ is the trial subspace. \\
\texttt{U.HasOrthogonalProjection}
  & The orthogonal projection onto $U$ is available; likewise for $V$.  This
    supports $U^\perp$, compressions, and the angle constructions. \\
\texttt{Reduces A U}
  & Both $U$ and $U^\perp$ are invariant under $A$. \\
\texttt{compressOperator U A}
  & The orthogonal compression $P_U A|_U$; for a reducing subspace this is the
    restriction of $A$ to $U$. \\
\texttt{spectrum Real (...)}
  & The real spectrum of the self-adjoint compression, used to state the
    interval and gap hypotheses. \\
\texttt{V.subtypeL}
  & The bounded inclusion $V\hookrightarrow E$, corresponding to the source's
    trial-basis map $E_0$. \\
\texttt{M : ContinuousLinearMap Complex V V}
  & The operator on the trial coordinates, corresponding to $A_0$. \\
\texttt{residual A V.subtypeL M}
  & $A\circ E_0-E_0\circ A_0$, the source residual $R$. \\
\texttt{SymmetricNormingFunction}
  & A normalized, dimension-coherent symmetric gauge on singular values used to
    represent the source's unitarily invariant norm $N$. \\
\texttt{N.Mem T}
  & $T$ lies in the canonical operator ideal on which the norm determined by
    $N$ is finite. \\
\texttt{N.gauge T}
  & The real-valued norm/gauge of $T$, i.e. the quantity written $N(T)$ in the
    source-level inequality. \\
\texttt{sinTwoThetaIdealBlock U V}
  & A bounded operator whose singular values encode the directed
    $\sin 2\Theta$ quantity, so its \texttt{N.gauge} is the norm appearing on
    the left-hand side of the estimate. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The residual, spectral gap, norm inequality, and directed-angle quantity in
Formalization~1 are the intended mathematical objects.  The mismatch is in
scope: the declaration fixes the scalar field to $\mathbb C$ and takes $A$ to
be bounded, while the source also covers real and appropriate unbounded
self-adjoint settings.  The scalar field and the type of $A$ in the signature
therefore show why this theorem could not by itself serve as the source-exact
witness.

The interface consolidates evidence that had previously been inspected through
source files, LLM reviews, and small Lean queries.  A reader learning Lean may
still need help understanding structures, predicates, coercions, and typeclass
assumptions in a signature.  Lean Compass's theorem-centered semantic dependency
view suggests a useful extension: make those definitions inspectable from the
statement while omitting proof-only dependencies \citep{yanahama2026leanatlas}.
